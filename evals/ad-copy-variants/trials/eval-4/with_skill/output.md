## How your constraints re-rank the default order

B2B default efficiency order is `pain > mechanism > outcome > identity`. Three things about Fernhollow move that:

1. **The outcome asset already exists.** The 31% number is measured, cleared, and sitting on your desk — its cost is already paid, so per the library's promotion rule it jumps the queue regardless of the default efficiency read. This is the one number a competitor can't casually claim, so it's worth leading with even though this is a one-off push (normally a one-off would defer expensive-to-prove angles — but this one isn't expensive anymore, the cost was paid during the pilot).
2. **Solution-aware traffic promotes mechanism.** Readers already believe the problem is real; they're comparing _how_ solutions solve it. Opening on "why this works" earns more than opening on the pain they've already accepted.
3. **The 3-week deadline is a drafting-speed constraint, not a copy constraint.** It doesn't argue for urgency-flavored copy (deleted above); it argues for fewer angles, all cheap to draft _right now_. All three angles below are same-day: two ride the existing pilot stat, one is mined straight from the problem you already understand.

## Recommendation

**Set A, 3 angles, discrete head-to-head test** (pending confirmation you're not running an ML asset-pool placement).

Reasoning in one line each:

- **Outcome first** because the asset is already paid for and un-copyable — leads with your only hard number.
- **Mechanism second** because solution-aware readers are shopping on "why this works," not "does this problem exist."
- **Pain third** because it's free, grounds the set in something every reader recognizes, and gives you a genuine third argument rather than a reworded first one.
- **Identity held back** (not deleted — logged for round two) because its evidence is thin and a one-off, deadline-boxed push should spend its three slots on the angles with the clearest grounding, not the widest net.

This is a default ranking, not a fixed one — if you can tell me the buying-committee role (AP manager building an internal case vs. a CFO signing off directly), that could move Pain vs. Mechanism relative to each other, though it won't change the Outcome lead.
