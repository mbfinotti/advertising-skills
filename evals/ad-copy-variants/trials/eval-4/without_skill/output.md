## Recommended angle: **Proof-led single-metric, not story-led social proof**

Lead with the number. Make the 31%/90-days stat the entire hook — not a supporting detail under a narrative. This is the right call given your specific constraints, not a default preference:

- **No competitor names, no testimonials** → the only differentiation lever left is specificity. A precise, oddly-exact number ("31% in 90 days," not "up to 30%" or "significant savings") reads as credible _because_ it's specific — it does the trust-building work a quote or comparison would normally do.
- **Solution-aware audience** → skip problem education ("expenses are leaking money") and category explainer ("what is expense audit"). They already know this category exists and are evaluating within it. Copy should compete on outcome clarity, not awareness.
- **No deadline/promo** → don't fabricate urgency ("limited spots," "ends Friday"). It'll read as false on a feed full of people who evaluate SaaS for a living. Let the number carry the tension instead.
- **One-off push, results due in 3 weeks** → optimize for direct response (demo/benchmark requests), not brand lift. A single flight can't sustain a nurture arc, so the ad needs to convert cold-to-lead in one exposure.

## What the angle looks like in practice

**Primary variant — the number as headline:**

> "One finance team cut duplicate-expense payouts 31% in 90 days. No process overhaul. Just an audit layer that catches what approval workflows miss."

**Secondary variant — reframe the sub-problem** (works well for solution-aware buyers who think they've already solved "expense audit" broadly but haven't isolated duplicates as a distinct leak):

> "Duplicate payouts hide inside expense tools that were built to catch policy violations, not duplicates. One customer found 31% of that specific leak — gone in 90 days."

This second variant differentiates by _narrowing the problem_ rather than comparing vendors — a legal-safe way to imply "the tool you have isn't built for this" without naming anyone.

**What to avoid:**

- Rounding or softening the stat ("up to 30%+") — precision is the credibility mechanism here; don't sand it down.
- Any phrasing that could be read as an implied quote ("Our customers say...") — you have zero cleared quotes, so keep it in third-person observational voice ("one customer," "a pilot customer"), never first-person attributed.
- Manufactured urgency or countdown framing — inconsistent with "no deadline" and will undercut trust with a finance/ops audience that's allergic to it.
- Broad "save money" framing — dilutes the one asset you have. Stay anchored to duplicate-expense payouts specifically; that's the claim that's actually cleared.

## CTA and measurement

Given the 3-week board deadline and single-flight nature, optimize the campaign for **demo requests or a benchmark/calculator tool**, not top-of-funnel content downloads — a gated ebook won't cycle to a reportable result in time. Set a short attribution window (7-day click) and report on cost-per-qualified-lead against the flight spend, since impressions/reach won't mean much to a board in 3 weeks.

**One line for the board narrative in advance:** frame this flight as a _message-validation test_ ("does a proof-led angle convert on solution-aware traffic"), not a pipeline-volume bet — that's an honest framing for a one-off push and sets the right bar for what "success" looks like at the readout.
