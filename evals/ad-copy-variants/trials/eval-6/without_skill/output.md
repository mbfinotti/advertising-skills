## What's really happening

CTR is not "this ad is winning." CTR is "this ad's promise matches what a lot of clickers want." Your data shows those two things have come apart:

- **2.1% CTR** = the "free plant doctor consult" headline is pulling in people who want a free consult.
- **Conversion < half the others, CPA ~2x** = when those people land on a page that sells a $29/month box with zero mention of any consult, most of them bounce or convert at a much lower rate — because you showed them something you don't sell.

This is a **message-match failure**, not a headline-quality win. The other 3 headlines (0.8–1.0% CTR) are almost certainly pre-qualifying better: fewer clicks, but the clickers already know it's a subscription box, so more of them buy.

If you write 8 more variants of "free consult" and put them in front of the same landing page, you'll very likely repeat the pattern: CTR goes up, CPA gets worse, and you'll have burned budget "optimizing" for a vanity metric. Worth deciding this on purpose rather than by accident.

## Two ways to actually use this insight

**Option A — make the promise real.** If "plant doctor" resonates this hard, it's a signal to *add* something like a "free care consult with every box" or "text a plant doctor anytime" perk to the subscription and land people on a page that says so. Then the CTR win becomes a conversion win too.

**Option B — keep the resonance, drop the overpromise.** Reframe headlines around expertise/care-guarantee language that's actually true of the box you're selling today, so the ad and the landing page tell the same story.
