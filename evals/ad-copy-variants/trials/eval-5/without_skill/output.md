No, don't write the 12. The "3-5 variants per ad group" rule is copied from a world with far more traffic than you have — it silently assumes you'll accumulate enough conversions per variant to actually tell them apart within a sane timeframe. Check that assumption against your numbers before following the advice.

**The math that kills this plan**

18 demo requests/month across your whole account. Split 12 ways, each variant averages **1.5 demos/month**. To get even a weak read (~15-20 conversions per variant, the rough floor for LinkedIn ad testing) on a single variant, you'd need 8-13 months — *per variant*, run in parallel with 11 others competing for the same small pool of impressions and budget. You will not reach significance on anything before your CMO forgets this was ever the plan. You'll have 12 rows of noise, not 12 learnings.

There's a second problem hiding in "12 variants": you're bundling two different experiments into one bucket.

- **Ad copy testing** (which headline/angle converts better) — needs same audience, one variable changed, enough volume to compare.
- **Segment testing** (does driver-safety even work as an audience) — a go/no-go question about a market, not a copy question.

Mixed together across 12 ads, a bad result tells you nothing: you won't know if it failed because the *copy* was weak or the *segment* doesn't respond to LinkedIn ads at all. You need to isolate them.

**What to actually launch**

1. **Protect the winner.** Keep 70-80% of budget on the proven cost-savings ad. It's your only reliable demo source — don't starve it to feed an experiment.
2. **One incremental test on the known-good angle.** A single new variant (different headline or CTA, same cost-savings angle) run against the control. One variable, one comparison, readable.
3. **One isolated test for driver-safety.** Its own small campaign/budget, own ad, run for 4-6 weeks minimum. Goal isn't "beat the winner" yet — it's a directional signal: does this segment respond at all (CTR, cost-per-click, any demos), worth a second round or not.
4. **Total new variants: 2, not 12.** That's already stretching your monthly volume thin; 12 makes every result unreadable.
5. **Extend the test window.** At this volume, think in 4-8 week windows per test, not the weekly cadence "3-5 variants" implies. Use CTR/CPC as early leading indicators, but let demo requests (the real business metric) be the actual decision criterion — leading indicators alone will steer you wrong on a $14k sales-led product.
6. **Sequence, don't parallelize, once you're past these two.** After you get a read on the headline test and the driver-safety segment, use the winner as new baseline and test the next single variable. Serial testing beats a 12-way fan-out when your sample size is this small — you learn something real each round instead of nothing at all.

**What to tell your CMO**

"3-5 variants per ad group" is right-sized for accounts with thousands of weekly clicks, not one with 18 monthly demos. At our volume, 12 parallel variants guarantees zero statistical power and starves our one working ad. Give me one new copy test and one new segment test — that's what our sample size can actually support — and I'll bring back a real read in 4-6 weeks instead of a wall of unreadable numbers.
