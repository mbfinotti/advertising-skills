## The 6 I'd run

| # | Headline | Angle | Why it earns a slot |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cycle tracking that's actually accurate** | Skepticism/contrast | Sharpest hook for problem-aware traffic — "actually" implies every other app they've tried wasn't. Highest expected CTR of the set. |
| 2 | **Finally, cycle predictions you can trust** | Relief from frustration | "Finally" is a strong pattern-interrupt and names the emotional payoff, not just the feature. |
| 3 | **Know your cycle before it knows you** | Mastery/control (indirect) | The only genuinely different linguistic register in the batch — everything else is "[accurate/predict] + cycle." You need one structurally different variant or a multivariate test can't isolate whether *style* or *claim* is winning. Also maps cleanly to "plan your life around it." |
| 4 | **Cycle predictions that don't miss** | Precision, punchy | Negative framing + distinct vocabulary (no "accurate" or "predict" doing double duty). Memorable, scroll-stopping. |
| 5 | **Accurate cycle predictions, every month** | Reliability/consistency | Best literal match to the value prop — "plan your life around" is a claim about *ongoing* reliability, not a one-off accuracy score. Serves as your reliability-framed anchor. |
| 6 | **Predict your cycle with confidence** | Emotional reframe | Plain, clean control variant. Reframes accuracy as the *feeling* it produces (confidence to commit to plans), which is closer to what actually drives conversion than the accuracy claim itself. |

## The 6 I'd cut, and why

- **"Your cycle, predicted accurately"** — flattest phrasing in the set, strictly weaker than #5 and #6, adds no new information.
- **"The most accurate way to track your cycle" / "Track your cycle with total accuracy" / "Your most accurate cycle forecast"** — cut as a cluster. All three are the same superlative claim ("most/total") restated three ways. Running all three wastes test slots on redundant hypotheses, and stacking unsubstantiated superlative health claims raises your Meta ad-review disapproval risk in a reproductive-health category — one is already a stretch without data to back "most," three is asking for it.
- **"Accuracy-first cycle tracking"** — reads like an internal positioning line, not a feed ad. Weak emotional hook, no benefit stated.
- **"Trust your cycle predictions again"** — near-duplicate of #2 ("Finally... trust"). Same angle, and "again" narrows the claim to users who specifically had a bad prior experience with *this exact behavior*, which is a slightly awkward assumption to force onto everyone in the audience. Keep #2, bench this one.

## Two things worth flagging

- **Redundancy was the main problem with the agency's set, not quality.** Of the 12, roughly 7 are variations on "[intensifier] + accurate + cycle predictions." A 6-way test only tells you something if the variants are testing different hypotheses (angle, emotion, voice) — not just synonyms of the same claim.
- **The 200 app-store reviews are wasted if you stop here.** None of these 12 headlines use customer language. Worth a follow-up pass mining the reviews for how users actually describe the "I can finally plan around this" moment — that's usually where the next winning headline comes from, not from another round of agency-generated synonyms.
