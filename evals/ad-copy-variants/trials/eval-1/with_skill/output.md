## My call: run these 6

The agency's 12 headlines are almost all one angle wearing different clothes — the phrase "accurate" (or a synonym) appears in 9 of the 12. Per the distinctness gate, headlines that make the identical argument are rewords, not variants: swap them and nothing breaks. I picked the 6 that actually test different arguments, not just different adjectives.

| # | Headline | Angle | Awareness fit | Why it earns a slot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Know your cycle before it knows you"** | Identity / mastery | Problem-aware | Only headline that isn't a restatement of "accurate." Argument is personal foresight/control, not a product spec. |
| 2 | **"Finally, cycle predictions you can trust"** | Pain (skepticism relief) | Problem-aware ✅ best awareness fit in the set | "Finally" names the reader's frustration with unreliable trackers before pitching the product — textbook PAS opener for problem-aware traffic. |
| 3 | **"Trust your cycle predictions again"** | Pain (relapse/win-back) | Problem-aware | Close cousin of #2, but "again" targets someone who *specifically* got burned before — a real audience-level distinction, worth the cheap second wording. |
| 4 | **"Predict your cycle with confidence"** | Outcome — emotional payoff | Problem-aware | The benefit here is peace of mind, not the accuracy stat itself. Distinct enough from the others to teach something about which payoff resonates. |
| 5 | **"Cycle tracking that's actually accurate"** | Outcome + implicit comparison | Problem-aware | "Actually" quietly contrasts against every app that failed them, without naming a competitor or making an unsubstantiated superlative claim. |
| 6 | **"Cycle predictions that don't miss"** | Outcome — colloquial register | Problem-aware | Same core claim as #4/#5 but a different voice (slangy, confident) — legitimate as a tone/formula test, cheapest slot to fill. |

## Cut, and why

- ❌ **"Your cycle, predicted accurately" / "Accurate cycle predictions, every month" / "Track your cycle with total accuracy" / "Accuracy-first cycle tracking"** — straight synonym rewords of #4/#5/#6 above. You could swap any of these headlines with each other and the ad would argue nothing different. Testing them teaches you about adjective preference, not the market.
- ⚠️ **"The most accurate way to track your cycle" / "Your most accurate cycle forecast"** — benched, not killed. Two problems: (1) you haven't confirmed you can substantiate "most accurate" — Meta routinely flags unsubstantiated superlatives, and I won't ship a claim without that confirmation; (2) comparison/superlative claims are a solution-aware or product-aware argument (proof vs. the category), and your traffic is problem-aware, where naming the problem beats naming a rank. If you can back "most accurate" with a real number, run it as its own follow-up test — don't fold it into this one.

## Flags before you launch

- 🔴 **Nobody mined the 200 reviews yet.** Every headline above is a generic claim, not a customer-sourced angle. Somewhere in 200 reviews there's almost certainly a sharper pain point ("I stopped trusting every app until this one," irregular-cycle users specifically, a specific month-count of correct predictions) that would beat all 12 of these. Treat this round as a placeholder test and mine the reviews for round 2 — that's where the real lift is.
- 🔴 **Message match unconfirmed.** I don't have your landing page headline/promise. Before launch, check each of the 6 against it — if the page doesn't back up "trust" or "confidence" language specifically, that's a conversion leak, not just a copy problem.
- 🟡 **Confirm character limits in Ads Manager preview**, not from me — all 6 sit around 34–41 characters, which is normally safe for Feed, but the preview is the final authority, not a remembered spec.
- ℹ️ These are headlines only. You'll still need primary text / description / CTA per headline, and a check for whether Advantage+ text variations are on (they can rewrite what you're testing at delivery, which changes what "variant" even means for your results).
