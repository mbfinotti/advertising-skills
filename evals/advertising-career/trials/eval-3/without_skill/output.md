Honest take: it's not a good plan as written. Here's why, and what I'd do instead.

## The core problem

Certifications don't get media buyer jobs — a portfolio of live campaigns does. Hiring managers (and the agencies/brands who actually pay media buyer salaries) know that Google Ads and Meta Blueprint exams are multiple-choice tests you can pass by memorizing an afternoon of study material. They signal "I read the docs," not "I can manage spend." Five months spent stacking six certification logos with zero campaign experience is going to produce a CV that looks like everyone else's bootcamp graduate — and there are a lot of those.

What actually gets you hired as a media buyer:
- **Real ad spend under your management** — even $500-1000 of your own money run through Meta/Google, with before/after metrics (CPA, ROAS, CTR trends), beats a stack of certificates.
- **Case studies** — 2-3 campaigns where you can explain the strategy, what broke, what you changed, and the result. This is what interviews actually probe.
- **One platform mastered deeply**, not six shallow ones.

## Specific issues with your plan

1. **Order is backwards.** You're sequencing by "prestige" (Google → Meta → others) rather than by market demand. In most markets, **Meta Ads is the highest-demand skill** for entry-level media buyer roles (ecommerce/DTC brands hire more Meta buyers than Google Search buyers), so if anything it should come first or in parallel.

2. **$450 on three Meta Blueprint exams is a poor allocation.** Meta Blueprint certifications have limited exam-center availability in many countries and the content skews toward Meta's own product taxonomy, not buying strategy. That $450 would go further as actual ad spend (see below) or on a paid course with a mentor/community (e.g., a solid Meta Ads or performance marketing course) that teaches you to read Ads Manager data, not pass a quiz.

3. **Six certifications is scope creep, not focus.** TikTok, Amazon Ads, and LinkedIn Ads are genuinely different disciplines (Amazon Ads especially — it's closer to retail/marketplace economics than social buying). Spreading five months across six platforms means shallow competence everywhere and depth nowhere. Employers hire for depth on 1-2 platforms + adjacent literacy, not breadth across six.

4. **No mention of running actual campaigns.** This is the biggest gap. A CV with 6 certs and 0 campaigns run is weaker than a CV with 1 cert and $1,500 of self-funded ad spend you can talk through in detail.

5. **Your retail management background is an asset you're not using.** Six years managing a store means you understand unit economics, inventory/seasonality, customer conversion funnels, and P&L thinking — all directly relevant to performance marketing (especially ecommerce media buying). Frame this explicitly; don't bury it under generic certs. Agencies hiring junior buyers often value candidates who already understand a P&L over candidates who only know ad platform UIs.

## A better 5-month plan

**Month 1** — Pick ONE primary platform based on where you want to work (Meta if targeting ecommerce/DTC agencies, Google if targeting search-heavy/B2B agencies). Do Google Ads *Search* certification (free, ~1 week) as a baseline regardless — it's free and quick, so keep it, just don't over-invest in exam volume.

**Month 1-2** — Take one paid, practitioner-led course (not just the vendor's own certification) on your primary platform. Use $150-200 of your $450 budget here instead of on redundant Blueprint exams.

**Month 2-4** — Run real campaigns:
- Set up a small ecommerce store, an affiliate offer, or volunteer to run ads for a local business/nonprofit for free or cheap, using $300-500 of your own money as ad spend (or partner with a friend's small business).
- Document everything: hypotheses, budget decisions, what you optimized, results. This becomes your portfolio/case studies.

**Month 4-5** — Apply, using:
- 1 primary certification (the one matching your target platform)
- 2-3 documented case studies with real numbers
- Your retail management story reframed around funnel/conversion/budget ownership
- Optionally the free Google Ads Search cert as a secondary credential

Skip Blueprint's paid exam stack, skip TikTok/Amazon/LinkedIn certs entirely unless a specific job posting asks for them — you can always add a platform cert reactively once you're job-hunting and see what recruiters actually ask for.

**Bottom line:** trade "six certs, zero campaigns" for "one cert, one deep skill, real spend, documented results." That's the CV that gets interviews.
