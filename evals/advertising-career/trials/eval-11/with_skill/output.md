## Both friends are wrong, and here's the mechanism, not just the verdict

The skill ranks six entry paths on efficiency, on value (speed to a track record employers screen for), and on effort:

- **efficiency:** agency junior > self-funded own spend > lateral move > in-house coordinator > apprenticeship > freelance-first
- **value:** agency junior > in-house coordinator > lateral move > self-funded own spend > freelance-first > apprenticeship
- **effort:** freelance-first > apprenticeship > agency junior > in-house coordinator > self-funded own spend > lateral move

Your answer to the effort question — evenings only, must stay reversible — **deletes** freelance-first and apprenticeship from your menu. Not demotes: deletes. Freelance is a standing client-acquisition job that runs for years before it produces proof, on top of the craft — that's more than evenings, and it carries income volatility that fails your no-pay-cut constraint outright. The Level 3 apprenticeship pays £11,000–£11,500 (funding band, 8–15 months) and locks you to one employer for the duration — that's a direct pay cut from where a 2-year marketing executive already sits (UK survey median for that band: £33,815, Marketing Week 2026 Career & Salary Survey, 2,350 respondents, 16 April 2026 — and that figure is _down_ from £36,106 in 2025, so the market is contracting, not growing). Both friends are recommending the two options your own constraints rule out.

**Deleted by name:** freelance-first (standing sales job, income risk), UK Level 3 apprenticeship (pay cut, single-employer lock-in).
**Also flag:** agency junior — UK postings cluster £24,000–£30,000 (itjobswatch, 2025 postings). That's very likely a pay cut from your current level too. Don't take an agency-junior offer without checking it against your current salary; don't rule it out either if a specific offer matches or beats your pay — the number is what decides it, not the label.

## What the constraints actually point to: lateral move

You already sit in an adjacent seat (SEO exec, same industry, same company or same sector). The skill promotes the lateral move to first choice exactly when that's true, because it's the only path that keeps the salary, the employer, and the reversibility every other rung spends. That's a direct match to all three of your stated constraints at once — this isn't a coincidence, it's the reason the answer to "where are you now" moves the order.

**Run these two in parallel, both fit inside evenings:**

1. **Push for the lateral move internally.** Ask your manager for a defined slice of paid-media ownership — even a small always-on Google/Meta budget — inside your current role. It costs a conversation, not a resignation, and it's fully reversible. Observable signal: you're handed budget or forecast responsibility on a paid channel, with a date attached.
2. **Run self-funded spend in evenings, starting now, regardless of whether #1 lands.** Small store, affiliate offer, or a pro-bono local business. This is what makes any lateral pitch or later application answerable — "I've done SEO for two years" doesn't demonstrate you can run and optimise paid spend; a self-funded campaign does. It's the credible no-experience proof, and it doubles as your portfolio's core. Effort: a quarter of evenings plus a small budget of your own money — no pay-cut exposure, fully within your evenings ceiling.

What this menu starves: agency junior is the highest-value, highest-efficiency path on paper, but it's parked because it fails your pay constraint by default. Promote it only if a specific offer matches or beats your current pay — don't chase it on the label alone.

## Certifications — table stakes, not a strategy

Do these in evenings, they cost almost nothing and their absence reads as carelessness:

- **Google Skillshop** — free, 75-minute open-book exam, 12-month expiry. Do this first.
- **LinkedIn Marketing Labs, TikTok Academy, Amazon Ads** — all free. Take LinkedIn if your travel company has any B2B angle (corporate travel, agency partnerships); take TikTok if the audience is consumer/short-form.
- **Meta Blueprint** (~$150) — only if a target listing names it. Don't fund it speculatively.

None of these move a hiring or promotion decision on their own. Never let a certification stand in for the self-funded spend proof above.

## Portfolio — you have no PPC to anonymise yet, so lead differently

Normally the skill ranks anonymised client work above self-funded spend on raw value (real budget, real stakes). You don't have that option yet — your existing work is SEO, not paid. So your portfolio is built from the two sources that carry zero confidentiality risk:

1. **A public written teardown** of a competitor's or another travel brand's paid ads — an hour of work, and it doubles as rehearsal for the live-audit interview format, which is the format that decides the most in paid-media hiring.
2. **The self-funded spend campaign** from above, written up with process and directional (percentage/indexed) outcomes — never absolute revenue or spend figures.

Once you land any paid budget internally, fold in anonymised examples using: descriptor not client name, indexed/percentage results only, no raw account screenshots. Template and worked example: `references/anonymised-case-study-example.md` in this skill.

## Benchmarks to hold yourself to

- **3 months:** you've either secured a defined slice of paid budget internally, or you have a clear "no" from your manager.
- **6 months:** if the internal move hasn't landed and you have no paid-media interviews from external applications, the gap is demonstrable results, not more certifications — that's the default benchmark this skill sets for anyone entering the field, and it applies to you too. At that point, re-open the agency-junior question against your actual current pay, and reconsider geography/channel scope rather than defaulting to freelance or the apprenticeship — those stay deleted unless your effort ceiling itself changes (e.g., you decide you're willing to take a temporary income hit, which you've said you're not).

## The honest tension to sit with

"No pay cut" and "fastest way in" pull in different directions in this market — the freelance day rate (UK median £300, itjobswatch, 6 months to 17 July 2025, down ~15% YoY) is the top of the entry-path menu on raw ceiling, and it's exactly the number both friends are pricing in. It loses on your menu not because it's a bad path in general, but because it's a standing sales job before any proof exists, and your evenings-only, must-stay-reversible constraint can't fund that. If autonomy rather than reps turns out to be what you're actually short of, and you later become willing to drop income for a period, freelance moves back onto the menu — but that's a different constraint set than the one you described today.
