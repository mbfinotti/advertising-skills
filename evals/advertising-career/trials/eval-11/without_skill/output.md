## Your situation in one line

You have 2 years of SEO experience and want paid media, but you're optimizing for certainty over speed — that changes which advice applies.

## Where your friends are each half right

**Freelancing is fastest — for someone who already has the skill.** You don't yet. Clients pay for proven ability to not waste their ad budget. Going freelance first means learning on strangers' money, which is slow to sell and risky to your reputation. Flip the order: build proof first, freelance later if you still want to.

**The Level 3 apprenticeship builds real skill — but structurally conflicts with your constraints.** Apprenticeships are employment contracts with lower pay bands and daytime off-the-job training hours. That means either quitting your job or taking a pay cut for a new one. It only fits your constraints if your _current_ employer funds it for existing staff (common at larger companies via the UK apprenticeship levy) — worth one conversation to check, otherwise this path is closed to you as stated.

## The route that actually fits your constraints

SEO and paid media are adjacent, not separate — you already have transferable assets: keyword/intent research, GA4/GSC, conversion funnels, stakeholder reporting. Paid search (Google Ads) is the shortest hop; paid social is a bigger jump (creative + audience targeting).

1. **This week — one conversation, zero cost.** Ask your manager or L&D whether the company funds Google/Meta certifications or apprenticeships for existing staff, and whether there's any paid media work you could shadow or pick up alongside your SEO role. If yes, this alone can get you to paid media with no pay cut and no quitting — the "certain" path your friends' options don't offer.

2. **Weeks 1–4, evenings.** Get Google Ads (Search) and Meta Blueprint certifications. Free, ~10–15 hours total, legitimate credentials that show intent and baseline competence.

3. **Weeks 4–8, evenings.** Run one small campaign with your own money — a personal project, affiliate site, or a friend's small business. Spend £100–300, track results, write it up as a case study. This is the "proof of skill" freelancing skips straight past, done at controlled risk.

4. **Month 2–3.** With certs + a real case study, push for an internal lateral move first (no pay cut, since it's a transfer, not a new hire). If no internal opening exists, apply externally for junior/mid paid media roles _while still employed_ — resign only once you have a signed offer, which is what "certain" actually looks like.

5. **Freelancing, if you still want it, comes after step 3** — once you have certs and a case study to sell, not before. Check your employment contract for moonlighting/conflict-of-interest clauses first, since you work at a travel company and any side client work in the same space could breach it.

## Bottom line

Don't take either friend's advice as-is. Skip the apprenticeship unless your employer funds it without a pay cut. Treat freelancing as the _last_ step, not the first. The realistic evening-only path is: certify → prove with a self-funded campaign → move internally or apply externally with an offer in hand before you resign.
