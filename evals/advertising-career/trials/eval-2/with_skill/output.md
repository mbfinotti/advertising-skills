**The two-minute answer:**

"There's a common misconception here worth clearing up first: Chrome didn't deprecate third-party cookies — Google reversed that plan in 2024 and confirmed it again in 2025. Cookies are still on by default there. What actually broke tracking was Apple's App Tracking Transparency in 2021, plus the fact that Safari and Firefox already block third-party cookies by default. So signal loss has been real and gradual, not a single cliff-edge — and it's already happened for a meaningful share of traffic.

For a furniture retailer specifically, that matters more than for a low-consideration category. Your customers research for weeks, cross-device — mobile at lunch, desktop at night, sometimes a showroom visit in between — so every extra touchpoint is another place attribution can drop the thread. The platforms compensate with modeled and view-through conversions, which is exactly why platform-reported ROAS tends to run hot: each channel is grading its own homework.

My playbook has three parts. First, first-party data — but earned properly: account creation, newsletter signup, a room-planner or financing pre-qualification tool, all tied to a persistent customer ID, including in-store capture. Second, close the technical gap with server-side tracking — Conversions API on Meta, Enhanced Conversions on Google — deduplicated against the order system, so we're recovering observed conversions instead of relying on estimates. Third, and this is the part people skip: don't trust any single platform's number. I'd anchor the team on blended MER — total revenue over total spend — and run periodic incrementality tests, geo holdouts especially, because for a business with a showroom, a meaningful chunk of revenue never touches a pixel at all.

So the real shift isn't 'prepare for cookies disappearing' — it's building measurement that assumes incomplete signal by default, and treating first-party data and incrementality testing as the correction, not cookies as the crisis."
