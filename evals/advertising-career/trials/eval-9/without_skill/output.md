**In-house, at £48K.**

**Why the freelance math is softer than it looks**

£300/day × 5 days × 52 weeks = £78K is a spreadsheet fantasy, not a forecast. Nobody bills 260 days in year one. Strip out sales time, gaps between contracts, holidays, sick days (unpaid), and admin, and realistic utilisation for a new freelancer is 55–65%. That's £43–51K gross — before accountant fees, PI insurance (non-negotiable when you're touching client ad spend), pension, and the tax you now have to save yourself. Net, you're likely looking at parity with the in-house offer or worse, with none of the stability.

**The "hint" isn't a pipeline**

One former client hinting at a project is not a contract. It's not even a verbal commitment. Building a freelance business on one soft lead means the first 3-6 months are spent selling, not billing — exactly the runway you don't have on £41K savings and zero buffer.

**Burnout is the actual problem, and only one option fixes it**

You're fried from context-switching across 8 accounts. Freelance doesn't reduce that — it usually adds a 9th "account" (running your own business: invoicing, chasing payment, finding the next client) on top of delivery work, solo, with no team to absorb the load. In-house collapses you to *one* brand, one stakeholder set, one campaign calendar. That's a direct fix, not a hope.

**What in-house actually gets you**

- 17% pay rise, immediately, not "if utilisation holds"
- Depth over breadth — you get to actually own a strategy instead of triaging 8 accounts shallowly, which is usually what "senior" growth looks like after agency-side generalist years
- Benefits, sick pay, holiday pay, pension — real money freelance quietly strips away
- A stable base from which freelancing later is a *choice*, not a bet made while exhausted

**Don't fully burn the freelance option — de-risk it instead**

Take the in-house job. Then go back to the former client and ask what "sending a project" actually means — get it in writing, get a scope, get a rate. If it's real and recurring, you can revisit freelancing in 12–18 months with an actual pipeline (that client, plus whatever else you build on the side, contract permitting) instead of one hint and a day-rate you saw on a job board. Jumping to freelance now is trading a known, managed stress for an unmanaged one, on weaker numbers than they first appear.
