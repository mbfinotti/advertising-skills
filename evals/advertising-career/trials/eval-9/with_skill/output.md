## The freelance math needs correcting before it drives anything

£300/day × 233 working days = £70K. That arithmetic assumes zero downtime: no gaps between contracts, no time spent finding the next client, no holidays, no admin — full utilization from day one, off a single *hinted* lead. That's not how a freelance year one runs. A more realistic first-year utilization (100–150 billable days while you're also doing the unpaid job of finding clients) puts you at **£30K–£45K**, not £70K — and that's before you net out self-employed tax/NI handling, no pension match, no sick pay, and accounting overhead that a payslip absorbs for you.

The rate itself is also softening, not a stable anchor: **itjobswatch, 6 months to 17 July 2025 — UK median PPC contractor day rate £300 (25th–75th £275–£325), down ~15% year-on-year.** A year-old, declining figure shouldn't be the number that tips a career decision.

None of this makes freelance a bad idea — it makes "one hinted client = £70K" a bad basis for choosing it *now*. A hint is not a signed contract, and one lead is not a network. The skill's own logic on this: freelance loses to in-house at your stage on effort, because its cost isn't the work, it's running a standing sales job for years before any track record exists — and the high day rate is exactly what that ratio has already priced in and still rejects it as a first move.

## Where in-house wins on the numbers you do have

**Marketing Week 2026 Career & Salary Survey (2,350 respondents, 16 April 2026):** Junior manager £43,173, Senior manager £60,577 — both down from 2025, so don't anchor on last year's figures either. Your current £41K sits *below* the junior-manager benchmark. £48K clears it and lands you inside **3Search's 2025 PPC Manager band (£50K–£70K, 1,300+ respondents)** — a real, certain, immediate raise (+17%), plus the things a payslip gives you that a day rate doesn't: pension, holiday pay, sick pay, income stability.

And on burnout specifically — the same survey found **65.3% of marketers felt overwhelmed, 60.7% undervalued, 55.1% emotionally exhausted, 51.2% saw remit grow without matching pay**, over the prior 12 months. You're not an outlier; you're the base rate. Freelance doesn't fix overload, it adds a second axis of it (client-getting on top of delivery) right when you have the least slack for it. In-house carries "moderate" burnout risk against agency's "highest — volume plus client demands" and freelance's self-managed income risk. Given you're already fried, that's the ordering that matters most right now, not the headline rate.

## The recommendation

**Take the in-house role — conditionally.** Before you sign, get a straight answer on whether it owns budget/forecast, or ask for it explicitly if the offer is vague: that's the senior-track signal, and without it £48K is a nicer job title on the same execution work. If they confirm it, take it — it's a certain raise, it clears your burnout risk down a level, and it's the stage-appropriate move (in-house beats both agency and freelance mid-level onward once reps stop being the scarce resource).

**Don't let the former client's hint go cold, but don't leap on it yet.** Ask them to make it concrete — scope, rate, timeline — and if it's real, take it as a small paid side project alongside the new job, not as your income. That converts "a hint" into an actual track record and a tested client relationship without you carrying the standing-sales-job cost while you're still depleted. That's the one thing that would flip this recommendation: if that relationship turns into two or three reliable clients rather than one hinted project, freelance's effort case changes completely — reopen the decision then, not now.

**What this deletes, by name:** staying at the agency at £41K running 8 accounts is off the table — it's dominated on both pay and burnout by the in-house offer, not merely ranked below it. Going full freelance *now*, on one unconfirmed lead, while burnt out, is also off the table — not "last on the list," genuinely not actionable yet given what you've described.

**Set a date to revisit:** give the in-house role 6 months. If budget/forecast ownership hasn't actually materialized by then, that's your signal the "senior" title isn't senior-track there — and by then you'll know whether the former client relationship has turned into something freelance-shaped, so you re-decide with real information on both sides instead of a day-rate headline.
