## Before you open a single tab: this brief is the documented failure pattern

"A few hours" briefing a full go-to-market plan (channel strategy + budgets + audience research + creative concepts + launch calendar + **12-slide deck**) for an unlaunched product line is exactly the scenario candidates report inflating to 12–30+ unpaid hours — and unpaid take-home ideas being implemented after a ghost is documented. Ask A Manager's standard: an hour or two is reasonable; a complex project isn't, paid or not.

**Your 20 blocked hours are the trap, not the resource.** The plan below caps real work at ~4 hours and gets you back most of your weekend.

## Step 0 — Push back, before doing any work (10 min)

Message Lumenmatic now: _"I'll time-box this to a few hours as briefed and focus on the channel/budget reasoning — a fully-realized 12-slide GTM plan with researched audiences and a launch calendar would normally be a paid scope. Here's what I'll cover in that window: [list below]."_

This is the single highest-leverage move available and it costs 10 minutes. It also signals seniority — a senior hire is expected to scope, not absorb.

## Step 1 — Set the real time box: 4 hours, not 20

Reclaim the other ~16 hours. Sinking a weekend into this doesn't move the hiring decision further than a tight 4 hours does — a take-home is the one format where extra hours buy almost nothing once the reasoning is on the page.

## Step 2 — Work order inside the 4 hours

Ordered by what the round actually screens for per hour spent, most valuable first:

1. **Channel strategy + budget logic** (~1.5h) — do this first, while fresh. This is the senior-track signal (cross-channel allocation, budget reasoning), and it's the frame everything else hangs off. Pro-installer is a B2B/trade motion, not consumer paid social: lead with search + trade-press/LinkedIn-style professional channels, not TikTok.
2. **Audience research** (~45 min) — directional installer personas (electricians, GCs, smart-home integrators) from public info and stated assumptions. Not primary research — that's a deleted option here, not a shortcut; it isn't reachable in 4 hours and faking it reads worse than not having it.
3. **Creative concepts** (~1h) — 2–3 angles per top channel, headline-level. Enough to show craft, not a full creative suite.
4. **Launch calendar** (~30 min) — a simple three-phase timeline (teaser → launch → scale). Skip day-by-day granularity.
5. **12-slide deck** (~45 min) — assemble into a plain template, one idea per slide, lead every slide with the reasoning or the one number that matters. This is deliberately starved of design polish: "lead with reasoning, not production polish" is the explicit rule for this format, and visual polish is the most expensive, lowest-signal line item on this list.

## What to leave out, by name

- Primary audience research (interviews/surveys) — not real without paying for it, so don't fabricate it.
- A fully worked, channel-by-channel media plan with exact CPM/CPA forecasts — you have no historical data to forecast from; state that as an assumption instead of inventing numbers.
- Polished slide design (custom graphics, brand system) — costs the most hours, moves the decision least.
- Competitive teardown beyond 2–3 directional examples — a full audit of the pro-installer competitive set is a paid-scope task, not a take-home task.

## State this on slide 1 or 2 of the deck

- Time-boxed to ~4 hours as briefed.
- Assumptions: [target segment definition, no historical spend data, no access to Lumenmatic's CRM/analytics].
- "A full plan would need: real audience data, existing funnel metrics, and confirmed launch budget — happy to build that out in a paid scope or the next round."

## Idea-theft guardrail

Keep creative concepts at the headline/angle level, not fully art-directed executions. Directional and defensible is the right resolution for a free round — it's also what a hiring manager screens for over a slick, over-produced deck; a generic tool-polished output reads exactly like the AI-generated audits interviewers are trained to reject.

## If they push back on the scope-down

That's your answer about the employer, not about the brief. A company that won't accept a time-boxed, reasoning-led version of this deliverable is telling you how take-homes — and probably scope in general — work there.
