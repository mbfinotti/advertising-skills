## Before the schedule: one flag worth 30 seconds

A "few hours" brief asking for channel strategy + budgets + audience research + creative concepts + launch calendar + a 12-slide deck, for a real unlaunched product line, is a known pattern for extracting free strategic work under the cover of a "take-home." You've already priced this correctly by blocking 20 hours instead of trusting the "few hours" framing. Two things worth doing regardless of what you decide about the ethics of it:

- Cap depth deliberately — go directionally sharp, not agency-grade. Real media-buy-precision budgets and exhaustive competitor teardowns are unpaid work product; a senior hire signals judgment by scoping ruthlessly, not by grinding 20 hours into a finished plan.
- State your assumptions and scope cuts on slide 1 or 2 of the deck. It reads as seniority, not corner-cutting, and it's your paper trail if scope is ever questioned.

## Order of work — 4 sessions, ~20h

**1. Evening 1 (~2.5h) — Scope lock + audience research start**
- 30 min: Lock the brief. One product, one primary buyer (pro installer — electricians, integrators, smart-home dealers), one core objective/KPI, one budget ceiling assumption. Write these down; they gate every later decision.
- 2h: Audience research. Who buys, who specs, who influences the install decision. Buying triggers, trusted channels, trade-show/distributor dynamics. Note 2-3 competitor plays (Lutron, Control4, similar) for context, not a full teardown.

**2. Evening 2 (~2.5h) — Finish research, start channel strategy**
- 1h: Close out audience research into 1-2 crisp personas (not five).
- 1.5h: Draft channel shortlist. For B2B trade audiences: trade pubs/programmatic, LinkedIn, distributor/partner co-op, trade shows/events, installer email/CRM, intent-based paid search. Map each to a funnel stage (awareness/consideration/conversion).

**3. Saturday (~7.5h) — Strategy, budget, creative**
- 1.5h: Finalize channel strategy — pick 3-4 channels, justify the cut.
- 2h: Budget allocation across launch phases (pre-launch/launch/sustain). One primary scenario, brief note on flex — not three fully-worked scenarios.
- 2.5h: Creative concepts — 2-3 campaign directions with headline, value prop, and visual/tone notes for a trade (not consumer) audience.
- 1.5h: Launch calendar skeleton — pre-launch, launch week, post-launch, mapped to the chosen channels.

**4. Sunday (~7.5h) — Deck build and polish**
- 4h: Build the 12 slides from what's already drafted. Suggested arc: problem/opportunity → audience → strategy → channels → budget → creative → calendar → KPIs/next steps → assumptions/scope note.
- 2h: Buffer for the parts that always take longer than planned (usually creative and budget).
- 1.5h: Polish pass — tighten narrative, check numbers are internally consistent, prep talking points if you'll present it live.

## Why this order

- Research before strategy — channels and budget are meaningless without a locked audience.
- Strategy before creative — creative concepts should serve the chosen channels, not the other way round.
- Calendar after budget/channels — it's just sequencing what's already decided, so it goes fast.
- Deck last, always — it's assembly, not new thinking. Building it early means rebuilding it every time an earlier phase shifts.
