## Is £75k right? Probably not — the benchmark doesn't match the role

Three gaps between your source and your actual situation:

1. **"Senior Marketing Manager" ≠ "Senior Paid Media Manager."** The £72k figure is a generalist title covering brand, product, and channel-agnostic marketing managers, often with bigger teams and broader P&L ownership. Paid media is a specialist track — sometimes it commands a premium (scarce, revenue-attributable skill), sometimes it's benchmarked lower because it's an individual-contributor/channel role rather than a general management one. It's not a safe stand-in.

2. **UK-wide ≠ Manchester.** National averages in marketing surveys are usually London-weighted. Manchester (and the North West generally) typically runs **15–25% below London** for equivalent marketing roles, though less of a gap than a few years ago given remote/hybrid norms. If that £72k figure includes London respondents, Manchester-market-rate for the same title could realistically be £55–65k.

3. **"Senior" is not standardised.** One company's "Senior Paid Media Manager" is another's "Head of Performance" or "Paid Media Lead." What matters more than the title is: team size you'd manage, total media budget you'd own, and whether it's in-house retail vs. agency-side (in-house retail often pays slightly less base but adds staff discount/bonus/stability).

**Net effect:** anchoring at £75k off a mismatched benchmark risks either underselling yourself (if this is genuinely a senior, budget-owning role at a well-funded retailer) or looking out of touch with market rate (if it's a mid-senior individual contributor role in a sector that isn't as flush as tech/SaaS). Before next week, spend 20 minutes cross-checking: search "paid media manager" + "Manchester" on Glassdoor/Totaljobs/Reed for live postings' advertised ranges, and if you know any recruiters in Manchester digital/marketing, ask what they're placing similar roles at right now — that's a much better anchor than a generic national survey.

## What to actually anchor on

Build your number from three inputs, not one survey:

- **Your current base salary** + the realistic increase for a lateral-to-upward move (typically 10–20%, more if you're closing a scope gap).
- **Live Manchester job postings** for equivalent titles (paid media, performance marketing, PPC — senior level) — these show what employers are actually budgeting, not just what current post-holders self-report.
- **The specific scope of this role** — team size, budget under management, channels owned. A £2m media budget with 2 direct reports justifies a different number than a solo contributor role.

Give **a range, not a single figure** — e.g. "£68,000–£78,000 depending on the full package," where the bottom of your range is a number you're genuinely happy to accept. A single anchor number invites them to negotiate down from it; a range gives you room and signals confidence without rigidity.

## How to phrase it in the interview

Don't lead with a bare number — frame it, then give the range, then open it back up:

> "Based on my research into paid media leadership roles in Manchester and my current package, I'm looking at a base in the region of **£X–£Y**, though I'd want to understand the full picture — bonus structure, benefits, and how the role's scope is defined — before pinning that down. What range has the business budgeted for this position?"

Why this works:

- **Anchors with a range, not a point** — harder to negotiate down, easier to defend.
- **Cites your own research explicitly** — signals you've done your homework and aren't just guessing.
- **Immediately asks total comp, not just base** — retail roles often have meaningful bonus/discount/benefits that change the real number.
- **Turns it back to them** ("what range has the business budgeted") — this is the single highest-leverage line. It's final round; they've already decided they want you. Making them state their number first, or at least react to yours, tells you whether your range is calibrated before you commit to it.

If pushed to give a single figure, state the **midpoint of your range**, not the top — it reads as a considered number rather than an opening bid you expect to be talked down from.
