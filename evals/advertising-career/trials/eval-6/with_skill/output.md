## The plan as stated has a problem

Running your agency's audit tool and walking two agency leads through its 40-point output *is* the exact failure mode this interview format is designed to catch. Jason Parks, an agency owner who runs this exact round, names it directly: *"It's easy to send off a generic audit generated by third-party software"* — and that's what he's screening against. Two paid-search leads watching a live audit will recognize a tool report on sight, and reading it aloud (even confidently) reads as a lack of independent judgment, not competence.

**Keep the tool. Change what you present.** Use it as your data-gathering pass before the call, or silently in the first few minutes on screen — then build your own prioritized point of view on top of it. The deliverable they're grading is your judgment, not the tool's coverage.

## Restructure: 45 minutes, 5 movements

Scale the standard live-audit skeleton from its 10-minute form to your 45, spending most of the added time on synthesis and Q&A, not on more checklist items.

**1. Orient — 5 min.** State the business model, the primary conversion action, and what "good" looks like for this account *before* opening a single setting. Say it out loud even if it seems obvious — it signals you diagnose top-down, not tool-down.

**2. Money first — 12 min.** Where spend concentrates. Whether the top spenders hit target CPA/ROAS. The single biggest waste line. This is where most of your 40 checklist points actually live (budget pacing, wasted spend, bid strategy fit) — collapse them into 2-3 dollar-weighted findings, not a scrolling list.

**3. Structure and signal — 12 min.** Campaign/ad-group logic, conversion-tracking sanity, bidding fit to the goal. This is where the rest of the checklist lives (naming conventions, match types, negatives, extensions, Quality Score). Same rule: cluster into findings, don't enumerate.

**4. Prioritised recommendation — 8 min.** Your top three changes, ranked by expected impact, each with the metric that would prove it worked. This is the section that actually gets scored — spend disproportionate rehearsal time here.

**5. What you'd need next — 3 min.** Name the data you couldn't see and would request (offline conversions, margin by SKU, historical Change History, attribution settings). This reads as rigor, not a gap.

**Buffer — 5 min.** Two leads in the room means more interruption than a single interviewer. Leave room for them to push back mid-walkthrough rather than treating this as five sealed blocks.

## Mechanics for compressing 40 points into a POV

- Before the call, run the tool and sort its output into exactly three buckets: **money** (spend/waste/bidding), **structure & signal** (tracking/campaign logic/QS), **backlog** (real but lower-leverage — this becomes your "what I'd need next," not something you present live).
- For every item you do surface, attach impact and a fix, not just a flag: "Broad match on non-brand terms is ~30% of spend with 2x the CPA of the account average → move to phrase, review search terms weekly." A checklist line without a number reads as the tool's voice, not yours.
- Rehearse the money-first section out loud at least once against a real account before Thursday — composure under a live screen-share is graded independently of the analysis.

## Two leads in the room

Expect one to probe technical depth (tracking setup, bidding mechanics, auction signals) and the other to probe the narrative (how you'd say this to a client, what you'd do first Monday morning). Close your top-3 recommendations with the client-facing line for each, not just the technical fix — that answers both leads without switching modes.

## One boundary to hold

The audit tool and its 40-point framework belong to your current agency. Presenting the *output categories* as your own analytical structure is fine — that's just a checklist you've internalized. Don't narrate the tool's name, scoring methodology, or proprietary weighting on someone else's screen-share; keep the borrowed structure invisible and the judgment visibly yours.
