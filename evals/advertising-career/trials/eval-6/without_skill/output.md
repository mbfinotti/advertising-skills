# Structuring a 45-Minute Live Ads Audit Walkthrough

## The core problem to solve first

A 40-point checklist read top-to-bottom in 45 minutes is a data dump, not an audit. Two leads at a performance agency aren't hiring someone who can run a tool — they're hiring someone who can **triage, prioritize, and communicate under pressure** on an account they've never seen. Structure the walkthrough to demonstrate that, not to complete the checklist.

## Recommended structure (45 min)

**1. Frame it before you touch anything (3 min)**
- State your process out loud: "I'll run the full 40-point check in the background, then walk you through it by business impact, not by checklist order."
- Ask 2-3 context questions first: account goal (leads/ROAS/volume), monthly spend, who's the target customer. Don't skip this — auditing blind and ignoring context is a red flag for a manager-level hire.

**2. Run the tool, narrate while it loads (2 min)**
- Talk through what the tool checks (structure, tracking, bidding, budget pacing, negatives, ad relevance, etc.) while it processes — fills dead air and shows you understand the tool isn't a black box.

**3. Snapshot first, not the list (5 min)**
- Before the 40 points: give a 60-second account health summary. "Campaign structure is reasonable, conversion tracking has a gap, budget is capped on the top performer, quality scores are mixed." This is the single highest-signal moment of the interview — do it well.

**4. Triage the 40 points into 3 buckets, live (25 min)**
Don't go in checklist order. Re-sort out loud into:
- 🔴 **Account-breaking** (tracking gaps, conversion mismatches, budget-limited campaigns, negative keyword conflicts cannibalizing spend) — 4-6 items, deep dive
- 🟠 **Meaningful but not urgent** (ad copy testing gaps, audience layering, bid strategy mismatch) — mention briefly, group them
- 🟢 **Hygiene/minor** (naming conventions, unused labels, sitelink count) — one sentence, batch them: "these six are hygiene, I'd fix them in week one but they're not moving the needle"

This sorting *is* the skill they're testing. Spend your time budget proportional to impact, not proportional to checklist length.

**5. Pick one issue and go deep (5-7 min)**
- Choose the single highest-impact red item and actually diagnose it: what you see, why it's likely happening, what you'd check next, what the fix is and expected impact. This proves you can operate beyond the tool's output.

**6. Close with a plan, not a list (3-5 min)**
- "If I owned this account starting Monday: fix tracking day 1, restructure the budget-capped campaign week 1, ad copy testing week 2-3, hygiene items whenever." A prioritized action plan is what a client actually pays for — end on it.

**7. Leave room for their questions throughout**
- Don't treat this as a monologue. Pause after the snapshot and after the red bucket: "Does that match what you'd expect from this account, or is there context I'm missing?" Two leads watching a screen-share will judge collaboration as much as output.

## Things that will hurt you

- **Reading the tool's output verbatim.** They can run the tool themselves. Your value is interpretation.
- **Treating all 40 points as equal.** Signals you can't prioritize under real client constraints.
- **Never saying "I don't know."** If the tool flags something ambiguous without account history, say so: "I'd normally check this against last quarter's data before calling it an issue." Fake confidence on a live account is worse than an honest gap.
- **Going over time on hygiene items.** Time-box buckets 2 and 3 hard — protect time for the deep dive and the close.
- **No point of view.** They're hiring a manager, not an analyst. The plan at the end is what separates "ran a tool" from "manages accounts."

## One thing to prep beforehand

Walk a live account with the tool once, timed, before Thursday. 40 points is a lot of surface area to triage live — knowing roughly how long your own narration takes per bucket will keep you from blowing the clock on buckets 2-3 and rushing the close, which is the part that actually gets you the offer.
