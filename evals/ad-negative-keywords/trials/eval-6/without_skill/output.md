## Recommendation: Meltbury Home Goods — Negative Keyword Strategy

**Bottom line:** Don't bulk-add the mega-list, don't set a 0% target, and don't move to daily reviews. All three moves work against each other and risk erasing four months of gains.

### 1. Reject the 0% wasted-spend target

- 0% non-brand waste is not achievable without cutting reach. Broad/phrase match and Smart Bidding rely on some exploration spend to keep finding new converting queries — that's a feature, not a leak.
- At 6%, you're already in solid-performer territory for a $40k/month non-brand book. Diminishing returns set in below ~5%: each additional point of "waste" removed increasingly comes from genuinely exploratory or long-tail converting queries, not junk.
- An account driven to 0% waste is a red flag, not a win — it usually means impression share and new-query discovery have been strangled. Watch for declining non-brand impression share as the real cost of chasing this number.
- Reframe the KPI: track wasted spend in **dollars with a floor** (e.g., hold 4–6% of non-brand), alongside conversion volume and CPA trend. A shrinking waste % next to shrinking conversions is not progress.

### 2. Do not bulk-add the 2,300-term mega-list

- Generic forum lists are built for someone else's catalog. Home goods has legitimate demand on terms other advertisers treat as junk (e.g. "wholesale," "bulk," "commercial," "supplier," "cheap" alternatives) — blindly applying them can cut real converting queries.
- Account-level negative keyword lists apply to Search campaigns; they do **not** reliably control Performance Max. If PMax is a meaningful share of the $40k, this list won't touch a chunk of your actual spend — check PMax search term insights separately before promising this moves the needle.
- Bulk, unvalidated changes destroy your ability to diagnose cause and effect. If conversions dip next week, you won't know which of 2,300 new negatives did it.

**Do this instead:**
1. Export the account's actual Search Terms report (last 90 days).
2. Cross-reference the mega-list against it — keep only terms with zero/near-zero conversions in *this* account's real data.
3. Manually screen for partial-match collateral damage (a broad negative like "free" or "cheap" can silently block converting queries containing those words).
4. Split survivors into two tiers: safe generic negatives (adult content, job search, unrelated verticals) → add account-level; anything home-goods-adjacent → add at campaign/ad group level with judgment, not in bulk.
5. Add in batches over 1–2 weeks, watching impression share and conversion volume after each batch — not all 2,300 at once.

### 3. Don't move to daily reviews

- The easy wins (irrelevant broad-match expansion) are already gone after 4 months of weekly cleanup. Daily reviews multiply analyst hours for marginal gains at this stage.
- Daily search-term volume per keyword is often too thin to make a confident negative call — you risk negating on 1–2 clicks that would have converted with more data (home goods has a multi-day consideration cycle; conversion lag is real).
- Keep the **weekly** cadence for full search-term review. Replace "more frequent manual review" with an automated anomaly alert (Google Ads Script or similar) that flags any search term spending above a $ threshold with zero conversions in real time — this catches the genuinely bad actors between weekly passes without burning daily analyst hours or triggering premature negation.

### This week's action plan

1. Run the mega-list against actual search-term data; expect to keep maybe 15–30% of it after filtering.
2. Add the vetted subset in 2–3 staged batches, not one bulk push.
3. Set up a daily automated spend-anomaly alert script instead of a manual daily review.
4. Replace the 0% target with a 4–6% waste-share band tracked alongside conversion volume, CPA, and non-brand impression share, so the team isn't rewarded for shrinking the account into invisibility.
5. Report back to your director with the before/after on impression share and conversions after batch 1, before continuing — that's the evidence that determines whether to keep tightening or hold.
