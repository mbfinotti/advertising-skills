# Format-fit verdict: Vantorra Legal demo-booking recommendations

**Bottom line: implement none of the three as written.** All three rest on the exact claims this skill treats as unreliable, and two of them point the account at a format that's structurally wrong for a considered, sales-assisted B2B purchase. Here's the row-by-row reasoning.

## (a) Move demo bookings to in-platform lead forms — reject

- The "13% vs 4%" comparison is on this skill's explicit discard list: it conflates different studies/audiences and ignores that qualified-opportunity rate frequently runs the _opposite_ direction, since landing-page friction filters for intent. A lead form that doubles raw conversion while halving lead-to-opportunity rate hasn't improved anything — and nobody in this brief measured that downstream number.
- The skill's destination-match rule is a hard decision rule, not a preference: **landing page ≥5% → keep sending traffic to it.** Yours converts at 6.5%. That alone settles it.
- It's explicitly named as demo/trial + calendar-booking: "a form creates friction for calendar-booking goals — use a page with an embedded calendar." You already have the embedded calendar.
- This is also a named misuse pattern: _"Lead form used to book sales meetings → form adds friction to a calendar goal → fix: landing page with an embedded calendar."_ The deck is recommending the misuse and calling it the fix.

**Verdict: misfit.** Keep the landing page as the demo-booking destination. If lead volume from cold/awareness placements needs a lighter-weight offer, use the in-platform form for a _different_ CTA (content/webinar registration, not demo booking) and keep both live rather than replacing one with the other.

## (b) 6-second bumper ads for demo requests — reject

- "8-second attention span, shorter than a goldfish" is the skill's first named discard-entirely metric — it doesn't describe real behavior and shouldn't gate a format decision.
- Structurally, bumper/non-skippable (≤6s) fits awareness, reach, recall — and is named structurally wrong for considered purchase and direct response: "a forced few seconds cannot carry a complex message." A demo request for B2B contract-review software is a considered, sales-assisted decision, not an impulse click.
- Named misuse: _"Non-skippable / connected TV run for direct response → impression-priced, forced view, completion carries no information → fix: reserve for awareness; use click-priced in-feed video for interaction."_

**Verdict: misfit for this objective.** Bumpers are fine as a _reach/awareness_ layer (top-of-funnel brand recall for the category), but they shouldn't be the vehicle for the demo-request conversion event. If video is wanted for the direct-response push, skippable in-stream or short vertical video is the structurally correct swap — same production input, right format.

## (c) Shift most budget to carousels — reject

- "Carousel delivers 30-50% lower cost per conversion" is the skill's other named discard: no reliable independent evidence supports it.
- Structural fit: carousel suits consideration, multi-product, warm audiences — and is named structurally wrong for cold first touch, since it "demands sequential swipe attention nobody cold gives." A B2B demo-request campaign is typically prospecting into cold or lukewarm audiences, which is the band carousel is worst at.
- Budget isn't your constraint, which removes the one thing that would make "lower cost per conversion" attractive even if the stat were real. Shifting _most_ budget into a format because of an unverified cost claim, when spend isn't the bottleneck, has no upside case left standing.

**Verdict: misfit as a majority-budget move.** Carousel can stay as a small consideration/retargeting slice for warm audiences who've already hit the landing page (case studies, feature walkthrough, proof points) — but not as the primary allocation, and not on this rationale.

## What to actually do instead

- Keep the landing page + embedded calendar as the demo-booking destination; it's already above the 5% threshold this skill uses as the decision line.
- Put creative and testing effort into the funnel stages that are actually under-examined here — hook/stop rate, hold rate, offer clarity — before touching format. The skill's own refresh priority is hook → visual → format → copy; nothing in this brief shows a format-specific failure signal (no placement-breakdown divergence, no frequency anomaly, no completion collapse cited), so there's no evidence the format is the problem at all.
- If the goal is more top-of-funnel reach, run it as its own awareness objective/budget line (bumper or short vertical video, not carousel), completely separate from the demo-conversion line — don't let a reach format optimize toward or get judged against the conversion event.
- Use carousel/document-style formats for warm retargeting of people who've visited the landing page but not booked, where sequential attention is earned rather than assumed.
