# Ranked: Copperleaf Q4 Video Openings (Best → Worst for Budget)

**1. Ad 3 — YouTube skippable: "Before you skip – what if your data warehouse bill dropped by half?"**
Directly defuses the one threat unique to this format (the 5-second skip button), then pairs it with a specific, quantified benefit. Curiosity gap + self-selection for cost-conscious buyers. This is the strongest hook of the five.

**2. Ad 1 — Meta feed: "If your dashboards take a week to update, listen up."**
Classic scroll-stopper: names a specific, painful symptom and addresses the viewer directly. Filters for the right audience in the first sentence — exactly what feed needs since there's no skip button to fight, just thumb velocity.

**3. Ad 2 — Meta feed: "Here's how we cut reporting time by 90 percent."**
Good quantified claim, but "we cut" is company-centric rather than viewer-centric — less of a pattern interrupt than Ad 1, reads more like a mid-funnel explainer opener than a scroll-stopper.

**4. Ad 5 — Bumper: "Copperleaf. Analytics your CFO trusts."**
Well-built for its job — name + one crisp positioning line fits 6 non-skippable seconds. But bumpers are a reach/frequency and recall tool, not a response driver, so this format inherently contributes less to a performance-focused Q4 push than 1–3. Rank reflects role, not script quality.

**5. Ad 4 — YouTube skippable: "Meet Copperleaf. We were founded in 2019 with a simple idea."**
Weakest by a clear margin: a founder-story/brand-intro opener in the one format engineered to lose the viewer in 5 seconds. No pain point, no benefit, no reason to stay — highest risk of paying for impressions that never finish playing.

## Budget takeaway
Put the largest share behind Ad 3, then Ad 1. Ad 2 is a safe number-two feed spend. Ad 5 plays a support role (don't judge it against the response-driven ads — it's doing a different job). Ad 4's opening needs a rewrite before it earns real spend; as written, it's the one most likely to waste impressions to the skip button.
