## Before anything else: confirm usage rights, not just booking rights

The expired creator contracts block a _new shoot_ — that's the 2-week/3-week wall you already know about. What's unconfirmed is whether the same contracts also gate _continued usage_ of the footage already in hand. Talent agreements typically license usage for a term ("6 months from first air date") separate from the booking engagement itself. If F's montage or G/H's lunchbox talent are paid creators (not the founder), and that usage window has also lapsed, running any of these ads creates legal exposure independent of how good the edit is.

**Action:** get legal to confirm, per asset, that the _usage license_ on existing footage is still active for a new flight — separately from the "re-signing takes 2 weeks" answer, which only covers new bookings. Do this today; it's the one thing that can kill all four options regardless of creative quality.

## E — Founder talking head

**Problem:** The entire pitch lives in audio, but ~85% of Meta feed views start muted. A static face with no text in frame 1 gives cold traffic nothing to read and no motion to interrupt the scroll — you're relying on sound _and_ asking someone to keep watching a still face before the payoff (the app auto-building a meal week) shows up later in the cut.

**Fix (editor-only, no reshoot):**

1. Burn in a bold value-prop headline on frame 1 — not a fade-in, present at the first frame — so the hook reads with sound off.
2. Caption the entire talking segment, not just the opening, so the whole pitch survives muted viewing.
3. Re-sequence the cut: open on 1–2 seconds of the app auto-building a week of meals (the strongest existing visual proof you have), _then_ cut to the founder with captions running under her. This turns an audio-dependent open into a visual-first hook without shooting anything new — it's a re-timeline of footage you already have.
4. Check text placement doesn't sit over her mouth/face for legibility once repositioned.

## F — Recipe montage, TikTok-positioned text

**Problem:** Text sits in the bottom safe zone for TikTok's edge-to-edge 9:16 player. Meta's feed UI (CTA button, profile info, reaction icons) covers a different — and often larger — bottom margin depending on placement (Feed vs. Reels vs. Stories). The montage itself is fine; the text is invisible where it matters.

**Fix:**

1. Reposition the text layer into Meta's safe zone (roughly the middle third, clear of the bottom ~20% and top ~10%) using the same footage — a graphics-layer re-edit, not a recut.
2. Export separate masters per placement (4:5/1:1 for Feed, 9:16 for Reels) instead of reusing one TikTok-first export across all of them — safe zones aren't uniform across placements.
3. Before launch, preview every export inside Ads Manager's placement preview tool specifically to confirm nothing is cropped by the CTA button or icons. This is the cheapest possible QA step and it's the exact failure mode you already found once.

## G vs. H — Lunchbox-packing, same footage, different copy

**Problem:** Not a technical failure like E/F — both are legible and safely positioned. This is a copy-strength gap. "The smarter way to eat healthy" (G) is a generic category claim any wellness app could run; it doesn't reference anything visible on screen, so it does nothing to speed up comprehension. "Meal prep without the Sunday marathon" (H) names a specific, visually-congruent pain point — the lunchbox-packing shot _is_ the Sunday marathon — which reads faster and creates recognition instead of a generic claim.

**Fix:**

1. Lead with H — specificity and image-text congruence consistently outperform generic claims in cold-traffic hook rate.
2. Since this costs nothing extra (same footage, swap-only text layer), don't pick blind — launch G and H as two ads in the same ad set so the first days of delivery data settle it empirically rather than on your judgment call, and shift budget toward whichever wins. This is the one candidate pair where you can hedge without spending extra editor time or delaying launch.
