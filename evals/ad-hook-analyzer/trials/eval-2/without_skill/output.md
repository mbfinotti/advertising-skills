## Does the data support the "TikTok-style" rebuild? No — not as presented.

**Verdict: ❌ Don't approve the rebuild budget on this evidence.** The 31% vs 22% comparison and the "industry standard 30%" benchmark are both apples-to-oranges. That doesn't mean TikTok-style creative wouldn't work on Meta — it might — but the agency hasn't shown that, and a full-budget rebuild is the wrong way to find out.

---

### 1. "Hook rate" isn't the same metric on both platforms

There's no universal definition of hook rate — it's always *(some early video-view threshold) / (some denominator)*, and both halves differ by platform:

- **Measurement window**: TikTok commonly benchmarks on a 2-second or 6-second view; Meta hook rate is typically built from 3-second video plays or ThruPlay. A 2-3 second gap is not trivial when most drop-off happens in the first second.
- **Denominator**: often impressions on one platform, video plays on the other. Same raw view count, different rate, depending on which tool/agency computed it.
- **Structural context, not just creative**: TikTok's feed is one full-screen video at a time, sound-on by default, swipe-to-continue — every impression is a forced binary decision (watch or skip). Meta's feed — even "feed placement" specifically — interleaves photos, carousels, and text posts, autoplays muted, and competes with a scrollable list rather than a single full-screen moment. Users can scroll past a Meta video ad without ever making the "watch/skip" decision TikTok forces on every viewer.

**Consequence**: a chunk (plausibly most) of the 9-point gap is explained by platform mechanics, not creative style. Rebuilding creative to close a gap caused by feed architecture will under-deliver relative to what the "close the gap to 31%" framing implies.

**Ask the agency**: exact formula (numerator threshold + denominator) used for each platform's hook rate, and whether it's the platform's native metric or an in-house agency calculation. If they can't produce this cleanly, the comparison should be discarded, not acted on.

### 2. The "industry standard 30%" is an unsourced number on a slide

Before this benchmark does any work in a budget decision, it needs:
- **Source and methodology** — which platform(s), which ad format, which measurement window.
- **Vertical relevance** — pet supplements / supplements DTC benchmarks, not a generic cross-industry average (these vary a lot; food/pet/CPG creative behaves differently from e.g. mobile games or fashion, which dominate a lot of published TikTok benchmark decks).
- **Recency** — hook-rate benchmarks move fast as platforms and ad loads change; a number from an old deck may already be stale.

A benchmark slide with no citation is not evidence — it's a prop for an upsell narrative ("your Meta is underperforming, let us rebuild it"). Ask for the primary source before treating it as a target.

### 3. Even a *real* gap wouldn't prove causation

Suppose the platform-adjusted gap is still meaningfully real. That still only tells you TikTok-native creative hooks better *on TikTok*. It doesn't establish that:
- The same creative style transfers to Meta's feed context (native TikTok pacing/format can read as low-production or out-of-place in a Meta feed and actually hurt trust/CTR for a supplement brand — this is a live risk for cold B2C traffic making a first impression).
- Hook rate is even the metric that matters for Fernwood's goals. It's a leading/vanity metric. What matters is whether a higher hook rate on Meta actually moves CTR, add-to-cart, CPA, or ROAS. Plenty of high-hook-rate creative bleeds attention without converting.

### 4. What I'd do instead of approving a full rebuild

1. **Get the agency to answer #1 and #2 in writing** before anything else — metric definitions and benchmark source. This alone may kill the "gap" narrative.
2. **Run a contained A/B test**, not a rebuild: 3-5 TikTok-style creatives vs. your current control, same budget tier, Meta feed placement only, held for statistical significance (watch spend/impression thresholds, not just calendar time).
3. **Track hook rate *and* downstream metrics** (CTR, ATC, CPA, ROAS) for both groups — don't approve based on hook rate alone.
4. **Only scale spend into a full rebuild if the downstream numbers win**, not just the hook rate.

### Bottom line
The quarterly report is comparing two different metrics wearing the same name, against an unsourced target, to justify a budget ask. Push back on the source data first. If the agency can produce clean, comparable methodology and the gap survives, test it on a slice of budget — then scale. Don't fund a full creative rebuild off a cross-platform metric mismatch.
