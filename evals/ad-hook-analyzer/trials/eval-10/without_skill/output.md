**Why I won't just pick a winner:**

Nobody can predict hook rate from copy alone with any real confidence. It depends on your audience, your footage's actual visual quality, the platform's mood that week, and interaction effects between the pour footage and the text overlay that don't show up until real cold traffic hits it. Anyone who tells you "Ad B will hook at 24%" without data is guessing with false precision — that's more dangerous than admitting uncertainty, because you'll anchor your whole budget on it.

What I *can* tell you directionally: "Finally, bone broth you'll crave" leads with an emotional payoff (craving) and works better as a scroll-stopper — curiosity/desire copy tends to outperform on hook rate. "Actually tastes good. $3 a serving" leads with reassurance + price, which is stronger mid-funnel (hold rate, CTR to a product page, purchase intent) but weaker as a pure thumb-stopper because it reads like a claim you have to already care about broth to notice. So if you're optimizing hook rate specifically, my directional lean is B ("Finally, bone broth..."). But "directional lean" is not the same as "will win" — treat this as maybe a 55/45 read, not a prediction.

**The actual problem with your plan:**

Putting 100% of $1,800 behind one untested creative on a guess (mine or yours) throws away the one thing a small budget is good for: a cheap real answer. You don't need to "afford" two full campaigns — you need to afford a few days of $1,800 split as a test.

**What I'd do instead:**

1. Run both, split roughly 50/50 (~$900 each), same audience, same placement, launched simultaneously — not sequentially, so you're not comparing different days/costs.
2. Let it run to statistical minimum, not gut feel: aim for at least ~1,000 impressions per ad (ideally more) before touching anything. At $1,800 cold-traffic CPMs, that's realistically 2-4 days depending on your niche.
3. Judge on hook rate (3-sec video views ÷ impressions) first, then don't stop there — check hold rate (ThruPlay or 15-sec) and cost-per-click too. A high hook rate with a low hold rate just means people bounce off fast, which is worse than a moderate hook that holds.
4. Once one ad shows a clear, consistent lead (not a single-day blip), kill the loser and reallocate whatever budget is left to the winner.

This costs you nothing extra — same $1,800, same footage, same launch day — and it replaces "my guess" with "your actual audience's behavior," which is the only signal that should be steering a $1,800 bet on a brand-new product.
