## What actually needs to change — and at what cost

The "drop test" _concept_ is not the problem — a durability stunt is a legitimate, specific, checkable demonstration (exactly what the Specificity dimension rewards: "this survives a 30-foot drop" is filmable and checkable). The problem is purely **which object is in frame and when**. That means the fix lives at a different rung than your CMO is reaching for:

- **Rung 1 (text rewrite) — will not work here.** No line fixes a 12-second product delay or 12 seconds of unrelated visual. Don't spend the 10 drafted lines on this footage; they're solving the wrong layer.
- **Rung 3 (re-cut from footage already shot) — check first, costs an hour.** Look for any frame in the existing footage where the case is visible in the first 3 seconds (being carried up, staged next to the watermelon, held before the drop). If one exists, a re-cut opening there could clear gates 2 and 3 without a reshoot.
- **Rung 4 (shoot a new opening beat) — the likely real fix.** Keep the drop-test mechanism, put the suitcase itself in frame from frame one: drop the case instead of (or alongside) the watermelon, or show the case surviving the same fall a fragile object just failed. This preserves the "stop the scroll" mechanism that earned the 41% while actually making it a luggage ad.
- **Rung 5 (rebuild the concept) — not warranted.** Don't throw out the stunt idea itself; the mechanism scores well, only the execution buries the product.
