**The CMO's inference doesn't hold up.**

"Best hook rate → make 10 more like it" treats hook rate as a proxy for ad quality. It isn't — it's a proxy for *scroll-stopping power*, full stop. A watermelon exploding off a parking garage stops thumbs because it's violent, novel, and satisfying in slow motion. None of that has anything to do with wanting a $265 carry-on. The 0.8 ROAS and flat sales aren't noise sitting next to a great number — they're the other half of the same story: you've built a very good watermelon ad that happens to have a suitcase in it.

Two structural reasons this particular ad can pull a 41% hook and still not sell:

1. **The hook and the product are decoupled by 12 seconds.** Whatever emotional payoff the viewer gets (the splat) is fully resolved *before* the suitcase shows up. There's no bridge — nothing in the first 12 seconds primes "durable," "travel," or "protects what's inside." The viewer's satisfaction closes the loop on watermelon, not on luggage.
2. **Hook rate likely selects the wrong audience.** People who watch past second 3 for a fruit-splatter payoff are disproportionately entertainment-seekers, not carry-on shoppers. You may be optimizing your top-of-funnel for exactly the segment least likely to buy.

**Why this matters for the "10 new first lines" plan specifically:** if the footage and the 12-second delay stay fixed, a new first line can move the hook rate (maybe), but it can't fix the decoupling — the product still doesn't show up until second 12, and the payoff viewers came for is still unrelated to luggage. So all 10 variants are likely to inherit the same pattern: great hook, weak ROAS. Producing 10 more of the same stunt is a bet that the *line* was the broken part. The data (hook great, ROAS bad, sales flat) points at the *structure* being the broken part instead.

**What I'd actually recommend:** treat this less as "rank 10 lines" and more as a test. Before green-lighting a full batch, ship 2-3 of the strongest lines *plus* one structurally different variant — same stunt, but pull the suitcase into frame in the first 2-3 seconds (e.g., watermelon packed *inside* the case before the drop, or the line makes an explicit luggage/impact connection) — so you can tell whether the fix is copy or structure before spending budget on all 10.
