## On the two specific pushes from your agency

**"More urgent, limited-time demo offer to drive immediate clicks"** — reject this for the batch as scripted. Under the 95:5 rule, roughly 95% of your B2B buyers are out-of-market on any given day; a cold LinkedIn video's job is to be *remembered* by a future buyer, not to convert a click today. Z's countdown timer imports a B2C direct-response device into that context, and the specific claim it makes — a decision "this week" — contradicts the 9‑month cycle you told me is real. That's not a copy problem a rewrite fixes; the funnel literally cannot deliver what the hook promises, which is the exact "43% hook rate, mediocre ROAS" failure mode this method exists to catch: the ad gets attention, nothing downstream converts, and now you've also trained prospects to expect a discount on a $40k considered purchase.

**"Hold the logo to the end card so it doesn't look like an ad"** — partially right, partially costly. Opening on a logo card is a real anti-pattern (it announces "ad" at the exact moment you're being judged), so don't do that. But *withholding all brand cues* until the end costs brand linkage — TikTok reports a 17-point drop when branding is left to the close — and that's the wrong trade for a memory objective aimed at buyers who won't act for months. X already threads this correctly: brand colour frames the shot in frame one with no logo card, so it reads as native content while still building the distinctive-asset recognition your 9-month buyer needs to recall later. Ask the agency to build Y's and Z's revisions the same way X already is, not to strip X's branding to match Y and Z.

## On the 40k organic views for Y

That number is evidence of reach, not qualification — and reach without qualification is precisely what got flagged as a gate failure above. Barry Hott's line applies directly here: "Clickbait can get tons of clicks without generating any sales." A stock clip of a lawyer ripping paper with an undefined "mistake" is built to travel broadly, which is exactly why it doesn't name your buyer. Don't let that data point buy Y a pass on the qualification gate; if anything, use the 40k as evidence the concept skews toward universal drama, not toward the ops/legal/procurement audience managing 200+ contracts.
