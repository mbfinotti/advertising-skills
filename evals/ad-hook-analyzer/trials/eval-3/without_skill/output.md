## Recommendation: Run X. Reject Y and Z as primary creative — and push back on both agency asks.

**Why X wins**

- "Managing 200+ contracts in spreadsheets?" is a self-qualifying hook — it filters for your actual ICP (someone with the specific pain Ledgerline solves) instead of maximizing raw attention.
- The screen recording shows the real product. For a $40k, 9-month, multi-stakeholder purchase, credibility beats spectacle — buyers (ops, legal, finance) will eventually compare the ad to the demo; X doesn't create a gap to close later.
- Brand colours in frame from second one starts building a category entry point ("spreadsheet contract pain → Ledgerline") for the ~95% of viewers not currently in-market. That's the mechanism that pays off "pipeline over the next few quarters" — mental availability now, recall when they enter the buying window.

**Why not Y, despite the 40k views**

- Organic views are a virality metric, driven by drama and shareability. Pipeline is driven by relevance to a buying trigger. These are different mechanisms — 40k views tells you the footage is watchable, not that it attracts contract-management buyers.
- "Lawyer ripping papers" / "mistake costing millions" is generic risk-fear, untethered to the spreadsheet/tracking pain your product actually fixes. It'll pull in a broad, low-fit audience — cheap CPMs, weak downstream MQL-to-pipeline conversion.
- Stock footage reads as stock footage to a sophisticated B2B audience (legal/procurement-adjacent buyers especially). It works against the "doesn't look like an ad" goal your agency claims to want from the logo placement.
- If you want reach/awareness as a secondary objective, test Y as a small side variant — but rewrite the line to name the actual trigger (e.g., a spreadsheet-version-conflict scenario), not generic catastrophe.

**Why not Z, and why to say no to your agency's urgency instinct**

- "Book a demo this week — 20% off" assumes an in-market, single-decision-maker buyer making a fast call. You have a 9-month cycle with a buying committee and procurement. Nobody accelerates a $40k contract-tooling decision because of a LinkedIn countdown timer.
- Manufactured urgency on a considered enterprise purchase reads as low-quality/desperate, especially to buyers evaluating a _contract management_ tool — they are professionally trained to spot fake terms and pressure tactics. That's a credibility own-goal specific to this category.
- A public blanket discount also creates downstream friction: sales now has to honor, explain, or walk back a term they didn't set.
- Kill Z outright. Don't test it.

**On the agency's two structural asks**

1. _"Make the opening more urgent with limited-time offers"_ — wrong tool for this funnel stage. Urgency/discounting is a short-cycle, low-consideration tactic; you're running long-cycle pipeline generation. Optimize the opening for relevance (does this person have the pain?), not for panic-clicking.
2. _"Hold the logo to the end card so it doesn't look like an ad"_ — this trades short-term CTR for weaker brand encoding, and brand encoding is exactly what a multi-quarter pipeline objective depends on. Distinctive brand assets (colour, wordmark, product UI) need early, repeated exposure to be learned — burying them until the end actively undercuts your stated goal. Keep the brand colour framing in the first 3 seconds like X already does; you don't need a full logo lockup immediately, but don't hide it either.

**Test plan**

- Primary: X, run at full budget as the pipeline-driving control.
- Optional secondary: a reworked Y (same fast-cut energy, but the line and footage tied to an actual contract-management failure mode, not generic "millions") as a reach/awareness test, small budget, tracked separately — don't let its view count influence budget allocation to X.
- Do not run Z. If the agency wants a limited-time offer, that belongs in mid-funnel retargeting/nurture once someone has already engaged, not in the cold opening hook.
