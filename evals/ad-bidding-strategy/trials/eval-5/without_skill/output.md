**Short answer: neither is fully right. Cutting brand entirely because of the eBay study is a misread of that study; the agency's "brand always pays" is also dogma. The right move is to test it on Brightquay's own data before touching budget either way.**

**Why the eBay study doesn't transfer**

The eBay research (Blake/Nosko/Tadelis, 2013) found near-zero incremental value from brand ads — but that's because eBay is one of the most universally recognized brands on earth with near-total organic dominance for its own name. Someone searching "ebay" is going to eBay regardless of whether an ad is there. That's an artifact of eBay's brand strength, not a general law of paid search.

Brightquay is a much smaller, category-specific marketplace. That changes the calculus:

- **Competitor conquesting risk**: in travel/marketplace verticals, competitors and OTA-style aggregators routinely bid on rivals' brand terms. If you're not #1 on your own name, someone else's ad shows above your organic listing.
- **SERP real estate**: brand ads bring sitelinks, extensions, and push down anything else trying to occupy that space (comparison sites, negative reviews, resellers).
- **Unproven organic dominance**: you don't actually know your organic click-through rate on brand queries without paid ads present — that's an empirical question, not an assumption to import from eBay.

**Why the 5.8x retargeting ROAS is also not the slam-dunk it looks like**

Retargeting ROAS is structurally inflated in last-click attribution — you're measuring people who already visited the site, viewed a listing, or abandoned a booking. Much of that "conversion" would have happened anyway through direct/organic return visits; the ad just claimed credit. Doubling that budget doesn't mean you get 2x the results at the same 5.8x — as you scale spend, you either:

- exhaust the warm pool (people who've actually shown intent) and start buying impressions on colder audiences at declining efficiency, or
- increase frequency on the same small pool with diminishing marginal return and rising annoyance/opt-out risk.

**What I'd actually tell the CEO**

1. **Don't cut brand blind.** Run a brand incrementality test first: pause brand bids in a subset of markets/geos (or on alternating days) for 2–4 weeks, and measure the change in organic clicks/bookings for brand terms vs. paid loss. This is exactly the eBay methodology, just applied to Brightquay's actual brand strength instead of assumed from a 12-year-old case study on a much bigger company.
2. **Check Auction Insights now** — it's a five-minute gut check ahead of the test. Look at impression share and overlap rate on the brand campaign; if competitors are already showing up when you search "Brightquay," that alone answers a lot before you even run the holdout.
3. **Don't blind-double retargeting either.** Scale it in steps (e.g. +25–30% at a time), watching marginal ROAS and frequency per user as spend increases. If ROAS holds as you scale, keep going; if it decays sharply, you've found the ceiling of the warm audience.
4. **Reframe the conversation with the CEO**: the eBay finding is real but brand-specific — it says "test whether your brand ads are incremental," not "brand ads are always waste." Propose the geo-holdout test as the way to get a Brightquay-specific answer in a few weeks, rather than making an irreversible call off someone else's data.

That gives him a concrete, time-boxed way to get what he actually wants (proof, not a guess) without walking away from a channel that may be protecting real revenue.
