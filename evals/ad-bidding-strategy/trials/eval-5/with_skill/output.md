## Who's right

Neither the CEO nor the agency. Both are stating a universal rule where only a measurement exists.

- **eBay's finding is real but it's an upper bound, not a universal law.** The skill's incrementality section is explicit: eBay's experiment found near-total organic substitution for brand-keyword clicks, but the Edmunds.com replication found the opposite, under 50% substitution, with paid brand traffic economically meaningful. eBay's result reflects eBay's exceptional brand strength, not a general property of brand search. The instruction from the published research is "test, don't assume," never "cut brand because eBay did."
- **"Brand always pays" is the same error pointed the other way.** The agency isn't citing an incrementality test on Brightquay's account either. It's a convention. A holdout or geo test is the only thing that actually tells you if brand clicks are incremental on *your* traffic, not eBay's or a generic advertiser's.
- **The 5.8x ROAS on retargeting doesn't automatically justify doubling it.** That number is an average return. The rule to apply before scaling any channel is: scale on marginal return, not average. A channel can hold a strong average return while the marginal return on the next dollar has already fallen below break-even, because you've saturated the pool of users who were already going to convert. Retargeting is structurally the highest-risk channel for exactly this failure. It's showing ads to people who already visited Brightquay, often people who were already going to book. High ROAS there is consistent with genuine incremental lift and consistent with mostly re-charging people who'd have converted anyway. In-platform ROAS can't distinguish the two cases. This is flagged directly as a failure mode: platform metrics healthy, holdout test shows near-zero lift. The fix is the same as for brand: test before you scale.

## What to tell the CEO

"We don't have the evidence to do either move yet, in either direction. eBay's own study is real, but a different company (Edmunds) ran the same test and got the opposite result, so brand-search incrementality is company-specific, not a fixed rule. We haven't tested Brightquay's brand traffic, so we don't know which side we're on. Same problem in reverse for retargeting: 5.8x is the best average in the account, but average ROAS doesn't tell us what the marginal dollar buys. It's very plausible some of that spend is just re-showing ads to people who already booked. Before we cut brand to zero or double retargeting, we run a two-week holdout test on both: a geo or audience holdout that turns off brand in one slice of the market and turns off (or caps) retargeting in another, and compares booking volume against the untouched control. That tells us the real incremental value of each, on our own data, not someone else's case study. Then we move budget based on what the test says, not on a blog post about eBay."

## The test to run this week

- **Brand search holdout.** Pick comparable geos or a % of search-impression share to pause brand campaigns in, keep organic and everything else untouched, compare booking conversions in held-out vs. control regions over the campaign's full evaluation window (one to two conversion cycles for Brightquay's booking lag, not one week). If bookings barely move in the held-out group, the CEO is right and organic really is absorbing the demand. If bookings drop meaningfully, the agency is right and the eBay comparison doesn't hold here.
- **Retargeting holdout.** Same design: cap or pause retargeting for a held-out audience slice, measure incremental bookings against a matched control. Don't jump straight to 2x spend; if the holdout shows real lift, scale in the ~20% budget-step increments the change-discipline default calls for, watching marginal return at each step, not one blind doubling. A pool this concentrated (retargeting is inherently a small, already-warm audience) hits diminishing marginal returns fast, so doubling could just buy more frequency on the same converters rather than more bookings.
- **Don't do both changes at once, and don't do them as a hard cutover.** Brand-off-this-week plus retargeting-double-this-week at the same time confounds any read you'd get from either test, and a full cutover on brand also eats a relearning period on top of the real effect you're trying to measure. Use a platform experiment/split-test tool for each if Brightquay's platforms offer one; it isolates the effect instead of bundling it with a learning reset.

## The one-line decision rule for the CEO

Don't set policy off someone else's incrementality test, and don't set it off average ROAS. Run Brightquay's own holdout on both brand and retargeting, then move budget on what the test shows, in steps, not as a one-time zero-or-double move.
