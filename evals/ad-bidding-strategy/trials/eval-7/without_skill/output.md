## The math you need

**Target ROAS = 1 / (Contribution margin − Target net profit margin)**

Because: Net Profit Margin = CM − (Ad Spend / Revenue) = CM − 1/ROAS. Solve for ROAS.

## Why "4x, industry standard" is wrong for you

Industry-standard ROAS benchmarks are built around industry-standard margins. Yours aren't standard — you have two lines 33 points apart (55% vs 22%). A blanket 4x ignores that.

## What 2.6x is actually doing today

Net margin at 2.6x blended = 40% − (1/2.6) = **1.5% net profit**. You're barely above the breakeven ROAS for your blended margin (breakeven = 1/0.40 = 2.5x). The founder's 10% target is nowhere close to being met.

## What blended ROAS actually hits 10% net

ROAS = 1 / (0.40 − 0.10) = **3.33x blended** — not 2.6x (too low), not 4x (overshoots).

At the agency's proposed 4x: net margin = 40% − 25% = **15% net**, blended. That clears the founder's bar, but it does so by cutting spend/volume further than necessary — profitable growth gets left on the table if 10% is the actual target, not "maximize margin %."

## The real problem: one target can't serve two margin structures

Run the same formula per line at a 10% net profit bar:

| Line    | Contribution margin | Target ROAS for 10% net |
| ------- | ------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Premium | 55%                 | **2.2x**                |
| Basics  | 22%                 | **8.3x**                |
| Blended | 40%                 | **3.3x**                |

Now check what the agency's flat 4x actually does to each line:

- **Premium at 4x ROAS** → net margin = 55% − 25% = **30% net**. You're way over-constraining premium — capping it at 4x throttles volume on your most profitable line for no reason.
- **Basics at 4x ROAS** → net margin = 22% − 25% = **−3% net**. Basics _loses money_ at the exact target the agency wants to apply account-wide.

A single 4x target simultaneously starves your best line and bankrolls losses on your worst one. That's the core flaw — not the number 4x itself, but applying one number to two businesses with different economics.

## Recommendation

1. **Reject the single account-wide target.** Set it by product line, not by account.
2. **Premium campaigns: floor at ~2.2x ROAS**, scale spend as far as volume allows above that — this line can absorb far more budget profitably than a 4x cap permits.
3. **Basics campaigns: 8.3x ROAS is not a realistic paid-acquisition target** on a 22%-margin line — few last-click channels sustain that. Options: cap basics prospecting spend hard, treat basics as a bundle-attach/upsell/retention play rather than a paid-acquisition-led line, or accept a lower NPM target specifically for basics (e.g., breakeven-plus at ~4.5x) if its role is customer acquisition/LTV, not first-purchase profit.
4. **Set 3.33x as the blended reporting gate**, not the operating target — it's the number that proves you're hitting 10% net in aggregate, but it should emerge from the two line-level targets above, not be dialed in directly.
5. **This is technically doable now.** Since purchase values already flow back server-side, tell the agency to run value-based/target-ROAS bidding split by product-line campaign or product set — not one account-wide tROAS. That's the only way the platform's bidding actually respects your margin structure instead of averaging it away.
6. **Before finalizing numbers**, get the agency to break out the current 2.6x by product line. You don't yet know whether the shortfall is a premium underperformance problem or a basics overspend problem — the fix is different in each case.
