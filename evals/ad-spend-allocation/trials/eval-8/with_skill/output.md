## The equimarginal call

Google's headroom is the only line here with an observable capacity constraint behind it. Rank order for the next dollar: **Google non-brand > CTV (as a bounded test, not a scaled bet) > Meta Advantage+ (hold).**

That means:
- **Fund Google non-brand a bounded step — 15–20% of its current spend, not the full +30% Google asked for.** Size follows the skill's default increment, not the platform's number.
- **Meta Advantage+ stays flat this cycle.** No marginal evidence, no move. Re-open it once you can penetration-check Advantage+'s underlying reach or get a holdout read on it — right now you'd be scaling on the platform's word alone, which is the exact failure mode ("best performer, so double it") this method exists to block.
- **CTV gets a bounded experiment slice, sized to its funding floor, not a full allocation** — regardless of how good its projected ROAS looks, because that number isn't validated yet. Give it a hypothesis, a decision date, and a stop condition, same as any other bounded test.

## On CTV specifically

Before treating any CTV number as real:
- Get the **projected ROAS figure itself** — it wasn't in your message, and "beats 3.4x" isn't a usable bar per above.
- Ask whether it's platform-modeled or from an independent measurement (matched-market, brand lift). Platform-modeled CTV attribution is closer to Google's brand-search problem than to a real read.
- Fund it at test size first (bounded, floor-level), read it against Meta's *marginal* CPA/ROAS on a comparable window, not 3.4x blended.

Skip that move for now, not because Meta isn't a good channel, but because "proven" here means historically efficient in aggregate — it doesn't tell you what the *next* $30K into it returns, which is the only question a budget increase should answer.
