## The situation, reframed

You don't have three separate funding decisions. You have one: a fixed $110K, two platforms both claiming they're under-scaled, and a temptation to solve the conflict by writing a bigger check instead of resolving it. Don't fund both recommendations and don't skip to $140K. Do the allocation work first.

## Don't take either recommendation at face value

Both "Limited by budget" and the Advantage+ scale suggestion are the platform's own optimizer telling you to feed it more money. That's not neutral advice — it's each algorithm reporting how much it *could* spend, not how much incremental profit the next dollar produces.

- **Google's "Limited by budget" + 30%** is a real signal of headroom, but it's projected off average auction performance, not marginal ROAS. The 31st percentile dollar is worth less than the 1st.
- **Meta's Advantage+ advisor** recommending +25% is the same pattern, amplified — Advantage+ pools budget across broad AI-selected audiences, so its "scale" recommendation is especially prone to reflecting spend appetite rather than true incremental efficiency once you push past current saturation.

Neither number tells you what you actually need: **marginal ROAS at the proposed increment**, not average/blended ROAS at current spend.

## Why "just raise to $140K" is the wrong move

That's recency bias dressed up as pragmatism — "Meta is proven" describes the *last* dollar, not the *next* one. Two things wrong with skipping the exercise:

1. It funds both platforms' self-interested asks simultaneously without ever checking which one actually has better marginal returns. You'd be guessing $15K to Google and $15K to Meta based on who asked, not who deserves it.
2. It treats the $30K as free money. It isn't — it's $30K/month, ~$360K/year, that could otherwise go to CTV testing, margin, or a smaller, evidence-based increase. A budget increase is a separate decision from reallocation and deserves its own payback math (target CAC, incremental margin per order), not "we trust this channel."

## The right approach: reallocate before you expand

You have a $110K pie with two channels both claiming hunger. Test that claim with money you already have, before asking for more:

- Shift a test tranche (~10% of total, $10–11K) from your lowest-marginal-return placement inside Meta (likely bottom-funnel retargeting propping up the blended 3.4x) into Google non-brand for 2–3 weeks. Compare marginal ROAS, not the headline numbers the platforms gave you.
- Only after that shows genuinely better marginal ROAS on Google should you consider trimming Meta's true underperforming segments — not the whole Advantage+ campaign — to fund it further.
- If Meta's marginal ROAS still leads at the margin, the +25% ask may be legitimate — but verify with a geo or conversion-lift holdout, not the in-platform report, since Advantage+ attribution is not independently verifiable.

If this reallocation test shows *both* channels genuinely have marginal ROAS above your payback hurdle even after shifting, that's your actual case for a budget increase — bring that data, not platform recommendations, if you go back to leadership for the extra $30K.

## Fix the CTV benchmark — your plan has a flaw

Comparing CTV's projected ROAS against Meta's **blended 3.4x** is the wrong bar, and it will unfairly kill CTV:

- Blended 3.4x is inflated by retargeting/branded conversions riding on demand CTV itself may be creating. You'd be asking CTV to beat a number that partly measures CTV's own future contribution.
- CTV is an upper-funnel, view-through, long-window channel. Judging it on a same-window last-click-style ROAS number, before you've even built a lift methodology, sets up a mismatched comparison — you're benchmarking apples against oranges and calling it a hurdle.

Better structure:
- Benchmark CTV against **Meta's marginal (incremental) ROAS**, not blended — the number you're about to derive from the reallocation test above.
- Run CTV as a small, dedicated test (5–8% of total budget, $5–9K/month, 60–90 days minimum — CTV needs frequency and time to show up in conversion data).
- Measure it with a **geo holdout or matched-market lift test**, not last-click ROAS. Pair that with a secondary read on branded search lift and direct traffic, since that's where CTV's effect usually shows up first.
- Greenlight based on incremental ROAS vs. your CAC/payback target, informed by the Meta marginal number — not a single ROAS threshold borrowed from a different funnel stage.

2. Run the Meta→Google reallocation test (10% tranche, 2–3 weeks) to get real marginal ROAS on both, instead of trusting either platform's ask.

4. Set CTV's bar as incremental ROAS vs. Meta's *marginal* ROAS (once you have it), evaluated via lift test — not blended 3.4x.
5. Revisit the $140K question only with reallocation + CTV test data in hand — that's the conversation where "Meta is proven" either holds up or doesn't.
