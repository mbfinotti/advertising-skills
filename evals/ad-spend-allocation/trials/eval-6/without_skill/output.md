### Why the four proven lines are held flat, not reallocated among themselves

You gave me two hard signals in this data — a CPA outlier on display, and an attribution gap on search. You gave me zero comparative signal on LinkedIn, review-site listings, or retargeting (no CPA, no CRM revenue). Reallocating dollars *between* those three off of vibes would be exactly the kind of unsupported move a RevOps function shouldn't make. So they stay at last quarter's spend, and I fund the two new lines entirely from money freed up elsewhere — see below.

### Why search stays flat instead of scaling to the $310K number

Google Ads is claiming $310K in attributed revenue; CRM-closed revenue attributable to search is $190K — only 61% of the platform's claim. That gap isn't noise, it's Google's self-reported attribution (probably last-click credit on demand that LinkedIn, review sites, or retargeting actually generated or assisted). On the CRM number, search is running at **2.7x ROAS ($190K / $70K)** — solid, not spectacular, and nowhere near the 4.4x the platform wants you to believe. That's good enough to keep funding at current levels, but it's a trap to scale search up on the inflated figure while starving the channels that may be doing uncredited work upstream of it. Flat spend, and I'd push for better multi-touch attribution (or at minimum, geo/holdout tests) before Q3 so this stops being a guess.

### Why display prospecting is zeroed out

$800/month, CPA running 3x every other line, with a full quarter of history to confirm it's not noise. That's a clean kill, not a "reduce and monitor." Its $2,400 goes straight into the new-line test budget below.

### The $23,000 test budget for the two new lines

$200K − $177K (the four flat lines) = $23,000. That number is coincidentally close to two other framings that back it up: it equals last quarter's $2,400 (freed from killing display) plus the $20,600 in genuinely new Q2 budget over Q1's $179,400 total spend. Either way you slice it, $23K is what's actually available to fund the new bets — I'm not cannibalizing the lines with evidence behind them to pay for the lines that don't have any yet.

**Split: 60/40 in favor of niche-community sponsorship ($14,000 / $9,000).**

For a B2B payments platform, a well-chosen niche-community sponsorship (a vertical Slack/Discord/newsletter where finance ops, controllers, or payments-adjacent operators already congregate) puts you in front of a self-selected, high-intent audience with a much shorter inferential leap to "this person could buy" than a podcast audience does. Podcast sponsorships buy broad trust-transfer and brand lift through a host relationship, but conversion is slower and harder to attribute, especially as a first-time test with no baseline. Niche-community gets the larger share because it's more likely to produce an early, legible signal (clicks, demo requests, even self-reported "heard about you from X") that tells you whether either line deserves more budget in Q3. Podcast still gets a real test slot, not a token one — $9K is enough to run a proper flight on one or two shows rather than spreading too thin to learn anything.

**One flag for Q3 planning:** neither new line has a CPA yet, and the search number above shows how much platform-reported metrics can mislead — insist on CRM-closed attribution (even rough, self-reported "how did you hear about us") for both podcast and niche-community from day one, not platform/publisher-reported numbers.
