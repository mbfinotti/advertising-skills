## Fact-check summary

| Claim | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| McKinsey: 70/20/10 → **2.7x shareholder returns** | 🔴 **Don't use as-is — looks fabricated/conflated** | McKinsey does have a well-known "70/20/10" framework, but it's about **innovation investment horizons** (70% core, 20% adjacent, 10% transformational business bets), not marketing/media budget allocation. I'm not aware of any McKinsey study tying a 70/20/10 *marketing* split to a specific "2.7x" TSR multiple. This has the shape of a stat that got stitched together — real source name + real-sounding number, wrong pairing. A CFO or a sharp board member who searches for this in 30 seconds will find nothing, which damages your credibility more than not citing it at all. |
| 60/40 brand-to-activation rule | 🟡 **Real, but overstated as "proven optimum"** | This traces to Les Binet & Peter Field's IPA effectiveness work (*The Long and the Short of It*), which found ~60/40 brand-building/activation as the average effectiveness-maximizing split. Caveats that matter for you: (1) it's derived mostly from **B2C/FMCG** IPA award case studies, not B2B; (2) Binet & Field's own later B2B research points to a **different balance** (activation-weighted, since B2B has longer cycles and smaller buying groups) — closer to 50/50 or more activation-heavy; (3) it's an average, not "the optimum" for any given company. Calling it "proven optimum" for a B2B data-integration platform is the weak point a CFO will poke at. |
| "20-30% of spend wasted on saturated channels" | 🔴 **Unsourced — treat as internal estimate, not an industry stat** | This is a commonly *repeated* figure in marketing decks, but I can't attach it to a specific, citable study. It reads like a folk statistic (in the spirit of the old "half my advertising is wasted, I just don't know which half" line). Presenting it as "the industry stat" without a named source is a real exposure risk in a board setting — expect "wasted according to whom?" |
| Rule of 7 | 🟡 **Real maxim, weak evidence base** | It's a genuine, widely-cited heuristic — but it originated in 1930s Hollywood movie marketing, not controlled research, and modern effectiveness researchers (Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, Binet & Field) generally treat "7" as folklore rather than an empirically derived optimum; effective frequency varies heavily by category, channel, and objective. Fine as a heuristic name-check; weak as "justification" for a specific budget number. |

**My recommendation:** drop the McKinsey 2.7x claim entirely (or replace it with something you can actually source), attribute the 20-30% waste figure to your own media audit instead of "industry," and soften "proven optimum" / rule-of-7-as-proof language to heuristic framing. ## Polished slide: "2026 Marketing Budget — Reallocating to 70/20/10"

**Headline:** Shifting from [current split] to a 70/20/10 model — core channels, emerging bets, experimental spend

**Talking points (CFO-ready):**

1. **The shift.** "We're moving 10% of the marketing budget into deliberate experimentation and 20% into scaling channels that are proven but not yet mature — following the same core/adjacent/transformational logic McKinsey uses for innovation portfolios, applied here to demand-gen spend."
   *(Note: frame this as "borrowing the 70/20/10 logic," not "McKinsey proved 70/20/10 marketing budgets deliver 2.7x returns" — that specific claim doesn't hold up and shouldn't be the anchor.)*

2. **Brand vs. activation balance.** "Within the 70% core allocation, we're targeting roughly a 60/40 brand-to-activation split, consistent with Binet & Field's long-run effectiveness research — adjusted toward activation given our B2B sales cycle, where their own data shows brand-heavy splits underperform for long-consideration, small-buying-group purchases."
   *(This version survives a follow-up question. "Proven optimum" doesn't.)*

3. **Where the waste is.** "Our own channel-level audit shows an estimated 20-30% of current spend sitting in saturated or diminishing-return placements — [cite your actual audit, e.g., 'per the Q3 media mix review'] — which is the direct funding source for the 10% experimental bucket. We're not asking for new budget; we're reallocating waste we can already point to."
   *(Ground this in your own data, not an uncited "industry" number — it's both more defensible and more persuasive to a CFO, who will trust your audit over a floating stat.)*

4. **Frequency budget.** "Our frequency targets are built around effective-reach modeling for our funnel, informed by the 'rule of 7' heuristic as a floor, not a formula — we're validating actual effective frequency per channel via [attribution/MMM tool] rather than assuming a fixed number applies uniformly."
   *(Naming the rule of 7 as a sanity-check heuristic, not as the justification, is more defensible and actually stronger — it shows rigor instead of relying on a 90-year-old maxim.)*

**One-line close for the CFO:** "This isn't a bigger budget ask — it's a reallocation from measured waste into tested and experimental growth, with an initial 90-day gate on the 10% experimental tranche before further commitment."
