## Slide: Moving to a Disciplined 70/20/10 Budget Model

**Headline claim (revised):**
"We're adopting 70/20/10 as portfolio discipline — not a guaranteed-return formula."

**Talking points for the CFO:**

- **What 70/20/10 is:** 70% to channels proven to work now, 20% to the deliberate next bet, 10% to exploratory tests. This is the same resource-discipline logic Google popularized internally (Eric Schmidt, 2005) — a governance structure, not a marketing-science result. We're not claiming a return multiplier from it; we're claiming it stops budget drifting to "whatever we did last year."
- **Why now:** [insert Quintarra-specific reason — inherited/untrusted split, new measurement maturity, annual zero-based review, etc.] This is the honest trigger; state it plainly rather than borrowing external authority for it.
- **Brand vs. activation split:** Rather than anchoring to the oft-cited 60/40 (a B2C cross-category average from the IPA Databank, not a B2B number), we're targeting closer to the B2B-adjusted range — roughly 45–55% toward activation, tilting further toward activation the more sales-assisted our motion is. We'll state this as our chosen policy, not an industry-proven optimum.
- **On wasted spend:** Skip the 20-30%-waste stat — it has no traceable source and would not survive a follow-up question. If you want a spend-efficiency argument, use the sourced one instead: *integrated analytics and measurement discipline typically frees 15-20% of marketing spend without cutting the budget* (McKinsey, 400+ engagements, 8 years) — and frame it as the case for the measurement investment behind this reallocation, not for the 70/20/10 ratio itself.
- **On frequency budget:** Drop "rule of 7" — it's marketing folklore contradicted by the actual research (Krugman found ~3 exposures sufficient back in 1972). Justify the frequency budget instead from something real: our own funnel data (impressions-to-MQL by frequency band, if we have it), or explicitly label it a starting assumption we'll calibrate once we have account-level reach/frequency data — never present a number this weak as a research-backed input to a board.

**One line for the deck footer, if you want the CFO's confidence explicit:**
"Ratios above are operator policy, recorded with rationale — not claimed as proven optima. We'll report marginal return by line each quarter to validate or correct the split."
