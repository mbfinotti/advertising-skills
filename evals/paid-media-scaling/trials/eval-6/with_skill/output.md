## Short answer: no — kill the framing, keep the campaign, and don't do daily single-lever changes.

Your instinct to isolate variables is right. The two ways you're planning to do it will both backfire. Here's why, then what to run instead.

### Why "CPA doubled" is very likely noise, not a signal

This isn't a judgment call — the math says so:

- **Sample size.** 7 conversions is not a base you can read a 2x CPA move from. The skill I'm working from flags this exact pattern by name: *"a doubled CPA on 6 conversions with a 14-day conversion lag is noise"* (practitioner convention, unattributed — folklore, but it's describing your situation almost exactly, and yours is worse: 7 conversions, 21-day lag).
- **The lag makes today's number structurally invalid, not just noisy.** You raised spend on the 3rd. It's the 8th — 5-6 days in. Your click-to-booked-job cycle is ~3 weeks. That means the 7 conversions counted so far were mostly generated by *clicks that happened before the budget increase*, now being divided against the *new, higher daily spend*. Rough math: ~$8,000-9,600 spent since the 3rd, only ~$1,260 of it (7 × $180) attributed to a conversion. The other ~$7,000+ is spend still "in flight" — it hasn't had time to become a booked job yet and won't for another 2 weeks. You are not looking at the marginal CPA of the new budget. You're looking at an accounting artifact of the lag. This is the single most common scaling misread the skill tracks, and it's exactly why "judge a step on one good or bad week" is a named failure mode — here it's one good or bad *five days*, on a three-week cycle.
- **Killing today throws away the only data that would ever resolve this.** If you pause now, the clicks already bought under the new budget never get a chance to convert, and you'll never know if $1,600/day actually works. You'll re-launch blind and re-pay the learning-phase cost a second time.

**Reserve an instant kill for real emergencies** — runaway spend past a hard ceiling, a policy/legal problem, a broken landing page, or confirmed tracking corruption. Before assuming this is one of those, check (today, cheaply):

- Is anything actually broken — landing page, call tracking, conversion import — that would explain a real cost jump?
- Any seasonality (storm activity, competitor surge) pushing CPCs up account-wide right now, independent of your budget change?
- Are you running any other experiment/change this week that could be confounding the read?
- Is lead *quality* on the 7 conversions consistent with normal, or did volume dilute quality (junk leads, wrong locations)?

If those all come back clean, there is nothing here that justifies an emergency stop.

### Why "one fix per day" won't isolate anything either

Two problems, both documented platform behavior, not opinion:

1. **Each edit can itself reset learning/relearning** (Google: budget, target-CPA, and bid-strategy changes can trigger relearning that takes up to 3 weeks or 1-2 conversion cycles — documented). Stack three edits on three consecutive days and you get three overlapping resets, none of which exits before the next one lands.
2. **You can't read any of them before your conversion cycle closes.** Monday's location change won't show a real signal in booked jobs until ~3 weeks out — by which point Tuesday's headlines and Wednesday's bid target are already baked into the same data. You'd have three simultaneous, unresolved experiments, not three isolated ones. This is the inverse of isolating a variable.

### What to do instead

**Today (the 8th):** Do nothing to budget, targeting, headlines, or bid target. Run the checklist above. If it's clean, decide which fear is real — the CPA *number*, or cash burn at $1,600/day. If it's cash burn, step budget down partially (20-30% cut is the standard practitioner recipe — folklore, but a reasonable de-risk move) rather than to zero. If it's the CPA number alone, hold at $1,600 and let the cohort mature — that number is telling you nothing yet either way.

**Between now and the real read date:** Stop watching CPA-per-booked-job daily — it's a lagging metric and will stay noisy for weeks. Watch leading indicators instead: cost per lead/estimate booked, lead volume, and lead quality (are the calls/estimates from the higher-spend days as good as before). This is standard practice for any business with a multi-week conversion lag — judge on leading indicators, never on the lagging revenue metric, until the lag clears.

**Set the actual evaluation date now, in writing:** learning window + your 21-day lag ≈ 3-4 weeks from the 3rd → don't make a real go/no-go call on booked-job CPA before roughly **Oct 1-4**. Put it on the calendar so "it's been almost two weeks and still looks bad" doesn't trigger a panic move before the data is even valid.

**Real rollback trigger (pre-commit it now, not when you're anxious):** cost-per-qualified-lead (not cost per booked job) exceeding ~1.5x its pre-increase baseline, sustained across the full hold — not one bad day — or a confirmed lead-quality drop. If that trips: cut 20-30%, stabilize two weeks, resume smaller. That's the trigger that's actually diagnostic; the number you have today isn't.

**If you still want to test locations / headlines / bid target one at a time:** fine idea, wrong cadence. Given your lag, each single-variable test needs its own ~3-4 week hold to read cleanly on booked jobs (or a shorter read on leading indicators if you're willing to accept more noise). Pick the one lever you actually suspect moved the needle — my guess would be bid target, since that's the lever most directly tied to a budget-driven CPA shift — test that first, hold it out, then move to the next. Running all three in a week guarantees you learn nothing from any of them.
