# Media Scaling Sanity Check — Halberd Outfitters Prospecting Campaign

**Verdict: NO GO on the straight jump to $80K in September.**

## The number that changes the decision

Blended MER is the wrong metric to scale on. "Blended will always trail marginal — your marginal MER will become unprofitable before your blended MER" holds exactly here.

| Month  | Spend | Revenue | **Monthly MER (blended)** |
| ------ | ----- | ------- | ------------------------- |
| June   | $40K  | $122K   | 3.05                      |
| July   | $48K  | $140K   | 2.92                      |
| August | $56K  | $152K   | 2.71                      |

Blended MER — the number you're reading confidence from — degrades slowly and stays well above your 2.0 break-even all summer. That's real, but it's not the number that governs the next dollar.

| Step                                                        | Δ Spend | Δ Revenue | **Marginal MER on the increment** |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------- | --------- | --------------------------------- |
| June → July                                                 | +$8K    | +$18K     | 2.25                              |
| July → August                                               | +$8K    | +$12K     | **1.50**                          |
| Whole June → August band (for a larger, more stable sample) | +$16K   | +$30K     | **1.875**                         |

Both marginal readings on your two most recent expansions — the incremental $8K in July, and the wider $16K band across the whole summer — sit **below your owner-approved 2.0 break-even**. The last dollars you've already added to this campaign are, on this evidence, losing money, while the average of all dollars still looks fine. That gap is the whole mechanism the skill exists to catch: blended MER is arithmetically guaranteed to look better than the truth of what a _new_ dollar does, because it's diluted by the profitable dollars spent back when the campaign was smaller.

**Label:** this is your own account's data, so it's the strongest tier of evidence available (account history) — but it's still _attributed_, not causal (platform/blended revenue, no holdout). The eBay experimental result (Blake, Nosko & Tadelis 2015, _Econometrica_ — research) showed attributed measures can overstate true incremental return by an order of magnitude, especially as spend concentrates in warmer/branded pools. If anything, that means the true marginal picture could be _worse_ than 1.5–1.875, not better. There is no scenario in the data you've given where attribution bias would flip this to a "safe to scale" read.

## What actually explains the marginal collapse — check before concluding "this channel is dead"

The marginal MER dropping from 2.25 → 1.5 over two $8K steps could be any of:

- **Creative fatigue** — rising frequency, same ads riding since June.
- **Audience saturation** — prospecting pool for menswear DTC exhausting reach; check 30-day penetration.
- **Seasonality** — June–August includes summer slowdown patterns common in menswear; September could behave differently, better or worse.
- **A genuine profit-peak signal** — you may simply be past the point where this campaign profitably absorbs more dollars at current creative/audience supply, full stop (Common Thread Collective's profit-peak framing: stop when marginal contribution margin crosses $0 relative to your cost/margin structure, "even if blended numbers still look healthy" — which is precisely your situation).

You cannot tell these apart from three monthly totals. That's the actual next step, not the $80K spend.

## Recommended action for September

1. **Do not move to $80K.** That would also be a +43% single jump from $56K — on its own, independent of the marginal-MER problem, that's a magnitude no platform recommends and well past every practitioner default (15–20% per step, "never 30%+ in one move" — folklore, but consistently repeated across every operator school in the reference material, precisely because oversized jumps reset delivery learning at the same moment you're trying to read a signal cleanly).
2. **Hold spend at $56K** (or the smallest reversible step) for one more full cycle and pull weekly (not monthly) spend/revenue to get a real marginal read instead of two data points.
3. **Diagnose before spending more:** frequency trend, 30-day penetration, proven/non-fatigued ad count, and whether September's audience or seasonality differs from summer.
4. **Set the rollback you don't currently have**, even at current spend: e.g., "if a weekly marginal MER read stays under 2.0 for 2+ consecutive weeks, cut back to the last tier where marginal MER cleared break-even."
5. Only once marginal MER on the most recent band clears 2.0 with real margin (not 1.5–1.875) does a ramp toward $80K become a plan I can help size — at that point, this becomes a normal vertical-ladder exercise with proper steps and holds.
