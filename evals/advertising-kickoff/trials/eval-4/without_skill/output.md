# Hiring a Performance Marketing Manager (Pinefield, $70k/mo spend)

## Level to hire

**Mid-level Performance Marketing Manager (3–5 yrs experience), not a specialist and not a Head of Growth.**

- $70k/mo (~$840k/yr) sits in the range one strong generalist can own solo — no team needed yet.
- You said you run it "badly" — that's usually a fundamentals gap (tracking, testing discipline, incrementality), not a lack of a manager to direct someone. A junior needs direction you can't yet give; a Director-level hire is overkill and expensive for one person's workload.
- Screen for candidates who have **personally owned** $50–150k/mo budgets, not just executed inside a bigger team's strategy. Ask directly in screening: "whose decision was the monthly budget allocation — yours or your manager's?"
- Prefer a generalist who's touched Meta + Google + at least one more channel over a single-platform specialist. At this spend you need someone who can diagnose the account, not just optimize inside an existing structure.

## Interview process

Standard ads interviews reward buzzwords. Replace talking-point questions with real tests:

1. **Live account audit (take-home, paid for their time).** Export an anonymized snapshot of your actual Meta/Google account structure and give them 48 hours to identify the top 3 problems and what they'd fix first. This is your single best signal — it's literally the job, and you have real broken data to test against.
2. **Unit economics walkthrough (whiteboard).** Have them walk you through CAC, LTV, and payback period, and reason about what CAC is acceptable for Pinefield's sales cycle and AOV. Tests business reasoning, not platform trivia.
3. **Post-attribution stress test.** Ask: "iOS/consent changes broke clean last-click attribution — how do you make budget calls now?" You want to hear holdout tests, geo lift tests, or basic MMM thinking. If the answer is "trust platform-reported ROAS," that's a red flag at this level.
4. **Creative testing cadence.** Ask them to describe their actual testing framework — concepts/week, kill criteria, how they avoid iterating on vanity metrics.
5. **90-day plan exercise**, built from your real (anonymized) numbers. Doubles as your actual onboarding doc regardless of who you hire.
6. **Reference check, targeted question:** "Tell me about a campaign that failed under their ownership — what did they do?" You're listening for ownership vs. blaming the algorithm.

Red flag across all of these: fluent in platform jargon, vague on plain-language reasoning. That's the exact failure mode you're hiring to fix.

## Spend authority — first 90 days

Ramp it, don't hand it over day one — you also need to build trust that they won't blow the budget while you're not watching.

| Phase       | Weeks   | Authority                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ----------- | ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Audit       | 1–2     | No live changes. They audit tracking, account structure, and past performance; you approve their findings before anything ships.                                                                           |
| Test budget | 3–6     | Own ~~15–20% of spend (~~$10–14k/mo) for experiments. Core budget stays under your control. Any single change >$1–2k needs your sign-off.                                                                  |
| Ramp        | 7–12    | If test-budget KPIs hit agreed targets (CAC, at minimum no regression vs. your baseline), hand over majority ownership of the $70k/mo. New channel launches or >20% MoM budget swings still need sign-off. |
| Post-90     | ongoing | Full operational authority within a board/founder-set monthly ceiling. Monthly reporting, quarterly strategy review.                                                                                       |

Set a hard **daily spend ceiling per campaign** from day one regardless of phase — this is the actual failure mode that burns budgets overnight (a bad automated bid strategy running unattended), not strategic disagreement. Weekly 30-min syncs for the first month, biweekly after.

# For your cousin: organic social → paid ads

Her organic background is a real edge, not a blank slate — most paid social hires are weak precisely on creative, which is her strength. The gap is measurement and platform mechanics, not "marketing sense."

**Learning order:**

1. **Get hands-on immediately, not via courses first.** Open Meta Ads Manager with a real $5–10/day budget (her own content, a friend's small business, anything) and run actual campaigns. Passive learning doesn't build the instincts platforms need.
2. **Measurement basics.** Pixel/Conversions API setup, UTM tracking, and reading a dashboard past likes/reach: CAC, ROAS, CTR, CPM, funnel drop-off.
3. **Campaign mechanics.** Campaign structure, bid strategies, and — important in 2025/26 — broad targeting + creative-led testing, which is how Meta increasingly performs and plays directly to her strength.
4. **Creative testing discipline.** Turn her existing content instincts into a structured framework: hook variants, format tests, defined kill criteria instead of gut calls.
5. **Basic reporting numeracy.** Pivot tables, blended CAC — this is where a lot of creative-to-paid transitions stall when reporting to a stakeholder.
6. **Certifications (Meta Blueprint, Google Ads) are fine as study structure, not as a credential that matters to employers.** Real accounts managed matter far more.

One honest flag: don't put her in the external hiring pool for the Pinefield role you're recruiting above — hiring family into the exact role you're formally interviewing outsiders for gets messy fast. Better path: she freelances or manages a small budget for other businesses for 3–6 months to build real reps, then you two revisit whether there's a fit (e.g., owning organic-to-paid handoff as a defined sub-scope, not the core PMM seat).
