## New Structure — Broad, Tested Against Control

**Untouched (not part of the interest-vs-broad question):**
- Retargeting campaign — keep as-is.
- Past-buyers exclusion list — keep applied everywhere, control and test.

**Campaign A — Control (existing structure, unchanged)**
- 5 interest-stacked ad sets + 1 LAL, current budgets, current exclusion.
- Purpose: baseline to measure broad against. Do not touch for the test's duration.

**Campaign B — Broad Test (new)**
- 1 ad set, no interest/LAL targeting, broad audience, past-buyer exclusion applied.
- CBO, same creative currently running in Campaign A (don't introduce a creative variable at the same time as a targeting variable).
- Budget: start at 20–30% of current prospecting spend (~$19k–28k/month), not 100%. At current volume that's still ~150–230 purchases/month for the test — enough to read signal without betting the account.

**Test window:** minimum 2–3 weeks. You need the ad set past learning phase (~50 optimization events/week) plus enough calendar time to average out weekly variance.

**Decision rule after the window:**
- Broad CPA ≤ control blended CPA (or clearly trending there as it scales) → shift budget incrementally, e.g. 70/30 next cycle, then reassess. Don't jump to 100% in one step even if it wins.
- Broad CPA worse → hold or kill the test. You've only spent the test budget's learning cost, not the account's.

**One thing to clarify before Monday:** check whether the videos meant "remove interest fields from a regular campaign" or "Advantage+ Shopping Campaign." Those are different mechanisms — Advantage+ pools signal differently than a manually-built broad ad set, and it changes what you're actually testing.

**Creative note:** broad shifts audience-qualifying work from targeting onto creative — Meta leans on hook/format diversity to find your buyer instead of an interest signal doing it. Before scaling the broad test, make sure creative refresh cadence can support it; broad with stale creative variety typically stalls, not fails cleanly.
