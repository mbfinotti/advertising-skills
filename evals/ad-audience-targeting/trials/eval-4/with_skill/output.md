# Budget-Split Validation: Pellagrino Home — Do Not Approve As Proposed

**Verdict: Reject the 42% → 65% retargeting shift. The pitch is the textbook failure mode this decision area has a name for — "retargeting worship" — and the math the media buyer is leaning on (9.4x vs 1.8x) is the one comparison you're never supposed to use to justify this move.**

## Why "obviously right" is the trap, not the answer

Retargeting ROAS is structurally inflated by design, not by this buyer's skill. It's built from people who already visited the site, added to cart, or engaged with the brand — a meaningful share of them would have bought anyway, with or without the ad. Prospecting ROAS looks weak by comparison because it's doing the harder job: finding people who don't know the brand yet. Comparing the two head-to-head and funding the winner is exactly the move that inflates blended numbers while quietly starving the thing feeding the pool.

Retargeting is also explicitly not the tier you use to prove a plan is working, because its number isn't a clean read of incremental value — it's a mix of true incremental sales and sales that were coming regardless.

## The math the request doesn't show you

| | Current | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Retargeting | 42% ($25.2k) | 65% ($39k) |
| Prospecting | 58% ($34.8k) | 35% ($21k) |

Two thresholds this trips:

- **Retargeting above ~40% of spend is itself a warning signal, not a success signal.** Pellagrino is already past that line at 42%. The request doesn't fix a problem — it doubles down on one that should already have triggered a review.
- **Prospecting share should hold at ≥60% of spend** as a standing health metric, unless the brand is deliberately harvesting down a shrinking pool for a defined reason (e.g., winding down, cash-flow crunch). At 35%, prospecting is cut to roughly half that floor.

## What actually breaks if you approve this

1. **The retargeting pool is fed by prospecting traffic.** Cut prospecting by ~40% ($34.8k → $21k) and the pool that generates the 9.4x starts shrinking within weeks — a retargeting pool decays fast once the refill slows, so the buyer would be starving the input to the exact tier he's trying to scale.
2. **Retargeting pools are finite.** Bath goods is not a huge-ticket, long-consideration category — the pool of "people who recently engaged" is a fixed, relatively small universe. Pushing 65% of a $60k budget into it (up from $25.2k to $39k) mostly buys frequency against the same people, not incremental reach. Small/saturated audiences cost more per impression as you push spend into them — expect CPMs to climb and that 9.4x to compress toward blended reality, not hold.
3. **No incrementality read exists yet.** Nothing here says whether the 9.4x is platform/pixel-reported (likely, and known to overstate retargeting) or from an actual lift/holdout test. Without that, you can't tell how much of it is real.

## What's missing before anyone should decide (evidence gate)

Before approving any split change, get:

- **Is 9.4x pixel-attributed or incrementality-tested** (geo holdout, conversion-lift study)? If pixel-only, treat it as a ceiling estimate, not a true number.
- **Weekly conversion volume per tier** — needed to check whether either tier is even clearing the learning threshold at current spend, let alone at 65/35.
- **How long has retargeting sat at 42%, and is it already showing CPM/frequency creep** — a sign the pool is saturating at current spend, which would make scaling it further actively counterproductive.
- **Retargeting audience size today** vs. platform's minimum audience floor at $39k/month — if the pool can't absorb that spend, the extra dollars just inflate frequency and cost per result.

## Recommended counter-proposal

Don't approve a single 23-point jump — that's a learning-resetting-sized move on the tier funding the biggest share of the account, made on unverified evidence. Instead:

1. **Run an incrementality test on retargeting first** (platform conversion-lift tool or a simple holdout). This is the only honest way to know if 9.4x is real or mostly non-incremental.
2. **If retargeting still looks strong after that test**, move it up in small steps — cap any single change at roughly 20% relative move (e.g., 42% → ~47-50%), not 42% → 65% in one shot. Reassess each step before the next.
3. **Never let prospecting drop below ~55-60% of spend.** It's what trains the delivery algorithm and refills the exact pool retargeting depends on — cutting it to fund retargeting eats the account's own pipeline within weeks even if retargeting itself looks fine in isolation.
4. **Watch cost-per-impression and frequency in the retargeting pool** as you scale it any amount — rising CPMs there is the tell that you've hit the pool's ceiling, not a market to keep feeding.

**Bottom line for approval purposes:** approve a bounded test (incrementality check + a modest, reversible step up, prospecting held ≥55-60%), not the 65/35 split as pitched. The comparison used to justify it (9.4x vs 1.8x) is the one metric in this decision that's expected to mislead.
