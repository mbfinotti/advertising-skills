Don't approve this as written. The ROAS gap looks like an easy call, but comparing platform-reported retargeting ROAS to prospecting ROAS is comparing two different things, and acting on it at this scale will likely shrink your revenue and customer base even as the "blended ROAS" number goes up.

**Why 9.4x vs 1.8x is misleading, not decisive**

1. **Retargeting ROAS is inflated by selection bias.** Retargeting only fires on people who already visited the site, viewed a product, or abandoned a cart — i.e., people with high existing purchase intent. A meaningful share of those 9.4x "attributed" sales would have converted anyway via direct traffic, brand search, or email, with zero ad spend. Platform ROAS (last-click or view-through) takes credit for demand it didn't create. Prospecting, by definition, is reaching cold audiences — it's compared unfairly on the same metric.
2. **Prospecting ROAS looks weak because it's carrying the acquisition cost the whole funnel depends on.** 1.8x on cold traffic for a DTC bath goods brand isn't obviously bad — it's the number that determines whether you have any retargeting pool at all next month.
3. **The retargeting pool is fed by prospecting.** Cutting prospecting from 58% ($34,800/mo) to 35% ($21,000/mo) — a 40% cut — means far fewer new site visitors and cart events. Retargeting audiences decay in weeks. You will not get 65%-of-budget worth of qualified retargeting inventory; you'll hit frequency caps and audience exhaustion, and CPMs/CPA on the shrunken pool will rise. The 9.4x is very unlikely to hold at nearly 3x the spend against a shrinking, fatigued audience.
4. **Bath goods is a repeat-purchase, consumable category.** The value of prospecting isn't just this month's ROAS — it's the new-customer base that drives reorders (LTV), which retargeting can't create on its own.

**What I'd actually approve**

- **Get incremental ROAS before reallocating**, not platform ROAS. Either a geo/audience holdout test (hold out ~10-20% of retargeting-eligible users, no ads, compare conversion) or a short conversion-lift test. This is the only way to know if that 9.4x is real lift or attribution theft. Most retargeting incremental lift for a mature DTC brand lands well below platform-reported ROAS — often 2-4x true incrementality, sometimes lower.
- **If you want to move now, move partway, not to 65%.** Something like 42% → 48-50% is a real test of the hypothesis without starving the funnel. Full swing to 65% is not reversible in effect — by the time you see the pool exhaustion in the numbers (2-4 weeks of lag), you've already lost a chunk of new-customer acquisition for the month.
- **Set a prospecting spend floor** tied to new-to-file customers/month, not a fixed %, so retargeting can't structurally cannibalize its own supply.
- **Track leading indicators weekly**, not just blended ROAS: new site sessions, add-to-carts, retargeting audience size/reach, frequency, and new-customer count. If retargeting audience size or new-customer count starts dropping, that's the early signal the shift is backfiring before ROAS shows it.

**Bottom line for your media buyer:** the direction (retargeting is more efficient at the margin, prospecting could be tightened) may well be right, but 42%→65% in one move, justified by raw ROAS comparison, is the kind of decision that looks great in month 1 and shows up as a revenue and customer-growth problem in month 2-3. Ask him to run an incrementality read first, or at minimum counter with a smaller step (to ~48-50%) with a hard floor on prospecting spend and weekly tracking of new-customer volume before going further.
