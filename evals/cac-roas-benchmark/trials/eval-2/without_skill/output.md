## Where the calculation breaks

**You're attributing April spend to April closes, but your sales cycle is 4 months.** The 2 deals that closed in April were driven by leads generated back around December — not by the $60,000 you spent in April. April's $60,000 hasn't had time to produce a single close yet; those leads are still mid-pipeline. Dividing this month's spend by this month's closes when the cycle is 4x longer than a month is an apples-to-oranges comparison, not a CAC.

## What the number should actually look like

Use your own historical lead→close rate (1.5%) to project what April's cohort will eventually produce, since it hasn't finished its cycle:

- 500 leads × 1.5% ≈ **7.5 expected eventual closes** from April's spend
- Projected cohort CAC: $60,000 ÷ 7.5 ≈ **$8,000**, not $30,000

Check that against your own unit economics, which you don't need a third-party benchmark for:

- ACV $18,000 × 75% margin = $13,500 gross profit in year one alone
- $8,000 CAC recovered in ~7 months of gross profit, out of a 12-month contract — and that's before any renewal revenue on a SaaS contract
- That's a healthy payback, not a channel to shut down

Even the naive, too-harsh version — treating the $30,000 as real — is a red flag worth investigating, but "kill it entirely" is still the wrong reaction to a single month's cohort mismatch.
