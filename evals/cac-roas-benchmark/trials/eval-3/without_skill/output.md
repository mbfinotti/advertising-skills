## The $310 CAC is wrong — it's counting non-new customers as new

$93,000 marketing spend ÷ 300 "new customers" = $310. But 60 of those 300 are reactivations and renewals, not customers your marketing spend acquired. Real new customers = 300 − 60 = **240**.

**Corrected CAC = $93,000 / 240 = $387.50** (25% higher than reported)

## Blended ARPU (2/3 Basic, 1/3 Pro)

Of 240 new customers: 160 Basic, 80 Pro.

| Plan | Count | Price | Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic | 160 | $15 | $2,400 |
| Pro | 80 | $99 | $7,920 |
| **Total** | **240** | | **$10,320** |

Blended ARPU = $10,320 / 240 = **$43.00/month**

Monthly gross margin per customer = $43 × 70% = **$30.10**

## CAC Payback (Skok's formula: CAC ÷ monthly gross margin per customer)

**$387.50 / $30.10 = 12.9 months**

## Where you stand

You're **over the 12-month threshold**, not under it. The reported $310 CAC (10.3 months payback) was an illusion — it used an inflated "new customer" count as the denominator. Correcting for that pushes payback from a comfortable 10.3 months to a failing 12.9 months.

This is also worse than it looks in one more respect: because Basic outnumbers Pro 2-to-1 but Pro carries 6.6x the revenue, your blended ARPU is heavily diluted by the low-value plan. If Basic's mix grows further, payback keeps stretching even with CAC held flat — you're structurally dependent on Pro conversions to keep this metric under control.

**Bottom line:** fix the billing definition of "new customer" before reporting CAC anywhere else — reactivations and renewals shouldn't be in that denominator. On the corrected numbers, you're about 8% over Skok's payback bar, driven by a Basic-heavy mix at a fairly low $15 price point relative to acquisition cost.
