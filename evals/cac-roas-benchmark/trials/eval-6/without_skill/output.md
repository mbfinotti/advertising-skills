## The three targets are answering different questions — none of them is "your" answer

The agency, the podcast, and the blog are each citing a number from a different context, and none of those contexts is Tessellate Prints' cost structure:

- **Agency's "4x minimum"** is a generic media-buying rule of thumb, usually pitched to clients with unknown or unstated margins — it's a CYA number, not a calculation.
- **Podcast's "MER >4, 5-8 at scale"** is about blended Marketing Efficiency Ratio (all revenue / all marketing spend, not just paid), typically from brands with thinner margins than yours, where they need more revenue cushion per ad dollar.
- **Blog's "median ecommerce ROAS ~2"** is a market median across every margin profile from 15% dropshipping to 70% SaaS-like digital goods — it tells you what's typical, not what's profitable _for you_.

## Your number comes from your margin, not the market

The only input that matters here is your 55% contribution margin (post-COGS, shipping, payment fees).

**Break-even ROAS** = 1 / contribution margin = 1 / 0.55 = **1.82x**

Below 1.82x ROAS, every ad-attributed sale loses money on a contribution basis. That's your floor, not your target.

## Target ROAS: build it from break-even + desired margin on ad spend

Pick a target contribution margin _on the ad-driven revenue itself_ (i.e., after paying for the ads), then solve for ROAS:

**Target ROAS = 1 / (contribution margin − target margin on spend)**

| Target profit margin on ad spend | Required ROAS |
| -------------------------------- | ------------- |
| Break-even (0%)                  | 1.82x         |
| 10%                              | 2.35x         |
| 20%                              | 3.33x         |
| 30%                              | 5.45x         |

So a **2.4x–3.3x ROAS** is where you're actually printing healthy profit (10-20% margin after ad cost) on marketing-driven orders — not the 4x the agency wants, and comfortably above the 2x "market median" the blog cites (which just reflects thinner-margin competitors, not a bar you should be proud to barely clear).

## Why each source's number doesn't fit

- **4x from the agency**: implicitly assumes ~25% contribution margin, or that they want to bank a bigger buffer than your unit economics require. At 55% margin, holding out for 4x means turning away profitable spend — you'd cap growth well before you hit unprofitability.
- **MER 5-8 "at scale" from the podcast**: MER is a different denominator (total revenue ÷ total spend, capturing organic/brand lift too), and it naturally _rises_ with scale because organic/retention revenue grows faster than paid spend. Don't compare a channel-level ROAS target against a blended MER benchmark — they're not the same ratio.
- **Blog's "~2x is fine"**: technically true (you're above your 1.82x break-even), but "fine" here means "not losing money," not "hitting a good target." It's the median across all margin profiles, many far thinner than yours.

## Practical recommendation

1. **Floor / kill-switch**: 1.82x ROAS (true break-even) — pause or cut any campaign/channel below this.
2. **Target range**: 2.5x–3.5x ROAS as your steady-state paid-acquisition goal — this banks 10-25% margin on ad spend while still letting you scale spend aggressively.
3. **Track MER separately** (aim for the podcast's 4-5x+ as you scale) as a _health check_ on overall marketing efficiency including brand/organic lift — not as your per-channel bidding target.
4. Layer in **target CAC payback / LTV:CAC** if you have repeat purchase data (poster buyers often reorder), since ROAS alone ignores repeat revenue and could make you underinvest in acquisition that pays back over 2-3 orders.

Bottom line: **aim for ~2.5-3.5x ROAS**, not 4x — your 55% margin gives you more room than the agency's generic benchmark assumes, and chasing 4x leaves profitable growth on the table.
