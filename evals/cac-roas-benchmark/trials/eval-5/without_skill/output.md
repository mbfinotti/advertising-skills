**No — don't run with "CAC improved 35%" as currently framed. It's comparing two different metrics, not the same metric across two quarters.**

**The math as marketing has it:**

- Q1: $190,000 (paid spend) ÷ 2,000 (paid-attributed customers) = **$95**
- Q2: $217,000 (total marketing spend) ÷ 3,500 (all new customers) = **$62**
- Change: −34.7% ≈ "35% improvement"

**The problem:** the numerator switched from _paid spend_ to _total spend_, and the denominator switched from _paid-attributed customers_ to _all customers (paid + organic)_, in the same quarter that a one-time PR hit inflated organic signups. That's not an efficiency gain — it's a definition change that happens to land in the same direction as a lucky, non-repeatable event.

**What the same-methodology comparison actually shows:**

- Q1 paid CAC: $190,000 ÷ 2,000 = **$95**
- Q2 paid CAC: $185,000 ÷ 1,850 = **$100**
- Change: **+5.3% — paid CAC got worse**, not better.

So on a true apples-to-apples (paid-to-paid) basis, the team's paid acquisition efficiency slipped slightly quarter over quarter. The entire "improvement" in the blended number is explained by ~1,650 organic customers (3,500 − 1,850) landing for free-in-the-formula after the PR hit, diluting the denominator.

**Why this matters for a Series B deck specifically:** board members and their associates will build a model from your numbers, and CAC-by-cohort/channel is one of the first things diligence teams recompute. A metric-definition switch that happens to produce a round "35% improvement" headline, discovered later, reads as either sloppy or deliberately misleading — worse for credibility than just showing the real trend.

**Recommended slide framing instead:**

1. Show paid CAC Q1 vs Q2 honestly: $95 → $100 (+5.3%), and be ready to explain why (rising CPCs, channel saturation, sales cycle change — whatever the real driver is).
2. Show blended CAC as a _separate_ line, labeled clearly as blended, with a callout that Q2 benefited from a PR-driven organic surge (1,650 organic customers). Frame this as a channel-mix / brand story, not an efficiency story.
3. If you want one "improvement" number, use blended CAC vs blended CAC — but that requires Q1's _total_ marketing spend and _total_ new customers (organic included), which isn't in what you gave me. If Nivara didn't track organic customer count in Q1, say that explicitly rather than defaulting to the paid-only Q1 figure as a stand-in for blended.

**Suggested slide language:**

> "Paid CAC held roughly flat ($95 → $100, +5%) despite rising channel costs. A Q2 PR feature drove a step-change in organic acquisition (~1,650 organic customers), pulling blended CAC to $62 — validating investment in earned/organic channels alongside paid."

This tells a _better_ story than the flawed 35% claim: it shows paid discipline plus a credible organic growth lever, instead of a number that won't survive a follow-up question in the room.
