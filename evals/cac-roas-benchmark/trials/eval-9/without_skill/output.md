## Short answer: No — not on this evidence alone.

## The unit economics first

- Contribution margin per order: 52% × $95 AOV = **$49.40**
- At $38 CAC: $11.40 profit/order (23% of AOV) — healthy
- At $46 CAC: $3.40 profit/order (3.6% of AOV) — thin, but still **profitable**, and that's before counting any repeat purchases (watch straps are a repeat-buy category — people own multiple straps).

So Meta hasn't crossed into loss-making territory. It's degraded, not broken. That distinction matters because "CAC went up 20%" and "channel is saturated, diversify now" are two different claims, and only the first one is actually supported by the data given.

## What "CAC went up" doesn't tell you

A 20% CAC rise over two data points (essentially July→August) is consistent with several very different root causes, most of which have nothing to do with saturation and none of which are ruled out here:

1. **Spend scaling** — if budget was increased over those two months, a rising blended CAC is the *expected* diminishing-returns curve, not saturation. Pulling spend back down would likely pull CAC back down too. This is the single most important missing fact.
2. **Seasonal CPM inflation** — July is typically a cheap month for Meta CPMs; CPMs climb through Aug–Sept as more advertisers ramp toward Q4. Some (maybe most) of this move could be market-wide, not account-specific.
3. **Creative fatigue** — same creative, rising frequency, falling CTR is a very common and very fixable cause of CAC drift.
4. **Attribution/measurement drift** — Meta's attribution windows and modeled conversions shift; check if this is a real cost change or a reporting artifact.
5. **Actual saturation** — audience genuinely exhausted, but you can't distinguish this from #1–4 without more data.

## What to check before moving any budget

- Spend trend over the same 2 months (flat, up, or down?)
- CPM, frequency, and CTR trend (fatigue vs. auction inflation)
- New-customer CAC vs. blended CAC (retargeting can mask prospecting decay)
- Same period last year, if available (seasonality check)
- Repeat purchase rate / LTV — if straps have decent repeat/gifting behavior, a $46 CAC on the true LTV basis may still be excellent
- Whether an incrementality/conversion-lift test on Meta has ever been run — this is the actual test for saturation, not the CAC trendline

## On the proposed move

Shifting 30% of budget to TikTok and Pinterest with **zero pilot data** on what CAC looks like there is the riskier bet, not the safer one. Pinterest is plausible for a visual product like straps (aspirational/gifting intent); TikTok is a much colder, lower-intent audience for a considered $95 purchase and often converts worse without heavy top-of-funnel investment first.

**Recommendation:** Don't reallocate 30% off a two-month CAC trend and team consensus. Instead:

1. Spend a day pulling the diagnostics above (spend/CPM/frequency/CTR + LTV).
2. If spend didn't increase and CPMs/frequency point to real fatigue → refresh creative and test broader audiences on Meta before assuming the platform is dead.
3. Run a capped test (10–15% of budget, not 30%) into TikTok/Pinterest with a defined CAC target and a 4–6 week window, so you get real numbers instead of a hunch.
4. Watch the margin cushion: at current trend, another ~7–8% CAC rise (~$49–50) puts Meta at breakeven on first-order economics. That's the actual trigger to worry about, not the 20% move you've already seen — but it's a reason to diagnose fast, not to reallocate blind.
