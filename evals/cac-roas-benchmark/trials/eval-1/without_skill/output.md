## Short answer: No — you're not healthy in July, and the metric you're using to argue "healthy" doesn't hold up mathematically.

### 1. The 4.6 combined ROAS number is broken — check it against your own revenue

If Meta + Google combined report a **4.6 ROAS** on **$96,000** of spend, they're jointly claiming:

**4.6 × $96,000 = $441,600 in attributed revenue.**

Your actual net revenue for the *entire business*, across all channels, was **$240,000**. The platforms are claiming **84% more revenue than your store actually did** ($201,600 of phantom credit).

That's the signature of Meta and Google both taking last-click/view-through credit for the **same conversions** — "combined ROAS" by simple addition double-counts overlapping attribution. It's not a real number. Comparing it to a "4:1 industry standard" is comparing a broken metric to a benchmark measured the same broken way. Throw it out for decision-making.

### 2. Use MER (revenue ÷ total marketing spend) — it's the one number that reconciles to cash

⚠️ Your Jan–Jun series only makes sense (stays monotonic) if it includes **all** marketing cash cost, not just media buy:

| Month | MER |
|---|---|
| Jan | 2.62 |
| Feb | 2.55 |
| Mar | 2.48 |
| Apr | 2.41 |
| May | 2.33 |
| Jun | 2.24 |
| **Jul** | **2.18** ($240,000 ÷ $110,000 media+agency) |

(If you compute July on media spend alone — $240,000 ÷ $96,000 = 2.50 — the trend breaks upward for no reason, which tells you that's the wrong denominator. Agency/creative fees are a real, recurring cost of running paid media and belong in the ratio.)

That's **seven straight months of declining marketing efficiency**, not a one-month blip.

### 3. Your breakeven MER, and where July actually lands

With a 38% contribution margin, the MER you need just to break even on marketing (ignoring fixed overhead like salaries/rent/tooling) is:

**Breakeven MER = 1 ÷ 0.38 = 2.63**

You crossed *below* that line back in **January** (2.62) and have been under it, worsening, every month since. July's 2.18 is nowhere close.

### 4. What that means in dollars, this month

- Contribution margin generated: 38% × $240,000 = **$91,200**
- Total marketing spend (media + agency/creative): $96,000 + $14,000 = **$110,000**
- **Contribution profit: $91,200 − $110,000 = −$18,800**

🔴 You lost money on a contribution basis in July, before a single dollar of fixed overhead (salaries, software, rent) is even considered. To break even at 38% margin and $110,000 of spend, you needed **$289,474** in revenue — you were **~$49,000 short**.

### 5. On scaling next month

Don't scale spend as-is. You'd be pouring more budget into a channel mix that's been getting less efficient for seven consecutive months and is currently contribution-negative — scaling amplifies the loss, it doesn't fix it.

Before adding budget:
- Diagnose the 7-month decline: creative fatigue, audience saturation, rising CPMs, or discounting eroding margin — MER decay this smooth and consistent usually means saturation, not noise.
- Get platform reporting reconciled to actual revenue (fix attribution settings / move to a real MMM or incrementality read) so you're steering on truth, not on a number that overstates reality by 84%.
- Push MER back comfortably above 2.63 (with room to spare for fixed costs and actual profit — treat 2.63 as the floor, not the target) before increasing spend.
- Re-evaluate the $14,000/month agency fee — it hasn't stopped or slowed the decline.
