# Spend Health Verdict — Marketing, Last 6 Months

**Verdict: Spend is stable, not a fire drill — but "healthy" cannot be certified yet. We're missing the one number that would prove it.**

## What the data shows

| Metric | Trend | Range | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| CAC | 410 → 395 → 402 → 398 → 405 → 400 | $395–$410 (3.7% spread) | Flat. No drift up or down. |
| MER | 3.1 → 3.2 → 3.1 → 3.15 → 3.1 → 3.1 | 3.1–3.2 (3% spread) | Flat. No drift up or down. |

- Std dev on CAC is ~$5 (1.2% of mean) — that's noise, not a trend.
- First-half vs second-half average: CAC $402 → $401, MER 3.13 → 3.12. No inflection either direction.
- 5-week B2B sales cycle is already absorbed by your monthly windows — attribution lag isn't distorting these numbers.

**Bottom line on the raw metrics: the growth engine's efficiency is as steady as it's been in 6 months. Nothing here is getting worse. Nothing here is getting better either.**

## The gap that blocks a real verdict

CAC and MER don't triangulate into a profitability answer, even together:

- **MER (3.1x) is revenue ÷ total marketing spend.** In a subscription business, that revenue is dominated by your existing customer base (renewals, expansion), not by the new customers CAC is trying to measure. A healthy-looking MER can coexist with unprofitable new-customer acquisition — the existing book is carrying it.
- **CAC ($400) is only a cost.** Without contribution margin (gross margin minus variable cost-to-serve) per new customer, $400 could be trivially profitable or a slow bleed. Revenue per customer, contract length, and margin all matter and none of them are in these two numbers.
- Put together: MER tells you the business overall is generating >3x its ad spend in revenue. It does **not** tell you the marginal customer marketing is buying is profitable. Those are different questions.

**So the honest statement for Monday is: "spend efficiency is stable" — not "spend is healthy."** Don't let the room round the first into the second.

## Provisional read (labeled assumption, not a real answer)

If you want a directional number to say out loud, not to defend:

- PropTech/vertical SaaS B2B gross margins typically run 75–80%.
- At 78% gross margin, MER 3.1x → **~2.4x gross-profit-to-spend** ($2.42 gross profit per $1 marketing spend).
- That's before CS cost-to-serve, sales comp, and other non-marketing variable costs — so it's an upper bound, not contribution margin.
- **Flag this explicitly as an assumption pulled from category benchmarks, not from our own books**, if you use it Monday.

## What to tell the exec team

1. Spend is stable for 6 straight months — no efficiency crisis, no case for an emergency cut.
2. We can't yet certify the spend is *profitable* — that requires contribution margin, which finance can't produce until next month.
3. Directionally, using standard SaaS margin benchmarks (not our actuals), the math likely clears — best framed as "no red flag, pending confirmation," not "green light."
4. Recommendation: hold spend flat through the audit. A flat trend gives no urgency to move budget either way before we have real numbers.

## What unblocks a real verdict next month

- Contribution margin per new customer (gross margin − variable cost-to-serve − onboarding cost).
- MER should be split: new-customer revenue ÷ marketing spend, separate from expansion/renewal revenue — otherwise it will keep masking new-logo unit economics.
- Once you have contribution margin, CAC payback period (contribution margin ÷ CAC, expressed in months) is the number that actually answers "is this spend healthy."
