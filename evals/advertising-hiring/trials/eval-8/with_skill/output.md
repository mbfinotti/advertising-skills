**Salary — don't budget to your friend's $170k.**

That figure is real, but it's the exact number this data explicitly warns against using: in the Duane Brown / Take Some Risk 2026 PPC salary survey (445 responses, reported in Search Engine Land, Benjamin Wenner, April 13, 2026), the $170k in-house figure for the 6-9 year band is flagged by the source itself as a small, outlier-skewed sample. Your friend's team is one data point, not the market.

The reliable anchor for 7 years' experience (6-9 year band) in that same survey:

- **US median, 6-9 years: $100,000** — directional, source/date above.
- In-house does out-earn agency at every band, but the only band where that premium is reported cleanly is 10-15 years ($140,000 in-house vs. $123,545 agency, ~13%). No clean in-house premium exists for the 6-9 year band — the number that would show it is the flagged outlier.

**Budget: plan around $100k-$115k** (median, with a modest allowance for the general in-house premium seen at other bands), not $170k. Treat $170k as what you'd pay to retain a proven senior outcome-owner, not the entry point for the req.

**Turnover — never a single rate, quote the range:**

- Sampled-agency average: 18-20% (Campaign US Agency Performance Review, 2025)
- UK member-agency staff turnover: 26.8% (IPA, 2021)
- Industry average: ~30% (ANA, via Adweek)
- Junior agency turnover: ~45% in first two years (secondary source, The Trust Agency)

**Model backfill risk at 18-45%, depending on source/cohort/year** — there is no single defensible industry number. Note these are predominantly agency-side figures; no in-house-specific churn stat exists in the sourced data, so apply directionally. One attribution point worth building into your planning either way: churn that shows up in the first 45-90 days traces to onboarding and access delays, not the hire — budget your ramp (day-one access, week-one spend threshold) accordingly rather than assuming you'll need a backfill for cause.

⚠️ Flag: compensation terms need HR/legal review before the req goes out — these figures are scoping input only, not an offer band.
