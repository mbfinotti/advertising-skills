# Copperline — Senior Performance Marketer Screen

Three changes from your draft, before the deliverable:

- **"Target ROAS is the right answer" is a leading rubric.** It rewards buzzword recall over judgment. Reworded to ask when they'd _avoid_ automated ROAS bidding — seniority shows in the caveats.
- **"Performance Max under the hood" isn't fully knowable** — Google doesn't publish the algorithm. Reworded to test how they operate and audit it instead.
- **ROAS-centric framing may not fit Copperline.** B2B fintech usually runs on CAC/LTV/cost-per-SQL with long, CRM-based sales cycles, not same-session revenue. Added two fintech/B2B-specific questions the original screen was missing entirely (compliance, offline attribution). Also downgraded certifications from hard gate to soft signal — Google Ads and Meta Blueprint certs are self-paced quizzes with near-zero screening power on their own.

---

## Must-have gate (pass/fail before scoring)

1. Verifiable **$250k+/month managed spend** — get client/employer names and dates; this is reference-checkable, treat it as a hard gate.
2. **Google Ads + Meta Blueprint certified** — record as present/absent, but don't auto-reject on this alone. Weight it as a tiebreaker, not a filter.
3. **B2B or regulated-industry experience** — not disqualifying if absent, but flag it. It changes how much weight to give Q5 and Q6 below.

## Question bank

**1. Metrics fluency** _(60-second warm-up)_
"Walk me through your formula for CTR, and separately for the cost/return metric you use most day-to-day — ROAS, CAC, or cost-per-SQL, whichever applies."

- Good: correct formulas cold; explains _which_ metric they default to and why; distinguishes ROAS (revenue-based) from CAC (cost-based).
- Red flag: can't state formulas without hesitation; conflates ROAS and ROI.

**2. Bid strategy judgment**
"Tell me about a bid strategy you've relied on and why it fit that account — then one situation where you deliberately chose _not_ to use target ROAS or tCPA."

- Good: names conversion-volume thresholds automated bidding needs to work; flags that ROAS bidding is often wrong for lead-gen without clean revenue-at-conversion data.
- Red flag: "always use target ROAS," no counter-example offered.

**3. Performance Max — operating practice, not internals**
"How do you approach managing and auditing a Performance Max campaign, given it's largely a black box?"

- Good: asset group structure, brand exclusions, first-party data feeds, search terms insights, incrementality testing/holdout groups.
- Red flag: treats it as set-and-forget; no answer on control or measurement.

**4. Account structure — adapted to context**
"Walk me through how you'd structure this account for us, knowing we're B2B fintech with a longer sales cycle. What would you do differently than a typical ecommerce structure?"

- Good: consolidates for conversion volume (over-segmenting kills Smart Bidding at B2B volumes), offline conversion import from CRM, MQL/SQL feedback loop with sales.
- Red flag: generic ecommerce answer (shopping feeds, product campaigns) with no adaptation.

**5. Regulated-industry / compliance awareness** _(new — gap in original)_
"Have you run paid media for a regulated or financial services product? What's different about ad policy or claims review on Google/Meta for fintech vs. a typical B2C brand?"

- Good: aware of Google's Financial Products & Services policy, Meta's special ad category restrictions on credit-related targeting, working with legal on claims, higher account-suspension risk.
- Red flag: no awareness these restrictions exist.

**6. Attribution beyond last-click** _(new — gap in original)_
"How do you measure and report performance when the sales cycle is weeks or months and doesn't close in-platform?"

- Good: CRM-based offline conversions, cost-per-SQL/opportunity, comfort optimizing on lagging or proxy signals.
- Red flag: only fluent in in-platform last-click conversions.
