## Question Bank

Structured per competency; each question maps to exactly one. Order reflects judgment revealed per minute asked.

### 1. Measurement & attribution judgment _(senior filter — most expensive to ask and grade, decides the hire)_

- **ROAS-drop diagnosis.** "Blended ROAS on our top B2B campaign drops 30% week over week. Walk me through how you diagnose it." Expect the fixed order unprompted: measurement (pixel/server-event dedup, platform-vs-backend gap) → media (CPM, frequency, saturation) → creative fatigue (hook/hold rate, CTR by unit) → post-click (landing page, conversion rate). Follow-up: "Backend pipeline is flat while platform ROAS falls — what does that tell you?" (Correct: tracking/attribution gap, not true underperformance.)
- **Platform metrics vs. business reality, B2B version.** "A campaign reports a great cost-per-lead. How do you know if it's actually good?" Strong answer translates it into cost-per-SQL and pipeline value, and flags that platform-reported conversions are structurally biased upward (every platform claims credit for the same conversion). Weak answer stops at platform-reported CPL.

### 2. Commercial reasoning over recall

- **CTR/ROAS relationship (replaces the formula question).** "CTR jumps 40% on a campaign and ROAS stays flat. What's your read?" Score how they connect the two metrics (creative got more clicks from a less-qualified audience? landing page didn't hold? bid strategy chasing volume over value?) — not whether they can recite either formula.
- **Bid strategy (replaces the "I want to hear target ROAS" framing).** "What's your favourite bid strategy, and when would you _not_ use it?" There is no single correct platform feature to name. Score: "it depends, here's my reasoning" against stated conditions (data volume, conversion lag on a long B2B cycle, value-based bidding needing clean revenue signal) — not which named strategy they land on. A candidate who says target ROAS with no caveat scores the same as one who says Max Conversions with no caveat: both are reciting a favorite, not reasoning.
- **Account structure (replaces "clean best practices = top marks").** "How would you structure this account?" Ask _why_, not what. **Top marks:** structure justified against Copperline's specific funnel, sales-cycle length, and current data volume, with a stated test-and-learn plan. **Low marks (2/5 ceiling regardless of polish):** "best practice is..." or a generic textbook structure with no reference to context.
- **Performance Max, judgment version (your Q3, kept but reframed).** Don't ask "how does PMax work under the hood" as a mechanics quiz. Ask: "When would you _not_ run PMax, and what do you lose in visibility and control when you do?" Score honesty about the black-box tradeoff over a confident recitation of the auction mechanics — Jon Kagan's flag applies directly here: "If they tell me they understand it, I know they are lying."

### 3. Exaggerator-catching probes _(one minute each, gradeable by anyone on the panel)_

- "Who do you admire or look up to in paid media, and why?" (McGarry — reveals who's exaggerating experience.)
- "What industry publications or newsletters do you read?" (Mackey — ask at every level.)
- Note any claimed "full mastery" of PMax, Advantage+, or other automated campaign types as a yellow flag, not a green one.

### 4. Killed-campaign and incrementality probes _(pairs with the $250k+/month spend filter — never ask the spend number alone)_

- "Walk me through a campaign you killed, and why."
- "How do you separate incremental revenue from attribution-modeling noise?" On the resume's spend claim specifically: "You list $Xk/month managed at [previous company] — whose media strategy was that, yours or one you inherited and executed?" Answers that stay at the level of platform features mean they executed someone else's strategy.

### 5. B2B situational questions

- "How would you improve lead quality — not lead volume — on a mid-funnel campaign?" (Ani, adapted from lead/call quality to B2B lead/SQL quality.)
- "What's your current understanding of attribution, and how has it changed for you in the last 3 years?" (Andrews.)
- "How do you evaluate media budget allocation across two or more channels when conversion lag is 60-90 days?" (Schmelzle, adapted to a B2B sales cycle.)
- "What metrics do you track weekly, and how do you communicate results to a revenue-owning stakeholder who doesn't live in the ad platforms?" (DeFazio.)

### 6. Commitment screen _(cut first if the loop runs short — predicts tenure, not performance)_

- "Five years from now, the role is going exactly how you wanted. What does that look like?" (Aslam — filters tourists.)

## Scorecard

6 competencies, each owned by one interview stage. Weighting below is **this skill's own convention**, not a published rubric — say so if you share this externally. The only published anchor point (Pare, vendor content) weights Technical 40 / Communication 30 / Commercial insight 30; this scorecard leans further toward judgment given the seniority and the B2B-specific instinct-transfer risk.

| Competency                                    | Weight                                   | Owning stage         | Questions                             |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Measurement & attribution judgment            | 25%                                      | Technical/case round | Senior filters §1                     |
| Commercial reasoning (vs. recall)             | 25%                                      | Technical/case round | §2                                    |
| B2B pipeline instinct (CPL/CPSQL, long-cycle) | 20%                                      | Peer/founder round   | §5                                    |
| Spend ownership & incrementality              | 15%                                      | Screen               | §4                                    |
| Communication to non-specialist stakeholders  | 10%                                      | Peer/founder round   | DeFazio question, general observation |
| Provenance / exaggeration check               | 5% (disqualifying signal, not scored up) | Screen               | §3                                    |

**Anchor points (1-5), written per question rather than generically** — example for the account-structure question:

- **1-2:** Recites best practices or a generic template with no reference to Copperline's context; can't say why beyond "that's how it's done."
- **3:** Structure is reasonable and shows awareness of B2B funnel stages, but justification is thin or borrowed.
- **4-5:** Structure is derived from stated assumptions about Copperline's data volume, sales cycle, and current gaps, with an explicit test-and-learn plan for what they're unsure about. Naming their own uncertainty is a positive signal, not a hedge.

**Rules that apply to every question in this bank:**

- Every score cites a quote or concrete example from the answer. No evidence, no score.
- Interviewers submit scores independently before any debrief; debrief time goes where scores diverge.
- Certifications: never scored, never a disqualifier if missing. If flagged at all, it's in "nice-to-have," separate from the scorecard.
- Disqualifying signals (exaggeration flags, unpaired inflated spend claims) are recorded separately from the numeric score — they can sink a candidate regardless of how well they scored elsewhere, but they don't get averaged in.
