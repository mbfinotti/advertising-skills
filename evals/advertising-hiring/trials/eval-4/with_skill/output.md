## Fernhollow — Work-Sample Redesign (Paid Media Manager)

### What's actually broken

The current final round is the one format the evidence says to never use. From the sourced practitioner consensus this skill runs on: _"test projects during the interviews are mostly hated by the community"_ — Julia Vyse calls an unpaid take-home _"straight-up time-wasting, unpaid labor,"_ Julie Bacchini: _"if you are asking someone to do work on an account... you should compensate them for their time."_ Duane Brown, who runs a live version of this exact exercise: _"I wouldn't ask someone to take stuff home or do a 4-hour media plan for me."_

Your two withdrawals this month aren't a signal about this candidate pool — they're the documented failure mode. A 4-hour unpaid deliverable due in 3 days selects for candidates with uncommitted free time, which correlates inversely with seniority. You're filtering out the people you most want.

### Deleted from the menu, by name

Per the constraint you gave (Finance: no budget for paid candidate work), two formats are removed outright, not ranked last:

- ❌ **Paid trial / paid consulting engagement** — requires budget approval and pay terms. Deleted.
- ❌ **Job shadow day** — a day of team time for a diffuse read; also assumes some form of compensation norm. Deleted.
- ❌ **Multi-hour unpaid take-home (current format)** — not on the ranked list at any position. It's the one format whose funnel cost (your two withdrawals) exceeds its signal. Replaced below.

### What replaces it: live screen-share account audit

This is the top-signal format on the list _and_ it costs you nothing Finance would object to — it consumes interview time you're already spending, not extra budget.

It only wins here because of a constraint you already stated: your head of growth is a former agency buyer who's great at reading accounts live. The skill's own rule is that the live audit requires "a panelist able to grade an audit live" — without that, this format gets removed too, and you'd default to the data-table case instead. You have that panelist, so the top-ranked format is actually available to you, which is unusual.

You also already maintain a copy of your ad account for candidate access — that's the exact prep this format needs, and you're not starting from zero.

**Session design — 75–90 minutes, inside the interview loop, no take-home, no 3-day window:**

**Total candidate time: under 90 minutes, entirely inside a scheduled interview slot.** Duane Brown runs this shape at his agency and reports better hires for cutting to it.

### Deliberate ambiguity — build it into the account copy

Score naming ambiguity as a top answer, not a deduction. Concretely: seed the copy account with one place where the data is genuinely insufficient to give a clean verdict — e.g. a campaign with a tracking gap that makes platform ROAS unreliable, or a spend spike with no attributable conversion source. A candidate who confidently optimizes off that number without flagging it is showing the exact failure mode the role punishes (platform-operator-without-judgment, your dominant hiring risk per the field's own failure-mode table). A candidate who says "I can't recommend a change here until this tracking gap is fixed" passes with the top score.

### The two senior filters — make sure the account copy supports both

1. **ROAS-drop diagnosis, in fixed order:** measurement (pixel/server dedup, platform-vs-backend gap) → media (CPM, frequency, saturation) → creative fatigue (hook rate, hold rate, CTR) → post-click (landing page, conversion rate). Build the seeded problem so a candidate who jumps straight to "creative fatigue" without ruling out measurement first loses points.
2. **MER vs. platform ROAS:** the account copy should include enough revenue/spend data that a strong candidate translates channel ROAS into MER and acquisition MER unprompted. A candidate who cites platform-reported ROAS uncritically is showing the weak answer — platform ROAS structurally overstates itself because every platform claims the same conversions.

### Rubric — write this before the first candidate sits down

Five competencies, each with a role-specific behavioral anchor (no bare numbers):

1. **Measurement-first diagnosis** — does the candidate check tracking integrity before touching media/creative levers?
2. **Commercial reasoning (MER/break-even)** — do they translate platform ROAS into business reality unprompted?
3. **Ambiguity handling** — do they name the seeded data gap, or optimize past it?
4. **Restructure judgment** — is the verbal proposal reasoned from what they found, or a generic "best practices" restructure they'd give any account?
5. **Communication under scrutiny** — can they think aloud clearly while being watched, which is most of the actual job?

Weight commercial reasoning and communication over technical recall — the only published anchor (Pare, vendor content, label it as such) weights Technical 40 / Communication 30 / Commercial 30, i.e. 60% non-technical. Treat any specific point values you assign as your own house convention, not a published standard — say so if this rubric is shared externally.

Scoring discipline: interviewers (head of growth + whoever else is in the room) submit scores independently before debriefing. Every score cites a specific quote or moment from the session — no evidence, no score.

### Debrief agenda (15 min, right after the session)

The debrief carries more signal than anything written down, because reasoning walked aloud exposes method a polished deliverable hides. Go through the five competencies in order, each panelist states their independent score first, then discuss only where scores diverge. Disqualifying signals (certifications never count here) get logged separately from the numeric score.

### Loop shape update

This round now _is_ your case/technical round — it doesn't need to be a separate 4th step. Recommended shape: screen (context + spend-under-management paired with the kill/incrementality probe) → this 90-minute live-audit round → a shorter founder/peer round if you want a third data point. That's 2–3 rounds total, no round longer than 90 minutes, nothing sent home.

### What this fixes vs. what it doesn't

- Fixes: candidate drop-off (removes the exact format the field documents as causing it), Finance's constraint (zero added budget), and your original goal (you still see a full restructure proposal — spoken instead of written, and harder to fake).
- Doesn't touch: comp band, rung/leveling, or the rest of your loop's competency coverage — flag those separately if you want the full hiring packet rather than just this stage.

### Deletions and promotions, named (per this skill's own discipline)

- Paid trial, job shadow, multi-hour unpaid take-home: **deleted**, not ranked last, per the no-budget constraint and the sourced funnel-damage finding.
- Live screen-share audit: **promoted to top choice**, unlocked specifically because you have a panelist (head of growth) who can grade it live and an account copy already built — the two conditions that usually keep this format out of reach.
- Data-table case: your fallback if scheduling ever prevents the live session — same rubric applies, same anonymized-data rule, ties the live audit on effort and signal-per-hour, loses only on realism.
