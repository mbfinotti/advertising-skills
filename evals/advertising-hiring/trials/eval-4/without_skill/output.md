# Fixing Fernhollow's final-round work sample

## What's actually broken

- 4 hours, unpaid, due in 3 days = ~$150-300 of professional labor you're asking for free.
- Two withdrawals this cycle isn't noise — it's the market telling you the ask is priced wrong.
- Real ad account access (even a copy) raises IP/liability questions and reads as "free audit," which sharpens candidates' suspicion.
- Finance won't fund it, so the fix can't be "pay them" — it has to be "ask for less."
- You're sitting on an underused asset: a head of growth who reads accounts live, in real time. That's the actual skill you're hiring for. Test it live instead of grading homework.

## The fix: replace the take-home with a live working session

**Format:** 75-minute scheduled call, screen-share, candidate + head of growth. No prep deliverable, no take-home.

1. **T-minus 24h:** send a read-only export (campaign structure, spend/ROAS by campaign, last 90 days) — a PDF/sheet, not live account access. ~20 min candidate review time, not 4 hours.
2. **First 20 min — live read:** candidate walks through what they see, thinking aloud. This is the exact skill your head of growth already excels at judging.
3. **Next 35 min — live restructure:** candidate proposes a restructure collaboratively with your head of growth pushing back, asking "why," changing constraints ("budget just got cut 20%, what do you kill first?"). This is higher-signal than a static deck — you see reasoning under pressure, not just output.
4. **Last 20 min — reverse interview:** candidate asks you questions. Paid-media people who are good at this job are used to defending numbers to clients live; this is realistic and low-prep.

## Why this actually fixes the withdrawal problem

- Time ask drops from ~4-6 hours (build + polish + submit) to ~90 minutes total, scheduled like a normal interview — no "unpaid homework due Thursday" framing.
- No account access outside the call, no deliverable ownership question, no "did they just get free consulting" resentment.
- It uses your best evaluator's actual strength instead of asking a hiring manager to grade a slide deck against a rubric nobody agreed on.

## Guardrails to keep it fair and defensible

- **Written scoring rubric, locked before the call:** e.g. diagnosis accuracy (0-3), prioritization logic (0-3), reasoning under pushback (0-3), communication (0-3). Score immediately after each call — live formats drift toward gut-feel without this.
- **Same account export for every candidate** in a given hiring round, so nobody gets an easier data set.
- **Record the call (with consent)** so a second person can calibrate scoring, since "great at reading accounts live" is a strength for your head of growth but a single-rater risk for you.
- **Cap total process time** communicated up front: e.g. "30 min screen + 75 min live session + 30 min culture chat, that's the whole loop."

## What to keep from the old process

- Keep a short account-structure exercise as prep material — it's realistic and cheap. Just make it a _conversation starter_, not a graded deliverable.
- If you still want an async component for candidates who interview better in writing, cap it at 30 minutes: "Here's a screenshot of one campaign — write 3 bullet points on what you'd change and why." That's a coffee-break ask, not a workday.

## One thing to flag to finance, separately

You don't need budget for this fix. But if you ever want to A/B a paid micro-project for your top 1-2 finalists only (not all candidates) — say $150-250 for a 2-hour scoped task — that's a much smaller ask than what you had, and worth revisiting once you see whether the live-session format alone stops the withdrawals.
