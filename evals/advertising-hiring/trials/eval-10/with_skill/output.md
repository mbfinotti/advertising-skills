## Why Plan A fails

- **It breaks the ladder's own rule.** Creative strategist is defined as "a separate role bridging creative and buying, not a senior buyer" — not a tier you bolt onto an existing media-buyer title. Constant Hire's named failure mode is precisely a setup with "media buyers spending budgets... and no one owning the [creative] strategy tying it all together" — a hybrid title doesn't remove that gap, it just relabels the buyer as also owning it, with no added hours in their day.
- **It doesn't touch the actual constraint.** The problem is volume and win rate, not judgment quality in isolation. Splitting one person's attention across spend management and creative strategy doesn't produce more or better concepts — it produces the same 4/month from someone now doing two jobs.
- **Comp confirms it's underscoped even as a title change.** Published creative-strategy-director bands run $180k–$250k+ (Foxwell Digital, 2026, directional). A $10k bump on a media-buyer salary isn't in that zone. Foxwell's own warning applies directly: going cheap on this seat is "the fastest way to light your ad budget on fire." If you're not funding the role at strategist comp, don't grant it strategist scope.

**Delete Plan A. Name why in writing** (per the skill's own rule that every ruled-out option gets recorded, not left ranked last): role-scope conflict + comp mismatch, not "the buyer isn't good enough."

## Why Plan B is close but scoped wrong

- **"Mid-level creative" is the wrong rung.** If that means a designer/editor who produces assets, you'll reproduce the same named failure mode from the other direction: production without strategy ownership. Your actual gap is someone who owns *win rate* — reads hook rate, hold rate, CTR-by-unit, and briefs from that, not just makes more assets. Retitle the req: **Creative Strategist**, not "creative."
- **Reporting to the buyer inverts the role's purpose.** The whole point of this rung is sitting at the intersection of research, creative direction, and performance — as a peer, testing and killing concepts together with the buyer. Subordinate it to the buyer and you've just added headcount under the same bottleneck decision-maker, not new judgment.
- **"Portfolios all look amazing, screening should be easy" is backwards — and this skill flags this exact trap by name:** *"AI-generated portfolios have made portfolio screening unreliable for judging creative-strategy skill."* Easy-looking portfolios in 2026 are a red flag for this rung, not a green light. Screen on a live/paid work sample, not the deck.
- **$75k is unverified against any sourced band for this rung.** The only creative-strategy comp figure in this skill's sources is the $180k–$250k+ *director* band — not the right comparison for a mid-level hire, but also not evidence that $75k is fine. Flag it for a local market check before you finalize (HR/legal, outside this skill's scope).

## One more thing worth naming

Both plans assume the fix has to be permanent and immediate. If the search itself will take 4–8 weeks and the win-rate slide is painful now, a short freelance creative-pod retainer as a bridge is a legitimate stopgap — it doesn't compound the way an in-house strategist does, so don't let it become the permanent answer, but it buys concept volume during the hire window without locking in the wrong scope under time pressure.
