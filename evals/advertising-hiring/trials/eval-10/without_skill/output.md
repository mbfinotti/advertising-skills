**Neither plan as framed. Run a modified version — and fix the diagnosis first.**

**The stated problem is under-specified in a way that matters:** 4 concepts/month at $200k/month spend is low by DTC performance-marketing norms — most accounts at that spend run 8–15+ new concepts/month to outrun creative fatigue. A falling win rate at flat volume is consistent with *audience/creative saturation*, not just "weak ideas." Before picking a plan, confirm: is win rate falling because concepts are bad, or because the same 4/month can't refresh fast enough to beat fatigue? These need different fixes.

**Plan A — reject as written.**
- It doesn't add production capacity. Your buyer still has a full-time buying job; "creative strategy" gets bolted on with no reduction in buying scope. You'll get worse buying, not better creative — or a burned-out hybrid who does neither well.
- Media buying and creative strategy are adjacent but distinct skills (optimization vs. ideation/production). A great buyer isn't automatically a good creative brief-writer, and you're betting $10k/month with no test.
- Single point of failure: your whole creative-plus-buying function now sits in one head.

**Plan B — reject as written, for a specific reason: the screening logic is broken.**
- "Their portfolios all look amazing these days" is the tell. Portfolio polish predicts *brand/agency* creative ability, not direct-response performance creative. The two are almost inversely correlated — the ads that win on Meta/TikTok are often deliberately unpolished (UGC-style, native, hook-first). Screening on portfolio aesthetics will filter *for* the wrong hire.
- Reporting to the buyer is a structural mistake: the buyer has no management bandwidth or creative-management skill, so the hire gets no real direction — you'll have paid for a report line, not a function.
- One mid-level hire also doesn't move you from 4 to 10+ concepts/month on its own.

**What to run instead:**
1. Hire the creative role, but fix the target profile and screen: look for direct-response / performance-creative background (in-house DTC, or agency work you can verify against actual ad performance, not just reel quality) — and screen with a paid test task (turn a brief into 2–3 ad concepts, review against your own historical winners) instead of a portfolio review.
2. Have the hire report to you/founder or a marketing lead — not the buyer. Pair them tightly with the buyer via a weekly data readout (what's winning/fading) so creative briefs are driven by buying data, without making the buyer their manager.
3. Don't give the buyer a title change or comp bump yet. Instead, formalize a recurring (weekly) creative-brief session where the buyer feeds performance data into the brief — this captures Plan A's actual intent (buyer's data insight informing creative) without diluting their core job.
4. Treat volume as the first lever to pull: target 8–12 concepts/month within 60–90 days (mostly iterations/variants of proven winners plus a few net-new), not just "better" concepts at the same cadence.
5. Revisit comp/promotion for the buyer in ~90 days if the new brief loop demonstrably improves win rate — earn the raise with evidence rather than pre-paying for a role that doesn't exist yet.

**Bottom line:** Plan A overloads one person with the wrong second skill; Plan B hires the wrong profile into a broken reporting line and screens on the wrong signal. Fix the screening criteria, fix the reporting line, and treat volume — not just talent — as part of the bottleneck.
