Both the VP and the CMO are half-right, and the way they're each framing it is what's producing the deadlock. Here's the actual answer, then what to do in the final round.

## Who's right

**Your CMO is right that "media buying skill" transfers.** Six years scaling $2M→$40M means this person has demonstrably done the hard part of paid media: built attribution systems, managed CAC/LTV tradeoffs under real budget pressure, scaled spend without cratering ROAS, ran creative testing at volume, and survived platform changes (iOS 14.5, cookie deprecation, rising CPMs) that killed less disciplined operators. That's rare and it's real. Someone who can do that is not a novice at media economics, testing rigor, or budget ownership.

**Your VP of Sales is right that DTC and B2B enterprise motion are different disciplines** — but he's wrong about _why_ it matters, and that's the part worth correcting before the final round. It's not that "ecommerce people don't get B2B" as some kind of intelligence or sophistication gap. It's that the two channels optimize for structurally different things:

- **DTC paid media**: single-touch or short-window attribution, one decision-maker (the buyer), success measured in days/weeks, creative-led (the ad IS the pitch), spend scales against a purchase signal that happens in the funnel itself.
- **B2B infra paid media (6-month cycle)**: multi-touch attribution across a buying committee, success measured in quarters, the ad's job is to start a relationship not close a sale, "conversion" is a form fill or a demo request that then has to survive a sales cycle the media buyer doesn't control, and the real optimization target (pipeline influenced, not last-click ROAS) is much harder to instrument and often invisible for months.

So the VP's instinct is pointing at a real risk — but "reject" is an overcorrection. The risk isn't competence, it's **whether this person's 6 years of pattern-matching (fast feedback loops, single-touch attribution, optimize-to-purchase) will make them impatient or mis-calibrated in an environment where the feedback loop is 10x longer and the metrics that matter for the first two quarters are leading indicators, not sales.**

## What this actually predicts

People coming from DTC into long-cycle B2B tend to fail (when they fail) in specific, predictable ways:

- They over-index on last-click/MER-style metrics and undervalue upper-funnel/brand or ABM spend because it doesn't show ROI in 30 days.
- They get twitchy and reallocate budget too fast — DTC punishes patience, B2B infra punishes impatience.
- They don't know how to partner with sales on lead handoff, SQL definitions, or account-based targeting because DTC has no SDR/AE layer to coordinate with.
- They may not have run ABM, LinkedIn/intent-based targeting, or paid programs aimed at influencing a buying committee rather than an individual.

None of that is disqualifying. All of it is _testable_.

## What to do in the final round

Don't resolve this as a philosophical debate ("does skill transfer?") — resolve it as a diagnostic. Structure the final round to directly probe the failure modes above:

1. **Case study, not a pitch.** Give them a real (anonymized) Deckhouse scenario: "$500K quarterly paid budget, 6-month cycle, multi-threaded buying committee, sparse early signal." Ask them to build the measurement framework and budget allocation for the first two quarters. What you're listening for: do they reach for pipeline-influence / MQL-to-SQL / multi-touch attribution concepts on their own, or do they try to force DTC-style last-click logic onto it? Do they ask about the sales cycle and committee structure before proposing tactics?

2. **Ask them directly to name the transfer gap.** "What part of DTC media buying do you think does NOT transfer to a 6-month B2B infra sales cycle?" A strong candidate names the attribution/patience problem unprompted. A weak one insists it's basically the same game with a longer funnel.

3. **Probe patience under pressure.** "Board asks why paid spend hasn't produced pipeline in Q1. What do you tell them?" You want an answer built on leading indicators (engagement depth, account coverage, MQL quality, sales-accepted rate) — not "give it more time" with nothing to show, and not panic-driven reallocation.

4. **Test sales partnership instinct.** Put your VP of Sales in the room for this one specifically — have him ask how they'd define a qualified lead jointly with sales, and how they'd handle disagreement over lead quality. This also gives the VP a legitimate, evidence-based way to change his mind (or confirm his concern) rather than vetoing from a chair he isn't in.

5. **Reference check with intent.** Ask their DTC references one question: "Did they ever have to sell leadership on a metric that wasn't revenue?" That's the closest proxy you'll get for whether they can operate on leading indicators, which is the actual skill B2B infra media requires that DTC media doesn't.

**Bottom line:** don't let "ecommerce vs. B2B" stay an identity argument. Turn it into a skills gap you can observe directly in 60–90 minutes, then decide. If they show attribution/patience instincts native to long-cycle thinking, the DTC scaling experience is a genuine asset, not a liability. If they can't get out of last-click logic under questioning, your VP's instinct was correct — just for a more precise reason than "they don't get B2B."
