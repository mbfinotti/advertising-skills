# Role Scope: First Dedicated Paid Media Hire

Before the job description — two things upstream of it will determine whether this hire succeeds, and I'd fix the framing on both before you post anything.

## Two corrections to the plan

**1. Reconcile tracking before you set this person's targets, not after.**
You're about to hire someone and hold them accountable to ROAS/CAC numbers you haven't verified against Shopify. If Meta is over- or under-reporting attributed revenue — extremely common with CAPI dedup issues, view-through windows, or stale pixels — you'll either scare off your best candidate with a fake efficiency problem, or let a mediocre one coast on a fake win. Fix this in parallel with sourcing, or make it the new hire's literal first deliverable (see the process section below — I'd combine it with a paid trial).

**2. "Meta media buyer" is the wrong title for what you're actually building.**
Meta being 70% of spend today argues for Meta _expertise_, not a Meta-_only_ mandate. You're launching TikTok next quarter and you want this to be a long-term in-house capability — which means the first hire either owns the full paid-channel mix (Meta + Google maintenance + TikTok launch) or you're setting up a gap where TikTok has no owner in 90 days. A narrow "Meta buyer" hire optimizes for today's mix, not the one you're building toward.

I've scoped the role below as **full-funnel performance owner, Meta-weighted** — same person you were picturing, wider mandate.

## Role definition

|                 |                                                                           |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Title**       | Senior Performance Marketing Manager                                      |
| **Reports to**  | You (founder), dotted line to Head of Ops                                 |
| **Level**       | Senior IC — first dedicated hire, no team to manage initially             |
| **Spend owned** | ~$960k/yr today (Meta + Google), scaling with TikTok test budget added Q4 |

**Scope of ownership:**

- **Meta** (primary) — full funnel: prospecting, retention/retargeting, creative testing cadence, budget pacing, incrementality reads
- **Google** (secondary) — maintain and optimize Search/PMax/Shopping; not expected to rebuild what's working
- **TikTok launch** (Q4) — owns the test plan, creative sourcing/whitelisting, and the scale/kill decision
- **Measurement** — co-owns the tracking reconciliation with ops; owns keeping it correct going forward
- **Explicitly not in scope yet:** brand/awareness campaigns, influencer/affiliate, email/SMS — call this out in the JD so candidates don't expect a bigger remit than exists

## Requirements

**Must-have:**

- 4+ years hands-on Meta media buying with **$500k–$1.5M/yr** in managed spend, in-house or freelance with real P&L accountability (agency-only experience without ownership is a yellow flag, not a disqualifier)
- DTC/ecommerce background; consideration-purchase category experience (home goods, furniture, apparel) is a real plus over low-ticket impulse categories
- Working fluency in CAPI/server-side tracking, GA4, and Shopify's order data — needs to be able to say "the platform number and the Shopify number disagree, here's why"
- Can brief and evaluate creative, not just allocate budget against what's handed to them
- Communicates cleanly to a non-marketing audience — this person reports straight to a founder and an ops lead, no marketing layer to translate for them

**Strong plus:**

- Has stood up or evaluated an attribution/BI layer (Triple Whale, Northbeam, or similar)
- TikTok Ads experience — nice to have, teachable if everything else is strong
- Has managed a channel launch from zero, not just optimized an existing one

**Disqualifiers:**

- Can't explain a testing methodology beyond "we tried things"
- No answer for how they'd handle a tracking discrepancy other than "trust the platform"
- Treats budget growth as the goal rather than efficiency — you want someone who pushes back on scaling into bad CAC

---

## Compensation band

Assuming US-based hiring (adjust if not): **base $95k–125k** + **10–15% performance bonus** tied to CAC/MER targets _after_ reconciliation, not to raw spend growth — paying a bonus on spend growth is how you end up with someone inflating budget to hit a number. A first-hire equity/profit-share gesture is optional but common at this size company and worth considering for retention given you want this in-house long-term.

## Hiring process (2 rounds, panel = you + Head of Ops)

**Timeline reality check first:** today is Sept 12. Sourcing (2–3 wks) + two interview rounds + references + a 2–4 week notice period realistically lands a start date in **mid-to-late October**. If "producing this quarter" means Q3, that's not achievable — I'd frame the internal goal as "producing in Q4," which also happens to line up with the TikTok launch, so the timeline isn't actually a conflict, just worth naming so nobody's disappointed in three weeks.

**Round 1 — Live diagnostic (45–60 min, both panelists):**
Send a sanitized pull of your real Meta campaign data (spend, ROAS/CAC, CTR, frequency — strip brand/product names) ahead of time. Have the candidate walk through their read: what's working, what's not, what they'd touch in week one. This does double duty — it's a technical screen on real numbers instead of a generic case study, and it shows you how they explain a technical read to Ops, who isn't a marketer. That's the exact skill they'll need on the job.

**Bridge step — paid audit engagement (finalist only, between rounds):**
Before round 2, pay your top 1–2 candidates a flat fee (~$1,500–$3,000) for a short, defined deliverable: audit the Meta/Google/Shopify tracking setup and produce a findings memo. This solves three problems at once — it's the tracking reconciliation you haven't done, it's a real work sample beyond interview talk, and it doesn't cost you a third formal round.

**Round 2 — Ownership and fit (both panelists):**
Discuss their audit findings, verify claims from round 1 against 2 references from people who've seen them manage comparable spend, walk through the TikTok ramp plan ("if I gave you $5k for week one, what does it look like"), and be explicit about guardrails and reporting cadence (below) before you talk comp. This is also where you confirm they're comfortable being the _only_ marketing hire reporting to two non-marketers.

## First 30/60/90

- **Days 1–14:** Finish/validate the tracking reconciliation (if not already done via the paid audit), full account audit across Meta + Google
- **Days 15–30:** Quick-win optimizations on existing spend, TikTok test plan drafted
- **Days 30–60:** TikTok live, budget allocated to what's working, kill what isn't
- **Days 60–90:** Unit economics report against verified numbers, recommendation on next hire (creative support is usually the first addition once one person owns three channels)

---

## Guardrails for the first 90 days

Given this person will control ~$1M/yr with no marketing peer to check them:

- Any week-over-week budget shift >20% needs your sign-off until the account has a 90-day track record
- Weekly written report, not a verbal update — cheap insurance for a first hire
- No new channel added beyond the agreed Meta/Google/TikTok scope without your approval

---

## Still yours to decide

- Does TikTok stay with this hire long-term, or is it a bridge until you add a second person? (Scoped above as "this hire owns launch," but worth deciding explicitly before the JD goes out)
- Target start date — I used mid/late October as realistic; confirm that's acceptable against your Q4 goals
- Whether the paid audit engagement goes to one finalist or two in parallel (two costs more but de-risks the hire further)
