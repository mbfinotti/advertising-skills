# Brindle Analytics — Performance Marketer Hiring Packet

## Read this first

Two things in your brief will shape every decision below, so I'm naming them rather than quietly working around them:

- **6 weeks is thin for LinkedIn B2B pipeline.** Campaign Manager's learning phase alone can eat 1–2 weeks, and a B2B SaaS sales cycle usually runs longer than the remaining 4–5. If "week 1" of the 6 weeks is spent on account setup and tracking, you've effectively given the channel 4 weeks of live data before deciding to kill it. I've built the trial structure (section 4) to front-load setup into the hiring process itself, so spend starts day one of the contract, not week two.
- **Positioning in flux changes who you should hire.** You don't need someone who demands a finished ICP and a locked homepage before they'll touch Campaign Manager — you need someone who treats the ambiguity as the job and tests against hypotheses. I've built that into the scorecard and the work sample as an explicit, scored competency, not a hope.

**Assumptions I made without asking** (flag if any are wrong before you run this):
- Role is ~15 hrs/week, 1099 contractor, not an employee.
- The $3,500/mo is 100% media spend — the contractor's fee is separate.
- "Pipeline" means sales-qualified opportunities or booked demos in your CRM, not just form-fill leads. This is genuinely undefined in your brief, so I've made defining it explicitly a day-one deliverable and a work-sample test.
- You have (or will have) LinkedIn conversion tracking / an Insight Tag and some way to connect ad spend to CRM outcomes, even if manually via UTMs. If that doesn't exist yet, add "get tracking live" as a week-1 milestone — it's on the critical path for the whole 6-week clock.

## 1. Scorecard

**Role:** Part-Time Performance Marketer — LinkedIn Ads (Contract)
**Reports to:** Founder
**Scope:** ~15 hrs/week, owns the $3,500/mo LinkedIn budget end to end (strategy, targeting, copy, reporting)

### Mission
Find out, in 6 weeks, whether LinkedIn Ads can produce qualified pipeline for Brindle Analytics at a $3,500/mo spend level — and tell us the truth about it, fast, even while our positioning is still moving under them.

### Outcomes (what "did the job" looks like)
| Outcome | By when |
|---|---|
| Tracking live (spend → lead → CRM stage), agreed definition of "pipeline" with founders | End of week 1 |
| 2–3 campaigns live across distinct audience/message hypotheses | End of week 2 |
| First read on cost-per-lead and early funnel signal, written up, not just dashboarded | End of week 4 |
| Clear, evidence-based recommendation: scale, pivot targeting, or kill | End of week 6 |

### Competencies (weighted)

| Weight | Competency | What "meets bar" looks like | What "exceeds" looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25% | **Small-budget B2B SaaS LinkedIn experience** | Has personally run LinkedIn campaigns at ≤$5k/mo for a B2B SaaS company, not just as one line item in a bigger agency retainer | Has done it more than once, at more than one company, and can speak to what changed between those accounts |
| 20% | **Comfort operating under ambiguity** | Doesn't ask you to "finalize the ICP" before starting; proposes testing 2–3 hypotheses in parallel instead | Has a system for running tests against moving messaging (versioned ad copy tied to homepage versions, etc.) |
| 20% | **Honest, early signal reading** | Can articulate the difference between a vanity metric (CTR, impressions) and a real one (SQL, cost per opportunity), and will say "this isn't working" before week 6 if the data says so | Proactively proposes a kill/no-kill checkpoint earlier than week 6 if signal is bad, to protect your runway |
| 15% | **Hands-on execution** | Writes their own ad copy, builds their own audiences, no dependency on a designer or a copywriter you don't have | Ships fast — first campaign live within days, not weeks |
| 10% | **Founder communication** | Explains platform mechanics and results in plain language, not agency jargon; pushes back diplomatically when a founder request is a bad idea | Proactively over-communicates in a resource-constrained, no-slack-for-surprises team |
| 10% | **Budget discipline at small scale** | Understands that $3,500/mo on LinkedIn is enough for maybe 1–2 real audience tests, not five; won't spread it thin | Recommends where *not* to spend, not just where to spend |

### Screening bar
Below 3/5 average, or below 3/5 on either of the top two weighted competencies (small-budget LinkedIn experience, comfort under ambiguity) → no offer, regardless of overall polish. A great generalist marketer who has never run a lean B2B LinkedIn account, or who needs a locked ICP before starting, is a bad fit for this specific 6-week bet even if they'd be great in a calmer context.

## 2. Interview Loop

Kept to 4 touchpoints because you're 4 people and 3 weeks — a longer loop burns your own runway, not just the candidate's.

| Stage | Who | Length | Tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Screen** | Founder | 20 min, call | Basic fit: have they actually run a small B2B LinkedIn budget, are they available for the hours/timeline, do they understand this is a 6-week make-or-break trial (say this explicitly — it's a filter, not a secret) |
| **2. Work sample** | Candidate, async | ~2 hrs, paid | See section 3 |
| **3. Work sample review** | Founder (+ whoever owns product/positioning) | 45 min | Walk through their work sample live; push on their assumptions; see how they handle "actually our pricing might change again next month" said out loud |
| **4. Reference check** | Founder | 2 calls, 15 min each | Ask specifically: "Did they tell you early when something wasn't working, or did you find out late?" This is the single highest-signal question for this role |

**No separate "culture fit" round** — at 4 people, stage 3 *is* the culture fit round. Splitting it out just adds a week you don't have.

### 3-week timeline

| Week | Activity |
|---|---|
| 1 | Post role, source (LinkedIn itself, ironically, plus a targeted post in 1–2 marketing-contractor communities), run screens |
| 2 | Send work sample Monday/Tuesday, review by Thursday/Friday, run stage-3 interviews |
| 3 | References, offer, contract signed, **tracking setup starts immediately on signing — don't wait for an official "start date"** |

## 3. Work Sample

Paid, ~2 hours, $200 flat regardless of outcome. Do not give access to your real ad account or real pipeline numbers — use the synthetic brief below so you're not extracting free strategy work on your live account, and so candidates aren't penalized for not knowing internal context they were never given.

### Brief to send candidates

> Brindle Analytics is a B2B SaaS analytics tool, ~$21k MRR, 4-person team. We have $3,500/month to spend on LinkedIn Ads and want to know in 6 weeks whether the channel can produce pipeline.
>
> Two things to know going in: our homepage messaging is being rewritten (we're choosing between two directions — one leads with "stop exporting CSVs into spreadsheets," the other leads with "the analytics layer your engineers won't have to build"), and our pricing has changed three times in the last three months, currently three tiers roughly $99 / $349 / custom.
>
> Deliverables:
> 1. A month-1 test plan: how you'd split the $3,500 across campaigns/audiences, and why. Assume you can't spend it all on one bet.
> 2. Three ad variations (headline + body), each testing a different angle or audience — not just three copy tweaks on the same idea.
> 3. Define, in your own words, what "pipeline" should mean for this trial, and what leading indicator you'd check at week 2 to know if you're on track — before final pipeline numbers are even possible.
> 4. One paragraph: it's week 4, cost-per-lead is fine but nothing has hit "opportunity" stage in the CRM yet. What do you tell us, and what do you change?

### Rubric

| Deliverable | 1 (below) | 2–3 (meets) | 4 (exceeds) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Test plan | Spreads budget across 4+ campaigns, or ignores the pricing/messaging flux entirely | Picks 2 focused bets, acknowledges the moving positioning explicitly as a constraint to design around | Ties campaign structure to *which* homepage direction each ad supports, so results double as a positioning signal for the founders |
| Ad copy | Generic SaaS copy, interchangeable with any product | Copy clearly tests different angles/audiences | Copy is specific enough to Brindle's stated problem that it couldn't be reused for a competitor |
| Pipeline definition | Says "leads" or "MQLs" without pressing further | Lands on something CRM-stage-specific (opportunity, demo booked) | Also proposes the week-2 leading indicator explicitly, unprompted |
| Week-4 answer | Says "give it more time" with no reasoning, or panics and recommends killing it immediately | Distinguishes top-of-funnel health from pipeline conversion, gives a specific next test | Recommends a concrete adjustment *and* names the point at which they'd escalate to "recommend killing this" if the founders wait |

A candidate who scores 4/4 on deliverable 4 and weak everywhere else is still worth a strong look — that question is the closest proxy you have to "will they tell me the truth in week 6."

## 4. The 6-week trial, structured (not just "wait and see")

Since the whole engagement lives or dies on 6 weeks, don't let it run as one long silent bet. Put checkpoints on the calendar at hire time, not after:

- **Day 1–3 of contract:** tracking live, pipeline definition confirmed in writing with founders (use their work-sample answer as the starting draft)
- **End of week 1:** campaigns live, spend flowing
- **End of week 2:** early read — CTR/CPL only, explicitly labeled as not-yet-decision-grade
- **End of week 4:** first real pipeline read; go/adjust/kill conversation happens here, not just at week 6, since this is realistically the last point where a targeting change can still produce a week-6 result
- **End of week 6:** final decision, using week-4 as the comparison baseline so you're judging a trend, not a single noisy data point

This also protects you contractually and emotionally: if you do have to shut the channel down and let them go at week 6, it isn't a surprise to either side — it was on the calendar from day one.
