## The Prep Plan

Ranked by judgment revealed per hour, not comfort — this is a hard deadline, so it front-loads the highest-value, hardest-to-fake skill (live audit) and deletes anything that takes longer than a day to build (no new portfolio pieces, no take-home-style projects — not demoted, deleted: you don't have the runway).

**Day 1 (Sat) — Orient + bank stories**
- Resolve the B2B/B2C question above.
- Bank 4 behavioral stories (ownership, conflict/stakeholder pushback, a campaign failure, a result tied to a number) in three lengths each: 2 min, 60 sec, one line. Use only evidence you actually have — never invented figures.
- Write your "campaign I killed and why" story now — it's asked almost every senior loop and is easy to leave unprepared.

**Day 2 (Sun) — Unit-economics fluency**
- Drill the funnel chain out loud, both directions, until fluent: impressions → CTR → clicks → CPC → spend → conversion rate → conversions → CPA → AOV/ACV → revenue → margin → profit.
- Fintech-specific: be ready to swap AOV/ACV for whatever this company's real unit is — funded account, approved application, activated card, first transaction. Ask in the interview if unclear; naming the right unit metric reads senior.
- Learn cold: **MER (total revenue ÷ total marketing spend) vs. platform ROAS**, and why platform ROAS is structurally inflated (every platform claims credit for the same conversion). This is one of the two questions that decides the hire — see Scorecard below.

**Day 3 (Mon) — Live-audit structure, rehearsal 1**
- This is the room's signature round. Rehearse the 5-part spoken structure (below) on a real or example account — your own, a public one, or an anonymized past account. Time yourself: ~10 minutes total.
- The single most-cited failure mode in verified interviews: reading a generic tool-generated audit aloud. Bring a point of view, not a checklist.

**Day 4 (Tue) — Metric-diagnosis scenarios**
- Drill the ROAS-drop diagnostic order until it's automatic (see Scorecard — it's the other filter). Then run these verified prompts against it or against a general answer structure:
  - "High CTR, low conversion rate — why?"
  - "Campaign not delivering impressions — why?"
  - "A/B test shows 5% lift at p=0.08 — what do you do?" (Answer with a decision frame — cost of being wrong each direction, not "significant/not significant.")
  - "What would you do with a $50K/month budget?"
  - "I doubled the budget and CPM nearly doubled with much lower ROAS — why?" (Answer: audience saturation / diminishing marginal returns.)
- B2B branch: prep "how would you structure lead-gen targeting for decision-makers" and cost-per-SQL reasoning.
- B2C branch: prep "ad fatigue is hurting performance — what do you do" and CAC/LTV reasoning.

**Day 5 (Wed) — Live-audit rehearsal 2 + fintech friction**
- Second timed rehearsal, fresh account, ideally one that resembles a lending/neobank/payments funnel if you can find a public one.
- Add the fintech-specific layer interviewers will expect you to know unprompted: financial-services ad policy restrictions (Meta's Special Ad Category limits targeting on credit/finance products), APR/disclosure requirements in ad copy, and that compliance review slows creative-testing velocity compared to DTC. Naming this unprompted signals you've actually operated in the vertical.
- Prep your NDA-safe case study if you have client/employer work to reference: anonymize the company (descriptor, not name), use indexed/percentage figures only, no raw screenshots. If everything is under NDA, say so and lead with reasoning instead — don't over-share to compensate; interviewers read leaked data as a preview of how you'd treat theirs.

**Day 6 (Thu) — Light review only**
- Re-read your story cards and the diagnostic order once. No new material — cramming past readiness doesn't move the round, it just raises fatigue risk for Friday.

## The Scorecard: What They're Actually Grading

Interview panels in this field score judgment over recall — "never cite best practices as your rationale" is the standing advice from practitioners who build these loops. The strong answer pattern is always **"it depends — here's my reasoning,"** never a memorized rule. Below is the competency breakdown a structured panel uses, with what weak/mid/strong looks like at each. One published vendor rubric (Pare, labelled as vendor content, not universal practice) weights this Technical 40 / Communication 30 / Commercial insight 30 — i.e., 60% of your score is *how you reason and communicate*, not what you know.

| Competency | Weak signal | Strong signal | Where it's graded |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diagnostic judgment** | Jumps to "increase budget" or a single guess | Works a fixed order: measurement → media → creative → post-click; states confidence and what data would confirm it | Live audit + metric-diagnosis round |
| **Business/unit economics** | Cites platform ROAS uncritically | Translates to MER and break-even ROAS; names acquisition MER as the senior nuance; uses the *company's* real unit metric, not AOV by default | Live audit + case questions |
| **Measurement/attribution literacy** | Treats correlation as incrementality | Distinguishes claimed vs. incremental revenue; knows what a geo-holdout or lift test is for | Follow-up probes on any performance claim |
| **Creative & testing strategy** | Generic "test more creative" | Names hook/hold rate, frequency, fatigue signals, and a testing cadence | Metric-diagnosis, live audit |
| **Ownership under ambiguity** | Vague or inflated claims about impact | Precise about what you owned vs. influenced; a real "campaign I killed" story with a number attached | Behavioral round |
| **Communication** | Jargon without translating to business impact | Explains a metric to a non-technical stakeholder in one clean sentence | Every round, graded continuously |

**The two questions that decide the hire** (asked in some form almost certainly — these carry more weight than everything else combined):

1. **"Walk me through diagnosing a ROAS drop."** Answer in this fixed order, out loud: measurement (tracking/dedup, platform-vs-backend gap) → media (CPM, frequency, audience saturation) → creative (hook rate, fatigue) → post-click (landing page, conversion rate). The tell interviewers listen for: *low ROAS with flat backend sales = tracking/attribution problem; low ROAS with genuinely falling backend sales = real underperformance.* Get that distinction right and you clear the bar most candidates miss.
2. **"How do you think about ROAS vs. the business number?"** Answer with MER and break-even ROAS, not platform ROAS. This is the line between "fluent in the tool" and "understands the business" — the dominant documented mis-hire in this field is exactly the candidate who's great with the platform and lost the moment performance drops.

**Lower-weight but real filters, usually asked casually — just answer honestly, don't perform:**
- An "exaggerator probe" ("who do you admire in this field and why," "what do you read to stay current") — designed to catch inflated experience claims; answer plainly.
- A commitment/tenure question ("where are you in 5 years") — predicts retention, not skill; low-weight, don't over-invest prep time here.

**The live-audit spoken structure to actually rehearse** (~10 minutes, this is the format the round will run in):
1. **Orient (1 min)** — business model, conversion action, what "good" means here, before touching any setting.
2. **Money first (3 min)** — where spend concentrates, whether top spenders hit target CPA/ROAS, the single biggest waste line.
3. **Structure and signal (3 min)** — campaign/ad-group logic, tracking sanity, bidding fit to the goal.
4. **Prioritized recommendation (2 min)** — top 3 changes, ranked by expected impact, each with the metric that would prove it worked.
5. **What you'd need next (1 min)** — the data you can't see and would request. This reads as rigor, not a gap.

**Fastest way to lose points, independent of skill level:**
- Reading a tool-generated audit aloud instead of a point of view.
- Citing "best practices" instead of reasoning from context.
- Talking CTR/CPC without ever landing on CPA, ROAS/MER, or CAC — reads as mid-level regardless of years of experience.
- Overclaiming ownership you can't back with a specific action and result.
- Certifications as your main credibility claim — they clear ATS screens, they don't move a hiring decision; don't lean on them if asked.
