## Verdict: Don't fire them yet. The evidence you have doesn't support the decision — but two real problems exist, and they're yours to fix, not theirs.

### The timeline math your co-founder isn't doing

9 weeks elapsed. Actual usable time:

- **Weeks 1–2**: no Meta access. Zero campaign time possible, through no fault of the manager.
- **~4 weeks**: rebuilding conversion tracking.
- **~2–3 weeks**: two test campaigns.

That leaves roughly **2–3 weeks of actual paid campaign data** — likely without even exiting Meta's learning phase, which needs ~50 conversions per ad set per week to stabilize. Two "inconclusive" tests in that window isn't a red flag. It's the statistically expected outcome of a sample size that small. Judging a media hire on this is like firing a new sales rep after two cold calls.

### Reframe each fact

| Fact cited                         | What it actually means                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| No admin access until week 3       | Your handover failure, not theirs. This alone erased a third of their tenure.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| You get pinged for everything      | You never defined a spend/change approval threshold. Absent that, "ask first" is the _correct_ default for a new hire on someone else's ad account — not a sign they can't operate independently.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Two inconclusive tests             | Expected result given insufficient runway and sample size. Not evidence of skill or effort.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Month spent on conversion tracking | This is the correct move, not stalling. If tracking is broken, campaign data — including your two "inconclusive" tests — is unreliable regardless of what it shows. Optimizing spend on broken tracking is worse than spending nothing: Meta's algorithm optimizes against whatever signal it gets, garbage or not. Auditing and fixing tracking before scaling budget is standard practice for anyone taking over an account, not a delay tactic. |

"Revenue is what matters" is true — and precisely why fixing tracking first was right. You can't trust revenue attribution from a broken pixel/CAPI setup. Demanding revenue proof before the measurement system works is asking them to show you a number you already can't trust.

### What's actually broken (fix regardless of who stays)

1. **No decision rights.** Write down what they can change without sign-off — daily budget shifts under $X, audience/creative testing, bid strategy — and what needs approval. This is a 30-minute conversation you've been skipping for 9 weeks.
2. **No ramp-time expectations set.** A good hire proactively tells you "expect no clean signal for 4–6 weeks while I fix tracking and get through the learning phase." If they didn't say that, that's the real accountability gap — not the work itself, but failing to manage your patience. Worth raising directly with them.

### Recommended path

- Don't terminate now — you'd be firing before you've given them a fair chance to be measured, and you'd eat the sunk cost (ramp time, tracking rebuild, agency knowledge transfer) with the next hire too.
- Set a **explicit 30-day checkpoint** (day 40) with agreed success metrics — CAC/ROAS targets now that tracking is fixed, minimum spend to reach significance, number of tests run.
- Fix the decision-rights gap this week, in writing.
- Ask them directly for a written plan: what they're testing next, expected timeline to signal, what they need from you to move faster.

### What would flip my verdict

- They can't produce a clear, specific account of what the tracking rebuild involved (vague answers = red flag for competence, not workload).
- No proactive roadmap or communication about ramp time — you've had to drag every update out of them.
- At the day-40 checkpoint, campaigns are still inconclusive _despite_ adequate spend and working tracking.

Right now you have a governance problem and an impatience problem, not a performance problem.
