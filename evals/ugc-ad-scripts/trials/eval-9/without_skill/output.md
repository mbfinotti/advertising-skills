No — the plan under-uses your two richest sources and over-invests in your thinnest one.

**The problem**

- 4 reviews is not a corpus. A week scraping review sites for volume that doesn't exist is a week misspent.
- Public reviews are written in review-speak — polished, hedged, retrospective. Creator-style ads need spoken, raw language. That's not where it lives.
- 800 tagged tickets and a call-recording library are unscripted, at-scale, and already partially structured. That's your actual voice-of-customer.
- One concept drafted before you've mapped the pain-point landscape is a guess, not a synthesis.

**Where the real signal is, ranked**

1. Sales calls — prospects describing pain and relief in their own words, live. Closest thing you have to a testimonial script.
2. Support tickets — 800 tagged, at scale, shows frequency and intensity per pain point. Use the tags to cluster before reading.
3. Ad comments (~12) — small, but free validation of what already resonates or gets pushback publicly.
4. Public reviews — last, for polish quotes and to sanity-check what surfaces elsewhere.

**Revised plan for the week**

1. Day 1 — Pull ticket tags, rank by volume, sample transcripts inside top 5-6 clusters. Extract verbatim phrases (not paraphrases).
2. Day 2-3 — Sample 15-20 sales calls, weighted toward closed-won and closed-lost. Log verbatim objections, pain descriptions, and the moment a prospect reacts to the pitch.
3. Day 4 — Skim the 4 reviews and 12 ad comments in an hour. Fold anything that confirms or contradicts what you found.
4. Day 4-5 — Cluster everything into 3-5 distinct pain/angle groups (e.g., "audit panic before board meetings," "manual review burning analyst hours"), each with 5-10 real quotes.
5. Day 5 — Draft one concept per angle (3-5 short concepts), not one. Creator ads live or die on which pain hits — you won't know which without testing more than one.

**Why this matters**: scraping is busywork against a near-empty well; the tickets and calls are the actual customers talking, already sitting there.
