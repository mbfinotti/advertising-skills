# Vantoria — UGC Ad Script Package

_3 concepts × 4 hooks × 2 CTAs, 15–20s, US market, direct purchase ($39/bottle), gifted-product creators_

## ⚠️ Compliance flags — read before filming

Three elements in the brief trip rules in this category (dietary supplement, US-only) and need a rewrite, not a straight port. I've built the scripts around the compliant versions below; the literal requested lines are named so you can see what changed.

1. **"My sleep score went from 61 to 89 in three weeks" as the main hook — cut, not softened.** Two separate problems: (a) it's a specific numeric health-style metric moving from one figure to another, the exact pattern FTC-adjacent ad review routinely rejects outright regardless of wording; (b) it came in over email, not a public review — using a named customer's private words needs their consent first, and none is on file. I did not invent a workaround number. Instead, Concept C rebuilds the same "I can actually see it improving" motivation without ever stating a figure, using the _creator's own_ real tracker data once they've genuinely used the product.
2. **"Do you struggle with insomnia?" straight to camera — rejected pattern.** Second-person questions naming a diagnosable condition ("Do you have X?") are a standard rejection trigger. I rewrote every hook to describe the _experience_ (3am, ceiling, phone scrolling) instead of naming or asking about the condition. "Insomnia" doesn't appear in any spoken or on-screen line.
3. **Before/after contrast (exhausted-at-3am vs. glowing-in-the-morning) — deleted as a hook archetype.** Health-category comparisons are the one archetype this account can't run as a proof claim, and body-focused "glow" imagery specifically is restricted here too. Naming the deletion rather than quietly dropping it: the dramatic contrast survives only as a soft narrative beat in Concept C's body (tired → clear-headed, described in words, not staged as a split-screen transformation shot). Promote a real before/after only after legal clears the specific visual treatment.

General notes: dietary supplements can't carry disease claims ("cures/treats insomnia") — every line below describes what the creator personally experienced, never a diagnosis or a cure. Free product = a material connection under FTC rules; every script discloses it early and in plain language, plus the platform's paid-partnership label.

## Compliance (applies to all 3 scripts)

1. **Markets:** US only → FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR 255) disclosure; no EU/France labeling needed.
2. **Material connection:** free product from Vantoria, disclosed spoken + on-screen ("gifted by Vantoria") in beat 2 of every script, plus the platform's paid-partnership toggle used in addition (not instead of) the script line.
3. **Performer status:** creators are not the marketplace reviewers — scripts never claim the creator personally wrote quote #1 or #2; those are shown as someone else's public review (hook 4 in A and B) or paraphrased as the creator's own separate opinion, never misattributed.
4. **Every quote traced to real evidence:** quotes 1 and 2, yes (public reviews, dated). Quote 3, no — cut per flag #1; not used verbatim anywhere.
5. **Result claims:** no specific numeric health/tracker figure is scripted for anyone; Concept C's number (if any) is whatever the creator's real screen shows, filmed after the fact.
6. **Health/efficacy claims:** no disease claims (cure/treat/prevent insomnia); "insomnia" doesn't appear in any script. All claims are first-person, structure/function framed ("helps me wind down").
7. **Regulated category:** dietary supplement — flag this whole package for your own legal/regulatory review before filming, per usual practice for the category; this is not a substitute for that review.

## Measurement plan

Single variable under test: hook archetype (4 per concept), with body/CTA held fixed. Pull your account's trailing median hook rate and hold rate for this placement before launch — that's the pass bar, not a published benchmark.

- Low hook rate → the openings aren't landing; rotate in the untested tier-2 archetypes (question, first-person confession) next round.
- Healthy hook, low hold → check the hook→body transition line first (it should extend the specific hook's premise, not the generic body).
- Healthy hold, low CTR → test the 2 CTA variants against each other.
- Healthy CTR, low conversion → that's the $39/US-only offer or landing page, not the script.

**Sequencing:** ship A and B immediately — both are fully grounded and don't need anything you don't already have. Hold C's hook 4 until a cast creator has genuinely tracked three real weeks; run C's hooks 1–3 in the meantime since they don't depend on that asset.

## Before this goes to a creator

Run every spoken line and on-screen text through your humanizer pass — this draft is unshot and will read as scripted until it does. Get usage-rights sign-off from the creator before filming. And don't skip item 7 above: supplements are exactly the category where a script that looks fine on the page can still get flagged in ad review or draw regulatory attention if a claim slips past legal.
