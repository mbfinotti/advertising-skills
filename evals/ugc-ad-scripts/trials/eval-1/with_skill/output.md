# Brewlane — 2 UGC Concepts (20s, vertical, busy commuters)

## Before the scripts — 3 flags

**1. I did not write a fabricated "happy customer" testimonial.** The skill this is built on is explicit: _"Never invent a hook, pain point, claim, or testimonial line. Fabricated reviews and testimonials are a regulatory violation, not just weak writing"_ — and under the 2024 FTC fake-reviews rule this carries real per-violation penalties, not just a style ding. You have zero reviews and 40 boxes shipped a week ago — there is no "happy customer" to quote yet.

What I did instead: since your creator is a named, disclosed person (not an invented "Sarah from Ohio"), the line "this changed my mornings" is scripted as **her own first-person reflection**, to be said only if it's genuinely true for her — not locked as guaranteed copy. That's the compliant version of what you're asking for. Once real customers exist, swap this for an actual sourced quote and the package upgrades out of `UNVALIDATED`.

**2. Validation status: `UNVALIDATED — voice-of-customer not sourced.`** Every line below is written from your brief, not from mined reviews (none exist). Once you have replies from those first 40 customers, feed me the quotes and I'll rewrite the hooks in their actual words — that's the single highest-leverage next step for this account.

**3. Assumed defaults**, since they weren't specified — flag if wrong: market = US/English (confirm before shipping to EU/France, which need translated "publicité"/"collaboration commerciale" labels); platform = generic vertical feed (TikTok/Reels/Shorts — hook-rate thresholds differ slightly by platform, doesn't change the script). Also: **the free 3-month subscription is a material connection and must be disclosed in-video**, not just in a caption — I've written that in.

Archetypes **deleted** from this round, named per the skill's rule so they don't get silently dropped: **proof-first and bold claim** — you have no documented result to show or defend yet (product launched last week). **Before/after contrast deferred** (not banned, just not worth the shoot cost on a first flight with no data). ## Compliance (run before filming, both concepts)

1. **Material connection**: creator received a free 3-month subscription → disclosed spoken _and_ on-screen in beat 1 of both scripts, plus the platform's paid-partnership/gifted label. Don't rely on the platform toggle alone.
2. **No fabricated testimonial**: the "changed my mornings" line is the disclosed creator's own stated opinion, not attributed to an unnamed third party. Confirm with her before filming that it's genuinely how she feels — if not, replace it, don't force it.
3. **No health/efficacy claims**: nothing about energy, focus, or health benefits — unsubstantiated for a caffeinated product and not needed for this angle.
4. **No fake urgency**: neither CTA uses a false deadline.
5. **Subscription disclosure** (not in the skill's checklist, but relevant given ROSCA/negative-option rules on recurring billing): make sure the $24/month is understood as recurring, and your landing page — not the video — clearly states cancellation terms. The video doesn't need a legal disclaimer, but don't let the CTA imply a one-time purchase.
6. **Markets**: written for US/English. If this runs in the EU or France, add the market-specific "publicité"/"collaboration commerciale" label in French, visible throughout — confirm before I add that language.

## Measurement plan

No historical baseline exists — this is a brand-new account, so **this flight sets the baseline** rather than beating one. Single variable under test within each concept: hook archetype (body and CTA held fixed). Concept A vs. B additionally tests which pain point (time-pressure-at-home vs. skipping-the-coffee-run) resonates more — treat that as a second, coarser read, not a clean single-variable test. Diagnose in sequence once data lands: low hook rate → rewrite hooks; healthy hook rate/low hold → fix the hook-to-body transition; healthy hold/low CTR → the CTA is unclear; healthy CTR/low conversion → it's the $24 price or checkout page, not the script.
