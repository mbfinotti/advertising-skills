## Reality check on the numbers first

Two of your framings don't hold up when you look at what the metrics actually measure:

**🔴 "We're crushing the top with 31% hook rate" — no.** A 30%+ benchmark from an external report is a generic cross-industry number. Your own trailing median is 28%. You're 3pts above _your own_ baseline — a modest move, not "crushing the top." Cross-report benchmarks vary wildly by vertical, format, and platform mix, so they're weak evidence next to your own history. Before reading anything into it: how many days/impressions is "this month"? A 3pt move on a thin sample is noise.

**🔴 "Hooks must be getting tired" — contradicts your own data.** Creative fatigue shows up as _declining_ hook rate (people recognize and skip). Yours went _up_. The thing that's actually broken is hold rate: 18% vs a 35% median — nearly cut in half. That's the headline, and it's being buried under the hook-rate story. Something between second 3 and second 15 is failing — payoff, pacing, offer reveal, or the audience watching isn't who the ad is for.

**⚠️ "TikTok hooks are way better than Reels (31% vs 19%)" — likely a confound, not a creative signal.** Hook rate is heavily shaped by platform mechanics, not just the first 3 seconds of your video: autoplay/sound defaults, feed vs. FYP context, competing UI, and even slightly different definitions of a "3-second play" between Meta and TikTok. Comparing the same creative's hook rate _across_ platforms as if it's an apples-to-apples quality score is misleading. The right comparison is each platform against **its own** trailing median — is 19% low _for Reels_, and is 31% actually strong _for TikTok_? You don't know that yet.

## What's really going on (most likely story)

Hook rate up + hold rate collapsed usually means one of:

1. **Hook/body mismatch** — the hook is more curiosity-driven or novel now, but the body doesn't deliver on what it promised, so people bail once they realize it.
2. **Reach quality shift** — you're pulling in more low-intent/colder viewers who click into the hook but aren't your buyer, so they drop before 15s. This would also explain why CTR is "steady" in aggregate — a smaller qualified segment may be propping it up while overall engaged attention falls. Worth splitting CTR by whether the viewer hit 15s or not.
3. **Structural change in the ad** — reveal (product, price, offer) moved later, pacing slowed, or the body got longer/more generic.

## Check before you touch anything (in order)

- 🎯 Sample size behind the 31%/28% and 18%/35% deltas — are these real or noise?
- 🎯 Hook and hold rate **per platform vs. that platform's own historical median** (not against each other)
- 🎯 Drop-off curve between 3s–15s: cliff at one point, or gradual bleed?
- 🎯 CTR computed on full impressions vs. on 3s+/15s+ viewers only — is "steady" hiding a shift?
- 🎯 Did anything change in the video structure/edit recently (reveal timing, length, VO, captions)?
- 🎯 Is the hold drop concentrated in specific audiences (broad/Advantage+ vs. retargeting) or specific creatives, or is it uniform?
- 🎯 Frequency — are impressions being pushed toward already-fatigued audiences via automatic bid/placement expansion?

## Should you broaden targeting?

**Not yet.** The logic "hook rate is elite, so let's get more eyeballs" skips the fact that hold rate — the signal for whether those eyeballs are the right eyeballs — just collapsed. Broadening now risks scaling the exact problem you haven't diagnosed: more people getting hooked, fewer sticking around, likely worse CPA once it hits CVR downstream.

Sequence it instead:

1. Diagnose the hold-rate drop (checklist above) this week.
2. If it's a **creative/messaging mismatch** → fix the body/payoff before spending more on reach.
3. If hold and downstream CVR are actually fine on a specific segment (e.g., TikTok only, or one age/interest cluster) → run a small, controlled expansion test (10–15% of budget into a lookalike/interest broaden) rather than a global broaden, and read hold rate + CVR, not hook rate, as the gate metric.

## 10 fresh hooks

I don't have your specific product details (flavor lineup, hero ingredient, price point, USP), so these are built on standard DTC dog-treats angles — swap in your actual specifics. Given the hold-rate problem, each hook is written to set up a payoff the video can actually deliver in the next 12 seconds — don't let the edit promise more than the body follows through on, that's likely what's causing the bailout.

1. **POV/relatable:** "POV: your dog just did the thing that made you switch treats forever." _(sets up: show the exact moment/behavior, then the treat)_
2. **Pattern interrupt, visual:** Close-up on a treat literally crumbling/being torn apart in slow motion — "This is what a treat with real [ingredient] actually looks like." _(sets up: ingredient reveal)_
3. **Myth-bust:** "Most 'natural' dog treats aren't. Here's how to check yours in 5 seconds." _(sets up: label-reading demo, ends on your product passing)_
4. **Problem-agitate:** "If your dog has itchy paws or a sensitive stomach, it might be the treats — not the food." _(sets up: ingredient/allergen angle)_
5. **Dog reaction, cold open:** Start mid-reaction — dog going wild for the treat, no context yet — "Wait for it..." then reveal why. _(sets up: taste test or ingredient story)_
6. **Vet/expert framing:** "A vet told me this about dog treats and I haven't bought the same brand since." _(sets up: the specific claim, tie to your formulation)_
7. **Before/after:** "3 weeks of switching my dog's treats — here's what changed." _(sets up: coat, energy, breath, whatever's true and provable)_
8. **Cost/value reframe:** "I did the math on treats per pound of actual meat — most brands are mostly filler." _(sets up: ingredient % comparison)_
9. **Skeptic-to-convert arc:** "I thought treat brands were all the same marketing BS. Then I read the ingredient list on this one." _(sets up: label walk-through)_
10. **Micro-story/stakes:** "My dog wouldn't eat ANY treat until we found this one — here's what finally worked." _(sets up: picky-eater proof, texture/smell cues on camera)_

Production note: keep the promise made in seconds 0–3 narrow and specific (one claim, one visual payoff), and deliver on it by second 8–10 — that's the fix for a 35%→18% hold-rate collapse, more than any new hook copy will be on its own.
