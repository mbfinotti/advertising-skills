# No, you don't owe B2B's patience — but keep one piece of it

That playbook is built for a different funnel and you shouldn't import it wholesale.

## Why the playbook doesn't fit Amberpetal

- **Trust-layer-first** solves a B2B problem: nobody buys $50k software from a founder they saw once. You don't have that problem — Lucia's videos _are_ the trust layer, and it's already free and organic.
- **No conversion objective** exists in B2B thought-leader ads because there's no reliable purchase signal to optimize toward (long, multi-stakeholder cycles). At $42 AOV with real volume, Meta/TikTok's algorithm gets enough purchase events per day to optimize directly — withholding the conversion objective just starves it of the exact data it needs.
- **Ignore CTR** makes sense when the ad's job is brand impression, not click. Yours isn't — you're running paid amplification of content designed to sell a $42 product.
- **Multi-quarter payback** is a function of high LTV and long sales cycles. DTC haircare payback should be measured in days-to-weeks, not quarters, unless your model depends heavily on repeat/subscription LTV.

## What to do instead

1. **Objective**: run conversion campaigns (Meta Advantage+ Shopping, TikTok conversion) from day one, spark-ad/whitelist the organic winners under Lucia's handle.
2. **Judge fast, on real numbers**: hook rate (3s), CTR, CAC, and blended payback period — target payback inside one purchase cycle, not quarters. Kill or iterate weekly, not quarterly.
3. **Still track one slow metric in parallel**: branded search volume, direct traffic, repeat-purchase rate. Review it monthly/quarterly as a secondary health check — but never let it gate spend or creative decisions. That's the one thing worth keeping from the playbook: don't over-optimize short-term CAC into a brand that has no memorability.

## Rights: lock this down before you scale paid spend on her content

Contractor status means copyright does **not** automatically transfer to you. Work-for-hire only applies without a contract if the work fits one of nine narrow statutory categories — video content usually doesn't. Treat this as a real gap, not a technicality, before you put ad dollars behind her videos.

Get a written **Creator IP Assignment & Usage Rights Agreement** covering:

- **Copyright assignment**, not just a license — "Lucia assigns all right, title, and interest" — plus a belt-and-suspenders work-for-hire clause.
- **Publicity/likeness release** — separate from copyright. Explicit grant to use her name, image, voice, and likeness in marketing and _paid advertising_, worldwide, across current and future platforms.
- ⚠️ **Paid usage is a separate right from organic posting** in most creator norms — many creators charge extra for ad whitelisting. Negotiate this explicitly (flat fee, rev-share, or included in her base comp) rather than assuming organic permission covers it.
- **Term and territory** — perpetual/irrevocable if you want to keep running evergreen spark ads; otherwise define renewal explicitly. Also define what happens to _already-running_ ads if the relationship ends.
- **Platform ad-permission mechanics** — TikTok Spark Ads and Instagram Partnership Ads require Lucia to actively generate a code / toggle permission in-app, independent of your contract. Make this a contractual obligation with a deadline ("provide Spark Ads code within 5 days of posting"), not just an assumption.
- ⚠️ **Trending sounds** — organic virality often rides on music not cleared for commercial/paid use. Audit before whitelisting; swap to licensed audio if needed.
- **Exclusivity** (optional) — decide if you want to restrict her from making similar UGC for competing haircare brands while under contract.

Get a lawyer to draft this rather than relying on an informal understanding — the whole point of scaling paid spend on her content is that the stakes just went up.
