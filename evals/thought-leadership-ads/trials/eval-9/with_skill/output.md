## Short answer to both questions

**No, you don't wait quarters.** The playbook you read describes LinkedIn Thought Leader Ads specifically. That product structurally cannot carry a conversion objective — no lead form, no purchase objective, no link in the ad itself. That's *why* it needs a trust-layer-then-retarget sequence and a multi-quarter pipeline verdict: there's no other way to measure it.

Lucia's videos live on TikTok/Instagram. Spark Ads (TikTok) and Partnership Ads (Meta) are a different product with the full objective set, including conversions/purchase, and the advertiser controls the CTA and destination. For a $42 AOV DTC brand, this collapses to a single direct-response campaign from day one — no forced trust layer, no structural handoff, no CTR taboo (the CTR warning is about an engagement-objective denominator that doesn't exist on a conversion campaign). Judge it on pixel CPA/ROAS on a short window, same as any other paid social channel.

**Rights: treat her as a licensor, not an approver with a click-button.** A platform authorization code is not a commercial agreement, and "contractor" cuts against you here specifically: for an employee, work made in the course of employment is normally the company's by default; for a contractor, copyright defaults to *her* unless a written agreement assigns or licenses it. Absent that, she owns the videos and can pull authorization any time. Fix it with a written agreement before you spend another dollar amplifying her content — details below.

## Campaign plan — Amberpetal, Lucia founder-fronted ads

**Open items I'm flagging, not assuming** (fill these in before launch): monthly budget for this format, target audience size, whether Lucia has already agreed to paid promotion of specific videos, whether any written agreement exists today, EEA/UK audience exposure, and a launch deadline. Reasonable defaults are used below where the answer doesn't change the shape of the plan.

### 1. Roster and consent

- **Roster: Lucia only, for now.** Flag as a single-person program — the skill's governance rule applies regardless of platform: one revocation, dispute, or departure ends the whole thing. Mitigate over time by adding a second creator or UGC customer once one exists; don't block launch on it.
- **Consent required per video, in writing, before spend** — not "she'll probably say yes," not a hallway nod. TikTok's authorization code and Meta's partnership code are the *technical* mechanism, not the *commercial* agreement.

### 2. The rights problem — what "contractor" actually changes

Because she's a 1099/contractor and not an employee, the content she films is presumptively hers unless your agreement says otherwise. Given how central her videos are to the brand, don't settle for the minimum "usage license" — negotiate an **assignment of rights** (or a broad, brand-favorable perpetual license as a fallback) in her contractor agreement, covering:

- **Ownership/license scope**: assignment of IP in videos made for or featuring Amberpetal, or at minimum an irrevocable license covering paid amplification, whitelisting, and repurposing across channels.
- **Term**: TikTok/Meta authorization is per-video with a fixed duration (7/30/60/365 days) — pick 365 days by default and put renewal on a calendar; this is a licence-with-expiry to manage, not a revocable toggle.
- **Usage fee** (if any, separate from her founder comp/equity).
- **Spend caps** and **who may edit or pull a video from rotation**.
- **Pause/revocation handling**: what happens contractually if she wants a video pulled mid-flight (distinct from the platform-level authorization).
- **Disclosure obligations**: "paid partnership" label plus her visible founder role — required regardless of employment status, because the brand is paying to amplify a post about itself (material connection).
- **What happens if she leaves or the relationship ends**: define whether existing authorizations/licenses survive, for how long, and write a takedown-and-replace trigger now, not after it happens.

Do this before the next dollar of spend touches her content — everything below assumes it's signed.

### 3. Post selection

- Pull her trailing videos, rank by engagement rate against **her own median** (saves/shares/watch-through, not follower-count-normalized against anyone else). Promote from the top quartile.
- Default to videos that already popped organically — the existing comments/shares carry into the ad as social proof, and a dead comment thread inherits as dead.
- Let organic surge settle (~1 day past peak) before turning any video into an ad; treat the ad as tied to that specific, unedited video.
- Weight evergreen haircare content (routines, product demos, problem/solution) over one-off trend or news-jacking content — evergreen keeps paying after the trend dies.
- Skip pure product pitches as the majority of the roster; the "genuine content vs. pitch" ratio still matters even in a conversion campaign, because AI/ad-slop framing is what kills this format's credibility (a real person, not a script, is the whole asset).

### 4. Link / CTA

Not a LinkedIn-style problem here — **delete the pinned-comment workaround entirely**, it doesn't apply. Meta and TikTok give the advertiser full control of destination, so put the tracked link/product page directly in the ad (Spark Ads / Partnership Ads let you attach it without touching her original caption). Full attribution, no ask made of her beyond authorization.

### 5. Sequence and objective

- **Single performance campaign, direct-response objective, from day one.** No trust-first/awareness-first choice to make — that's a B2B LinkedIn distinction that doesn't exist here.
- Standard retargeting still applies downstream (cart abandoners, video viewers, engagers) — build those audiences before launch regardless, since captured data is never retroactive.
- Split-test her founder-fronted ad against your brand-account creative as the standing structure, not a one-time check.

### 6. Audience

- Start from **lookalikes of Lucia's own followers/engagers** + haircare/beauty interest layers — not a B2B account list (doesn't apply to a DTC brand).
- Size to budget, not to market: compute expected frequency (budget ÷ CPM ÷ audience size) and shrink the list until frequency is real. Provide the actual monthly budget and I'll size this precisely; until then, treat "audience too big for the budget" as the most common way this fails.
- Exclusion list: existing customers, employees, competitors — build this before launch, it's usually longer than the inclusion list and every impression on it is wasted spend plus wasted credibility.

### 7. Budget and bidding

- Floor for meaningful data: ~$50–100/day per campaign (practitioner-cited, not a target — treat as cohort-dependent).
- Bidding pattern: max delivery for the first 7–14 days to gather cost data, then shift to cost-cap.
- Refresh creative on a 3–7 day short-video cadence, not the slower B2B two-week rotation — TikTok/IG creative fatigues fast.

### 8. Measurement and pass gate (B2C — this is the whole point)

- **No landing-page-CPC gate, no 2–4 quarter pipeline window** — those exist because LinkedIn can't measure a purchase directly. You can.
- Keep the person-fronted ad live only while its **pixel CPA/ROAS is at or below your brand-account creative control**. That's the entire gate.
- Reported CTR still isn't the judge — even in a conversion campaign, judge on CPA/ROAS, because CTR (especially under any engagement-flavored delivery setting) doesn't predict purchases.
- Cap the share of Lucia's total output that's ever "sponsored" — even as founder, an all-ad feed burns the authenticity the whole channel is buying.

### Options deleted, by name, and why

- **Trust-first / awareness-first sequencing** — deleted, not demoted. That choice only exists where the platform can't carry a conversion objective. TikTok/Meta can, so there's no trust layer to sequence.
- **Pinned-first-comment link workaround** — deleted. It's a fix for platforms with no advertiser CTA rights; Meta/TikTok give you the CTA directly.
- **2–4 quarter lagging pipeline verdict, ACV/ICP eligibility bar** — deleted. Those are B2B constructs (pipeline, sales-accepted opportunities, $25k+ ACV justification). A $42 AOV consumer purchase is measured in days, on a pixel.
- **"Never judge on CTR" as an absolute rule** — narrowed, not deleted: the caution is specifically about engagement-objective CTR polluting the denominator. On a conversion objective, judge on CPA/ROAS; CTR just isn't the primary signal either way.
- **YouTube, Reddit** — not applicable regardless of B2B/B2C; neither platform has an equivalent mechanic.
