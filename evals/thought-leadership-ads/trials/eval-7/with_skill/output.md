## Verdict: not ready to launch next week — two eligibility-gate items fail today, and the regulated/EEA-UK context adds requirements neither of your two "approvals" cover.

### Gate 1 — Consent isn't actually secured yet

Both "yes" answers are the wrong kind of yes:

- **Client's DM "go for it"** — informal, not per-post, not written. It's not a licence, has no term, no revocation mechanism, no usage-fee terms, no rights for you to keep running the ad if they go quiet or change their mind.
- **Head of Advisory's platform click-approve** — that's the in-platform switch (revocable instantly, by them, at any time). It is explicitly _not_ a commercial agreement. It says nothing about spend caps, who can edit the live post, or what happens if he leaves the firm (on LinkedIn, departure breaks eligibility — the ad turns off and the creative can't be re-hosted).

**Fix before launch:** a short written agreement per person — usage fee (if any), term (30/60/90 days), spend cap, who may edit the post, pause/revocation handling, disclosure obligations. For the client specifically, add licensed usage rights, since they're not an employee.

### Gate 2 — Compliance review hasn't happened

You're a regulated UK/EU wealth-management platform, so neither post can go into paid distribution on approvals alone:

- **Market commentary post**: this is investment-adjacent content from your Head of Advisory. It needs pre-publication compliance/financial-promotion sign-off _as an ad_, independent of his platform click. Ask your compliance function directly — this is a legal question, not a marketing one, and it should happen before ad spend, not after.
- **Client testimonial**: an unprompted client endorsement of a wealth manager is itself the kind of content regulators have gone after (a 2024 enforcement action fined a firm $850k specifically over unsupervised endorsement posts). It needs the same review, plus a **partnership label** in addition to LinkedIn's paid-promotion label, since the client isn't an employee.
- **Retention**: keep records of both the live post content and the ad creative at time of approval — you need to prove what was actually running if either post gets edited later.

Neither of these clears in a DM or a platform click. Budget the time for an actual compliance pass.

### Disclosure (UK/EEA specific)

- Ads must be obviously identifiable as advertising even though the posters are genuinely connected to you — LinkedIn's paid-promotion label covers the mechanic, but confirm your compliance team is comfortable it satisfies UK/EU ad-identification rules, not just the platform's.
- EEA targeting: group/affinity-based targeting is disabled there — don't plan audience layers that depend on it.
- Sponsored messaging (if you ever add InMail) needs explicit EU consent — not relevant to feed ads, flagging in case it's on the roadmap.

### The link problem (both posts currently have none)

LinkedIn Thought Leader Ads carry **no advertiser-added CTA, headline, or URL** — a link exists only if the author put one in the post. Neither post was written with a link. Options, cheapest ask first:

1. **Pinned first comment with a tracked link** — smallest ask, doesn't touch the approved post, works for both people. Default recommendation here, especially for the client, since you can't assume they'll agree to re-edit a post they wrote unprompted.
2. **Author edits the live post to add a tracked link** — most clicks, but it's a bigger ask, needs another wait on the Head of Advisory's calendar, and — because you're regulated — a second compliance pass on the edited version.
3. **No link, declared in writing as a pool-builder only** — fine as a deliberate choice, but if you pick it, say so explicitly; posting with no link and no such declaration is a documented failure mode.

Decide and instrument this before launch — "people will find us" isn't a plan.

### Recency check

Both posts already published organically. Best practice is to launch roughly a day past each post's organic engagement peak, once the surge has settled — not immediately on publish, and not stale by the time you actually launch. Confirm neither post is now old enough that its commentary reads as dated, or has a dead comment thread (a dead thread promotes into the ad as a dead thread).

### Format eligibility

LinkedIn TLA accepts single image, single video, text-only, article, newsletter, or event posts. It rejects carousels, polls, document posts, reshares, and celebration posts. Confirm both posts are eligible formats — mark this "verify in platform," since LinkedIn's own documentation here is inconsistent and changes often.

### Portfolio risk

You have exactly two featured people. One revocation, one departure, or one compliance objection ends half your program instantly, and — because there's no third post queued — could end all of it. Recommend identifying at least one more candidate post (another advisor, another client) now, even if it launches a week later, so the program survives a single person changing their mind.
