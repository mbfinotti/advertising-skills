Sponsoring these two posts is a materially different regulatory act than letting them sit organically. Here's what's actually missing before you can launch.

## 🔴 The core issue: paid ≠ organic, legally

Once you pay to promote a post, Sterret & Vail is no longer a passive channel — you are **causing a financial promotion to be communicated**. Under FSMA s21 (UK) and the equivalent MiFID II / national marketing-communication regimes across the EU, that shifts full compliance responsibility onto the firm, regardless of who originally wrote the words.

- A DM saying "go for it" and a click on "approve" inside a social scheduling tool are **not** financial promotion sign-off.
- Real sign-off requires: a person with appropriate authority and competence (not the content's own author — self-approval is a conflict of interest), reviewing the **exact final creative** (copy + image + link + any ad-unit framing), confirming it's fair, clear, not misleading, and includes the required risk disclosures — with that review **dated, named, and retained** (SYSC 9 / COBS record-keeping, typically 5 years).
- If "the platform" is just a LinkedIn scheduler's internal approval workflow, that's a marketing sign-off, not a compliance one. You need a second, independent compliance/legal review specifically for financial promotion purposes.

## ⚠️ Post 1 — Head of Advisory's market commentary

- Commentary from an advisory head that touches markets/investments is very likely to be classified as a financial promotion (or investment research) in its own right, not just an "opinion."
- Check it carries: a balanced risk section (not just upside), a past-performance disclaimer if any returns/markets data is cited, and a conflicts-of-interest disclosure if the firm or the individual holds relevant positions.
- Him "approving" his own post doesn't satisfy the independent-approver requirement — get a second compliance sign-off.

## ⚠️ Post 2 — client testimonial

- Unprompted and enthusiastic doesn't exempt it. The moment you pay to amplify it, it becomes your marketing content and inherits full financial promotion rules.
- Testimonials referencing performance or outcomes are tightly restricted — check it isn't implying typical/guaranteed returns from one client's experience. If it references specific results, you likely need an added disclaimer ("individual experience, not representative, capital at risk, past performance ≠ future results").
- Several EU regulators (e.g. Germany's BaFin, France's AMF) restrict or heavily condition the use of client testimonials in investment-product marketing — this can vary by member state, so "go for it" from the client doesn't clear those bars.
- Consent gap: a DM is not GDPR-adequate consent for identifiable personal data used in a paid, targeted, indefinite ad campaign. You need written, specific consent covering: that it's paid promotion, expected duration, geographic targeting, and an easy right to withdraw — plus confirmation they're comfortable with LinkedIn's ad targeting/analytics processing their name and likeness.

## 🌍 UK/EU cross-border exposure

- Sponsored reach is not organic reach — you're actively targeting audiences, which raises marketing-passport/local-licensing questions in every EU country the ad could serve into. Post-Brexit, there's no blanket passporting; each member state's local financial promotion/marketing rules can differ (language requirements, testimonial restrictions, risk-warning wording).
- Geo-fence the LinkedIn campaign tightly to jurisdictions where you're confirmed authorised to promote, rather than defaulting to broad EU targeting.

## 🔴 LinkedIn-specific blockers

- LinkedIn requires **financial services advertiser verification/certification** in the UK and EU before it will run finance-related ads — this can take days to weeks and is a real risk to a "launch next week" timeline. Confirm this is done now.
- EU Digital Services Act transparency rules require "who paid for this ad" disclosure and ad-repository logging for anyone advertising into the EU — make sure your ad setup satisfies this.

## ✅ Minimum punch list before launch

1. Route both posts (final creative, not draft copy) through a named, independent compliance approver — document the sign-off.
2. Add required risk/performance disclaimers to both posts as they'll appear once sponsored.
3. Replace the client DM with written, specific consent covering paid use, duration, targeting, and withdrawal rights.
4. Confirm LinkedIn financial-services advertiser verification is complete.
5. Geo-fence targeting to jurisdictions you're licensed to promote into; get local counsel to flag any member-state-specific testimonial/promotion restrictions.
6. File the approval records — approver name, date, exact creative reviewed — somewhere retrievable for audit.

Given verification lead times and the need for an independent compliance review, next week is tight but not impossible if you start item 1 and item 4 today.
