# Measurement Framework — LinkedIn Thought Leader Ads

## First, the metric as briefed is wrong — say this before it ships

The VP's target ("beat 2% CTR by week 4, then scale") is sourced from the agency's 2.68% vs 0.42% comparison. That number is the headline claim from **ZenABM's 2026 LinkedIn ABM benchmarks report** (vendor-published, self-serving, 119 sponsored posts / ~$300k spend / 211 companies).

The same report, using the same dataset, found reported CTR **negatively correlated with pipeline** (rho = −0.170). The agency handed the VP the one number their own source shows doesn't predict revenue.

There's also a denominator problem: reported CTR on an engagement objective counts reactions, comments, shares and follows — not just clicks to a destination. A person-fronted ad will beat a brand-page ad on this number almost by construction. It's not that thought leader ads don't work; it's that CTR is the wrong instrument to check it with, and the deck's own headline stat is the proof.

**Recommendation to put to the VP:** keep "beat 2% CTR" as a vanity number to report (agency will ask for it), but replace it as the gate. Lock the real gate on cost per landing-page click and pipeline pool growth instead — below.

## Leading indicators (weekly, diagnostic only)

Ranked by decisions changed per minute spent reading them:

| Rank | Metric | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cost per landing-page click** | The only leading number with a defensible link to pipeline. Never reported CTR. |
| 2 | **Retargeting-pool growth** (engagers captured) | This format's actual product is a warm audience, not the click itself. |
| 3 | Comment volume | Highest-intent engagement signal short of a click. |
| 4 | Account-level engagement (named-account coverage) | Tells you if you're reaching the list, not just generating volume. |
| 5 | Frequency | Diagnoses budget-vs-audience mismatch, not performance. |
| 6 | Engagement rate / reported CTR | Track it for the VP's deck. Do not let it move a decision. It's the closest cousin of the metric this format artificially wins, and it's the one number in the whole report proven to point the wrong way. |

## The real gate: Week 6, not Week 4

Push back on the week-4 timing too — two weeks of live data is inside the learning phase on any paid-social auction; both the platform's own guidance and the practitioner literature treat the first 7–14 days as bid-discovery, not signal. Week 6 is the earliest defensible read.

**Pass only if both hold:**

1. **Cost per landing-page click ≤ $5.80** — your own brand-campaign baseline to this same audience. This beats any published external number (the vendor's own "~$3.06" figure is from the same self-serving report) because it's your account's real data, ranked highest-provenance by definition.
2. **The engager retargeting pool has crossed the downstream platform's minimum serveable audience floor** (commonly ~300 matched members — verify the current threshold before launch). If this isn't crossed, there's no audience to hand off to retargeting regardless of how cheap the clicks were.

**Fail branches:**

- **Fails #1 only** → the post is the problem. Rotate in a different top-quartile post before touching audience or budget.
- **Fails #2 only** → audience/budget is the problem. Widen the list or raise spend toward the frequency floor.
- **Both fail again after one post rotation (~week 10)** → pause the format, return budget to retargeting/direct-response.

## The verdict: 2–4 quarter lagging review

CTR has no seat here either. B2B pipeline from a trust-layer channel takes a sales cycle to show up, not two weeks.

- **Metrics:** influenced pipeline, sales-accepted opportunities from engaged accounts, cost per opportunity.
- **Bar:** a pipeline-to-spend ratio agreed *now*, not at review time — account-based programs commonly plan against 3–5x. **This number isn't in your brief yet — get the VP to commit to one before launch,** or the 2-quarter review has nothing to be judged against and becomes a re-litigation of the CTR argument all over again.

## What this replaces, and why it's worth the VP conversation

Locking "beat 2% CTR by week 4" as the success metric means the campaign gets judged, and possibly killed or scaled, on the one number in the agency's own cited source that moves opposite to revenue. Two quarters from now, a campaign with great CTR and zero pipeline will look like a win on paper and be a loss in the pipeline report — or a campaign with mediocre CTR and real opportunities will get cut early for missing a target that was never predictive. Get the gate changed before week 1, not after week 4's number comes in and someone has to walk back a metric the VP already announced.
