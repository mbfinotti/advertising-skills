# Diagnosis: this isn't a budget problem, it's a reach-math problem

Doubling the budget will double the failure, not fix it. Here's the arithmetic your CEO isn't doing.

## The core issue: 310K audience, $2,400/month

LinkedIn Sponsored Content typically runs ~$30–80 CPM. At a mid-range $50 CPM:

- **$2,400/month → ~48,000 impressions/month**
- Spread across 310,000 people → **~0.15 impressions per person, per month**

That means the average person in your ICP sees the ad _once every 6-7 months_ — nowhere near the 5-7 touches B2B buyers typically need to register a message. Most of your 310K never see it at all; spend concentrates on a small random slice each month, and that slice changes constantly (no frequency buildup, no recall).

**Doubling to $5K/month → ~100,000 impressions → ~0.32 impressions/person/month.** Still far too thin. You'd need something like **10x the budget** to get meaningful frequency (4-6 impressions/month) against an audience that size. That's not a $5K conversation, it's a $24K+/month conversation — and nobody's proposed testing at that scale deliberately.

**The fix is the audience, not the budget.** Shrink the targeting to what your current spend can actually saturate: roughly $2,400/month at $50 CPM covers a real campaign (4-6x frequency) for an audience of **~8,000-12,000 people**, not 310,000. That means:

- Narrow from "full ICP" to actual buying-committee titles (Director+ in Ops/Supply Chain/Logistics, not every logistics professional)
- Or go ABM-style: build a target account list from your ICP and layer seniority on top
- Either way, you're trading reach for frequency — which is the trade you need

## Second issue: check what "results" and objective actually mean

Before touching budget or audience, confirm:

1. **Campaign objective** — boosted COO posts (Thought Leader Ads) are frequently run under Engagement/Brand Awareness objectives, which don't optimize for clicks or conversions at all. If the goal is pipeline, you may be optimizing for the wrong thing entirely.
2. **Is there a CTA and landing page**, or is this pure visibility? A post with no clear next step will show "engagement" but zero attributable leads by design.
3. **What "basically nothing" means** — zero impressions delivered (targeting too narrow after filters), zero clicks (creative/relevance problem), or clicks with zero downstream leads (landing page/offer problem). These require completely different fixes.
4. **Creative fatigue** — 8 weeks of a single voice (COO only) into the same audience segment will fatigue fast. Diversify formats/authors before assuming targeting is the only lever.

## Yes, add exclusions — and you don't need the site-visitor feature for it

Two different LinkedIn capabilities are being conflated:

- **Website Retargeting (Insight Tag-based)** — what your rep said isn't available at your tier. Excludes people who visited your site.
- **Matched Audiences — Company/Contact List Upload** — a separate, generally-available feature (not tier-gated) that lets you upload a list and exclude (or target) by match. **This is what you need.**

Action:

1. Upload your **1,900 customers** (email list or company list) as a Matched Audience → set as an **exclusion** on all cold-ICP campaigns. No reason to pay to "acquire" people who already bought — unless you're deliberately running a separate expansion/cross-sell campaign, in which case target them, don't exclude them.
2. Upload your **40 employees** (by company list or a manual contact list) as a Matched Audience exclusion, or exclude by "Company - current employer" targeting. Prevents internal engagement inflating vanity metrics and wasting spend on people who'll never buy.
3. Go back to your rep and ask specifically about **"Matched Audiences – list exclusion"**, distinct from website retargeting — reps sometimes conflate the two. If genuinely unavailable, this points to an account-type restriction worth escalating, since list-upload exclusions are standard Campaign Manager functionality.

## What to actually tell your CEO

> "Doubling the budget keeps the same audience, so we'd go from showing the ad to 0.15 to 0.3 times per person per month — still invisible. The $2,400 isn't failing because it's too small, it's failing because it's spread across an audience 25-30x too large. Let's cut the audience to ~10K high-fit decision-makers, add customer/employee exclusions, confirm the campaign is actually optimizing for clicks/leads not just engagement, and run that for 3-4 weeks before we talk about more budget. If that converts, budget is the next lever — not before."

## Next 3-4 weeks, concretely

1. **Week 1**: Rebuild audience (narrow to ~8-12K decision-makers), upload customer + employee exclusion lists, confirm/fix campaign objective and CTA.
2. **Weeks 2-4**: Run at the same $2,400/month against the tightened audience. Watch CTR, cost-per-click, and — critically — cost-per-lead/demo, not impressions or engagement.
3. **Decision point**: If CTR and lead cost look healthy at the tighter audience, _then_ scaling budget (to reach adjacent segments or increase frequency) is justified. If they still look bad, the problem is message/offer, not reach — and more budget would only burn faster.
