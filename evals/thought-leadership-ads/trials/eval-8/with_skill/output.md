## Sanity check: the agency's "founder amplification program" — reject as scoped

The core problem isn't the budget or the split, it's the mechanic. Two of the four named channels don't support "sponsor his existing post natively under his identity" at all — this isn't a suboptimal choice, it's not a product that exists.

### Channel-by-channel reality

| Channel | What the pitch claims | What actually exists | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **YouTube** | Run his existing videos as ads under his channel identity | No such mechanic. YouTube ads run from *your* uploaded video, under *your* advertiser account. There is no way to pay to promote someone else's organic upload as if it's still theirs. | **Delete.** Not "suboptimal" — doesn't exist. |
| **Reddit** | Promote his top subreddit answers | No personal-profile-sponsorship product. Reddit's Promoted Posts run as brand-labeled ads, not as "his" answer amplified under his identity. | **Delete.** Same category of error. |
| **X (Twitter)** | Sponsor his posts natively | Amplify exists, but the ad runs **from the advertiser's account** (Denholt Labs), not his. So it's not native to his identity either — it's a different mechanic than what's being pitched. | **Doesn't match the pitch.** Could work as a separate line item, but not as "his posts under his name." |
| **LinkedIn** | Sponsor his posts natively | Thought Leader Ads: the only one of the four that actually does this — his post, his identity, paid-promotion label, revocable consent in-platform. Awareness/engagement/video-views objectives only, no conversion objective, no CTA. | **The only channel that is what's being sold.** |

So the agency sold you a four-channel program where one channel does what's described, one is mislabeled, and two are fictional. An even four-way split of $12k/month ($3k each) is being spent 50% on things that can't be built.

### What's missing before *any* version of this can be planned

Per the eligibility gate for this format, I can't produce a real plan yet — these aren't optional:

1. **Consent.** "Ravi produces great content" isn't consent to sponsor it. Has he explicitly agreed, per-post? Platform click-to-approve isn't a commercial agreement either — you need a written agreement per person covering term, spend caps, who can edit the post, revocation handling, disclosure obligations.
2. **Downstream capture.** Is there a live retargeting/conversion setup today? LinkedIn's version of this format can't carry a conversion objective — it only pays off if engager audiences get created pre-launch and handed to a conversion campaign. If that scaffolding doesn't exist yet, data from month one is unrecoverable.
3. **ACV / sales cycle.** Practitioners cite ~$25k+ ACV with a narrow ICP as the bar for this being worth doing at all (slow payback, 90+ days). Below that, this budget belongs in direct-response/retargeting instead.
4. **Single-person exposure.** The whole roster is Ravi. One revocation, one resignation, and the program (or at least its LinkedIn leg, the only real one) ends instantly and the creative can't be re-hosted. Worth naming as a risk even if you proceed with just him — consider adding a second featured person (another engineer, a customer champion) as insurance.

### If the numbers check out, what a corrected plan looks like

- **LinkedIn Thought Leader Ads** gets the real budget — not $3k, the full amount minus whatever legitimately goes elsewhere. $12k/month against a reasonably narrow ICP account list is closer to the $2k–5k/month "optimal per campaign" band than a quartered $3k.
- **Reddit** gets routed to a standard brand-labeled promoted post (own account, own label) — still worth doing, just not "as Ravi."
- **YouTube** gets routed to a standard video ad campaign, or dropped for this program entirely and left as organic.
- **X** gets clarified with the agency: are they proposing Amplify from Denholt's account (fine, just say so — not "as Ravi") or something else?
- Reddit's top subreddit answers are a legitimate source of *proof* content (screenshot, quote, link back) inside a LinkedIn/brand ad — just not a "sponsor the Reddit post" product.
