## Sanity check: this plan has a real idea buried in it, but as described it has one technical impossibility, one governance gap, and a budget allocation that doesn't match platform economics.

### ❌ Not actually buildable as pitched

- **"Promoting his top subreddit answers" isn't a Reddit ad product.** Reddit Ads can only promote **posts** (submissions) made from an ads-enabled account — you cannot pay to boost a **comment/reply** in someone else's thread. If Ravi's "top answers" are comments, the agency is selling something that doesn't exist on the platform. Push them to clarify: do they mean turning his answers into standalone promoted posts (different, weaker artifact) or something else entirely?

### ⚠️ Access & consent gaps the pitch glosses over

- **"Running his YouTube videos as ads under his channel identity" requires Ravi's explicit delegated access**, not agency credentials. Legit mechanism: YouTube Studio → Permissions, or linking a Google Ads account to his channel — never hand over his password. Same pattern applies to LinkedIn (**Thought Leader Ads**, which specifically requires the individual to authorize the advertiser account — it does not let a company sponsor a personal profile's posts without that opt-in) and X (promoted-tweet access granted per-handle). Confirm the agency's plan uses these official consent flows.
- **Get this in writing from Ravi, not just from you.** It's his face, his account history, his personal reputation on the line across four platforms simultaneously. He should sign off on the specific posts being boosted, not discover it after the fact.

### 🔴 Strategic risk: this can backfire on the exact channels where it matters most

- **Reddit and X audiences are ad-averse and quick to notice paid amplification of "authentic" content.** The value of his organic answers is that they read as unpaid. Once labeled "Promoted"/"Ad," some of that trust evaporates — and once a personal Reddit account has ad history, future organic posts can look tainted too. This is the strongest reason to pilot, not roll out on all four at once.
- **Personal brand built with company money is a retention risk.** If Denholt is funding paid amplification of Ravi-the-person (not Denholt-the-product), you're growing an asset that leaves the building if he does. Fine if you've thought about it — worth thinking about before spending $36k/quarter on it.

### ⚠️ Budget: even split ignores platform economics

$3k/channel/month is not equivalent value across channels:

- **LinkedIn B2B CPMs run $30–80** → $3k buys roughly 40–100k impressions. Thin.
- **Reddit and YouTube CPMs are typically much lower** → $3k goes further, possibly further than the content/inventory can absorb.
- No stated objective (awareness vs. dev signups vs. community trust) means you can't actually judge whether $3k on each is right — allocate by funnel goal and platform cost, not by "fairness."

### ✅ What's legitimately fine

- Paying to distribute an executive's real, attributed content about his own company is **not** an FTC endorsement problem — he's obviously not a disinterested third party, and native ad units auto-label as sponsored. No disclosure gymnastics needed beyond what the platforms already do.
- Boosting organic content that's already resonating is a sound tactic in principle — cheaper than producing new ads, and it's proven content.

### Recommendation

1. Ask the agency for the actual ad mechanism per platform, in writing — reject "subreddit answer boosting" as stated.
2. Pilot on two channels for 60 days (suggest LinkedIn + YouTube — clearest native mechanisms, clearest B2B fit) before committing all four.
3. Get Ravi's written, per-post consent and use official delegated ad access — no shared credentials.
4. Set one KPI per channel before spending a dollar, and size budget to platform CPM/funnel role, not evenly.
5. Skip Reddit paid amplification for now, or restrict it to standalone promoted posts he's comfortable owning — not comment-boosting, which isn't a real product and carries the highest authenticity-backlash risk.
