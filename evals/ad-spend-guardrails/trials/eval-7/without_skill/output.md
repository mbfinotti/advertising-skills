## CAC ceiling: $196/customer

**The math:**
- Gross margin per customer per month = $49 × 80% = **$39.20**
- 5-month payback ceiling = 5 × $39.20 = **$196**

**Where you actually stand:**

## The real takeaway

You're not anywhere near the constraint your investor friend is describing. Your CAC could quadruple (to $196) and you'd still clear his 5-month bar. So "set a $196 ceiling" is the literal answer, but it's not the binding constraint on your business right now — you have ~4.4x headroom before that rule even matters.

**What actually limits you today:**
- **Runway (15 months)**, not payback ratio. At $400/mo ad spend you're not cash-constrained on CAC — you're constrained by whether $400/mo scales to a spend level that gets you to profitability before runway runs out.
- **Whether CAC stays flat as you scale spend.** $44 CAC on $400/mo is a small, possibly cheap/efficient channel. Doubling or tripling spend rarely holds CAC constant — diminishing returns on ad channels are the norm. The ceiling matters once you're testing whether scaled spend still pays back in time, not at your current volume.

**One gap in the 5-month rule itself:** payback period ignores churn. $196 CAC only "works" if the customer sticks around long enough to generate multiples of that $39.20/month beyond the payback point (LTV:CAC, typically targeted at 3:1+, i.e., ~15 months of retention against a $196 CAC). If your monthly churn is high, a $196 CAC could hit 5-month payback and still be a bad deal — you'd be recovering cost but not making a return. Your investor's portfolio benchmark likely assumes their companies' typical retention profile, which may not match a $49/mo two-founder SaaS. Before treating $196 as a real budget to spend up to, get a churn/retention number and check LTV:CAC, not just payback.

**Practical recommendation:** don't use $196 as a spending target — use it as a stop-loss. As you test scaling ad spend, keep CAC well under $196 (I'd aim to keep new-channel tests under ~$100 until you have real retention data), and watch whether CAC creeps up as spend increases. The ceiling protects you from a bad channel; it doesn't tell you what a good one costs.
