# Maribel Home Goods — FY Paid Media ROAS Target

**Prepared for: CFO / Annual Plan**
**Metric owner: Marketing**

## Recommended target

**Blended ROAS target: 3.5x, with a 2.5x floor.**

Do not adopt the generic "4x" benchmark. It isn't derived from our unit economics — it's a rule of thumb that happens to fit businesses with a ~25% contribution margin. Ours is 45%. Applying it blind would either overstate how much profit we need per ad dollar, or (more likely) cause us to cut spend on channels/campaigns that are still profitable, capping growth we could otherwise fund.

## The math behind it

- AOV: $68
- Contribution margin (after COGS + shipping): 45% → **$30.60 contribution profit per order**
- **Breakeven ROAS = 1 ÷ contribution margin = 1 ÷ 0.45 = 2.2x**

Below 2.2x, every order loses money before fixed overhead is even considered. Above 2.2x, each incremental point of ROAS converts directly to profit margin on ad-driven revenue:

| ROAS | Ad spend per order | Profit margin after COGS/shipping/ad spend |
|---|---|---|
| 2.2x (breakeven) | $30.60 | 0% |
| 2.5x | $27.20 | 4.6% |
| 3.0x | $22.67 | 11.6% |
| **3.5x (target)** | **$19.43** | **16.4%** |
| 4.0x | $17.00 | 20.0% |

So "4x" isn't wrong, exactly — it's just a choice to run paid media at a 20% profit margin. That's a legitimate number to pick, but it should be picked deliberately as a growth/profit tradeoff, not imported as an industry default. At $60K/month in spend, moving the target from 4.0x to 3.5x is the difference between requiring ~$240K/mo in attributed revenue vs. ~$210K/mo — a meaningful lever on how much of the account we can keep scaling profitably.

## Why 3.5x / 2.5x, not 4.0x

- **2.5x floor**: breakeven (2.2x) plus a ~15% cushion to absorb discounts, returns, and the fact that contribution margin doesn't include fixed overhead (salaries, tooling, warehouse). Spend below this floor should be paused or restructured.
- **3.5x target**: delivers a healthy ~16% margin on ad-driven revenue — enough to fund overhead and reinvestment — while leaving room to keep scaling into slightly-less-efficient audiences (prospecting, upper funnel, new creative tests) that a flat 4.0x target would choke off.
- **4.0x+ as a ceiling, not a floor**: fine to hold your best campaigns/audiences to this bar, but requiring it blended across the full $60K/month will systematically underspend — you'll be leaving profitable growth (everything performing between 2.5x–4.0x) on the table.

## Two caveats to flag to the CFO

1. **This is first-order (transactional) ROAS.** It doesn't credit repeat purchases. Home fragrance typically has real repeat/gifting behavior — if we have repeat purchase rate and 12-month LTV per customer, we should also frame this as an LTV:CAC target (commonly 3:1) alongside ROAS. That could justify accepting a lower first-order ROAS on prospecting spend that reliably converts to repeat buyers. Worth a follow-up analysis once cohort data is pulled.
2. **Use blended MER, not platform-reported ROAS.** Meta/Google dashboards over-credit themselves via last-click/view-through attribution. The number that should govern the annual plan is **blended MER = Total Company Revenue ÷ Total Ad Spend**, measured monthly. Platform-reported ROAS is useful for in-platform optimization, but the 3.5x target for the annual plan should be validated against blended MER, not the Ads Manager number.

## Bottom line for the annual plan

> **Target blended ROAS: 3.5x. Minimum acceptable: 2.5x. Anything below 2.2x is unprofitable by definition given our current margin structure.**
