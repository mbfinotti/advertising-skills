No — forcing a rotation right now would be optimizing for the agency's rule instead of your actual objective.

**Why the "mandatory 4-week rotation" doesn't apply here**

Creative wear-out is a *response-fatigue* phenomenon: people stop reacting to an ad (stop clicking, stop converting, stop feeling something) because they've seen it too many times. The proxy metrics for that are things like falling CTR, falling conversion rate, or falling engagement rate *despite* continued or flat exposure — not frequency itself. Frequency is an input, not a symptom.

Your objective here is brand lift and recall, not clicks or conversions. And your actual symptom metrics — view rate and aided recall — are still climbing at week 8. That's the opposite of what creative wear-out looks like. There's decent evidence (marketing science, e.g. Binet & Field, and Nielsen/Kantar brand-lift studies) that for brand-building campaigns, repetition *drives* recall for longer than performance marketers expect, precisely because recall is a memory-encoding effect that benefits from repeated exposure, up to a point.

**Why "4.1 frequency = burnout" is the wrong read**

4.1 is a generic threshold, not one derived from your funnel. Burnout thresholds are objective-dependent:

- For conversion campaigns, wear-out often shows up in the 3-5 frequency range because the ad is asking for a scarce action (click, purchase) repeatedly.
- For brand/awareness campaigns, especially with a 60-second cinematic film (high production value, story-driven, not a discount banner), effective frequency for recall lift is often cited as needing 3+ exposures just to *register*, and awareness campaigns routinely run to frequency 6-10+ before recall or favorability plateaus or reverses.

You have no internal data showing degradation at 4.1 — you have two metrics saying the opposite.

**What would actually indicate wear-out, and what I'd check before deciding anything**

- Split view rate and recall lift **by frequency band** (e.g., 1-2, 3-4, 5+ exposures) if your platform/lift study supports it. If recall among the 5+ frequency segment is flat or declining while it climbs at 1-3, that's early wear-out signal — even while your blended average keeps rising from audience growth.
- Watch for negative signals distinct from your two headline metrics: rising negative feedback/hide rate, rising cost-per-lift, declining completion rate on the 60s film, brand sentiment dipping.
- Check whether the recall/view-rate improvement is coming from *reach expansion* (new people seeing it for the first time) rather than the existing audience re-responding — if so, blended metrics can improve even as your core audience is wearing out.

**Recommendation**

Don't force the rotation on a calendar rule. Instead:
1. Ask the agency for the frequency-banded breakdown of recall/view rate (or at least completion rate) — that's the real wear-out test.
2. If high-frequency segments are still healthy, keep running the current film and consider capping frequency per user (e.g., 6-8) rather than replacing creative, so you're managing the actual risk (fatigue in the over-exposed tail) without discarding a working asset.
3. If the agency's "every 4 weeks, no exceptions" policy is a blanket ops rule rather than a data-backed one for brand campaigns, it's worth pushing back on it as a default — it's tuned for performance creative, not for a lift-optimized brand film with an 8-week upward trend.
