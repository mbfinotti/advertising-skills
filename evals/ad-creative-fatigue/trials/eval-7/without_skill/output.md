No. Don't rewrite everything — the data doesn't support that diagnosis.

**Why "Ad Strength dropped" isn't the alarm your account manager thinks it is**

- Ad Strength is a construct-diversity/best-practice checklist, not a performance metric. It scores how many assets, pinning rules, and unique-message combinations you have — it does not measure fatigue, CTR decay, or conversion decline.
- Google recalibrates Ad Strength thresholds periodically and reshuffles labels without any change to your ads (this happens across the whole platform, not just your account). A label move from Good → Average with no asset changes is a known, common event.
- Ad fatigue is a *behavioral* signal: falling CTR and/or falling conversion rate over time as the same audience keeps seeing the same creative. You explicitly have flat CTR and flat conversion rate for two months. That's the opposite of the fatigue signature — flat is stable, not decaying.

**What the asset report actually says**

- 9 of 10 assets are rated Good/Best — that's a healthy spread, not a broad staleness problem.
- 1 headline stuck at Low for 5 weeks is a specific, isolated underperformer. Google's per-asset labels *are* built from real impression/click performance data (unlike the aggregate Ad Strength score), so this one is worth acting on.

**What to actually do**

1. Pull the asset performance detail for that one Low headline (impressions, CTR relative to the ad's other headlines). If it's had enough impressions to be statistically meaningful and it's dragging, pause or replace *that single headline*.
2. Add 1-2 new headline variants to replace it, keeping everything else intact — this also nudges Ad Strength up somewhat, as a side effect, not the goal.
3. Leave the other 9 Good/Best assets alone. There's no CTR/conversion evidence implicating them, and rewriting working assets just resets their learning/performance data for no benefit.
4. Don't chase the Ad Strength label itself. Treat it as a checklist nudge (asset count/diversity), and manage the ad off CTR, conversion rate, and per-asset performance labels instead.
5. If you want a real fatigue check going forward, watch CTR/conv rate trended weekly, segmented by device/audience if possible — that's the metric that would actually tell you if creative is wearing out, not the Ad Strength badge.

**Bottom line:** swap the one Low headline, keep the rest, and don't let "Average" Ad Strength drive a full rewrite — the underlying performance metrics say this ad is stable, not fatigued.
