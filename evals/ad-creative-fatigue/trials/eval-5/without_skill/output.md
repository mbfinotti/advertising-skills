## Diagnosis first — this determines the creative plan

The fresh ad decaying to pack-level performance within two weeks is the key data point. If this were normal creative wear-out, that ad should have significantly outperformed the tired four for longer. It didn't — which means the problem isn't "the creative is stale," it's **audience saturation**. On a fixed matched list of 38K:

- **Reach flat + impressions climbing** = LinkedIn's delivery system has stopped finding un-served members in your list and is now recycling impressions onto people it has already reached. Effective addressable inventory inside a 38K matched audience is typically 40–60% (LinkedIn active-member coverage, not the full list) — so your real reached pool is likely closer to 15–22K, and _those_ people are absorbing all the frequency.
- **Freq 7.8 cumulative** on that smaller effectively-reached pool is high enough to produce banner blindness, which explains the CTR drop (0.43% → 0.29%) uniformly across all five ads, new one included.
- **Demo requests and click-to-demo both down** confirms it's not just top-of-funnel fatigue — the same narrow slice of people is clicking repeatedly out of familiarity/curiosity rather than net-new prospects converting.

Conclusion: a new creative batch alone will buy you maybe 2 weeks of lift, then decay again at the same rate, unless it's paired with **audience segmentation** so different creative reaches different people instead of all five ads (and now more) hitting the same saturated slice.

## How many ads to brief: 9, not "a batch of 5 replacements"

Don't do a 1:1 swap of the current five. Structure it as **3 angle clusters × 3 creative variants each**, mapped to three sub-segments of the 38K list (by persona/buying-committee role) rather than one undifferentiated audience. This does two things: lowers effective frequency per person (each sub-segment only sees 3 ads, not 9), and increases relevance by message-matching to role.

Ask your ABM/RevOps team to split the 38K matched list by persona before the brief goes out — CFO/Finance leader (economic buyer), Controller/Finance Ops (day-to-day champion), and Risk/Compliance or Treasury (technical evaluator) is a typical fintech split. If you can't segment by persona in time, segment by account tier (target vs. tier-2) instead — same principle, different axis.

## The angles (one cluster per segment)

**1. Economic buyer (CFO) — "Business case" angle**

- Quantified ROI/cost-of-inaction hook, not feature talk. Lead with the number ("$X reclaimed per quarter," "N hours cut per close cycle").
- Format: static image or short (15–20s) data-forward video.
- CTA: lighter-weight than "Request a demo" — an ROI calculator or benchmark report. This also diversifies your CTA mix, which should help the click-to-demo decay since not every click is being forced into the same high-friction offer.

**2. Champion (Controller/Finance Ops) — "Pain agitation + proof" angle**

- Open on the specific daily friction (manual reconciliation, month-end close pain, spreadsheet risk), then a named-customer proof point with a concrete outcome.
- Format: customer logo/quote carousel or short testimonial clip — social proof reads as less "ad-like," which helps against banner blindness.
- CTA: "See how [similar company] did it" → case study, not straight to demo.

**3. Technical evaluator (Risk/Compliance/IT) — "Category/trust" angle**

- Compliance, security, integration, auditability angle — the objection-handling content this persona actually needs before they'll champion internally.
- Format: could be a contrarian/POV or educational carousel (pattern-interrupts well against fatigue because it doesn't look like a typical demo ad).
- CTA: guide/whitepaper download — keep demo CTA reserved for retargeting warmer engagers only.

**Cross-cutting recommendation:** brief one native/organic-look "employee voice" variant per cluster (founder or exec talking to camera or a plain-text/first-person post style) — these consistently outperform polished ad units specifically against fatigue, because they don't pattern-match to "ad" the way your current five likely do.

## Operational changes to brief alongside the creative (won't cost extra budget)

- **Retire the current five entirely** rather than layering new ones on top of them in the same campaign — mixing tired and fresh creative in one ad set lets the algorithm keep serving whatever has the most accumulated engagement (the tired ones), undermining the new batch.
- **Cap frequency** at the campaign level (rotate creative sets every 2–3 weeks) instead of letting one set run 10 weeks straight.
- **Route each of the 3 creative clusters to its own campaign against its own segment**, not one campaign with all 9 ads rotating across the full 38K — this is what actually fixes the saturation problem; the angles alone won't if delivery keeps pooling on the same reached subset.
- **Re-check inventory/bid**: if reach has genuinely plateaued below the addressable list, a bid increase may unlock the unreached ~15–20K rather than continuing to recycle on the reached ones.

## Summary for the agency brief

- 9 ads total: 3 angles × 3 formats/variants per angle.
- 3 segments of the 38K list, each getting its own angle and its own campaign.
- CTA mix per segment (calculator / case study / guide), demo CTA reserved for retargeting engaged users only.
- 2–3 week creative refresh cadence going forward, not 10-week runs.
