**What the numbers actually say**

- Hook rate falling (24%→19%→16%) while hold rate stays flat (9.1% vs 9.4% baseline) is the classic fatigue signature: the same people are seeing this ad again and again and scrolling past faster, but anyone who *does* stop still watches it the same way they always did. If the concept itself had gone stale, hold rate would sag too — it hasn't.
- CVR flat at 2.8% while CTR is down ~20% confirms the back half of the funnel is untouched. The offer, landing page, and audience-message fit are all still fine — this is a top-of-funnel/scroll-stopping problem, not a "this concept stopped working" problem.
- Hide/report rate tripling is the tell. That metric moves almost exclusively with frequency — the same eyeballs getting hit too many times, not a general quality complaint. Nine weeks with no audience change is more than enough time for frequency to have crept way up on your core segment.

**Why "start over" overcorrects**

You'd be discarding a video that's still converting at 2.8% and holding attention normally, based on a symptom (hook decay) that's localized to the first 3 seconds and one likely cause (saturation). A full new concept also means running the current, now-fatigued ad as your volume driver for another 4-6 weeks while the agency works — that's the expensive part of this plan, not the agency fee.

**What I'd do instead this week**

1. Cut 3-4 new cold opens/hooks from the same footage (different first frame, different first line, different pattern interrupt) — reuses the proven back-half, only replaces the part that's actually degrading. This is exactly the "spare capacity" your in-house editor is suited for, and you can have it live in days, not weeks.
2. Refresh the thumbnail/first-frame image and primary text simultaneously — cheap, often moves hook rate on its own.
3. Address the frequency side directly: check delivery frequency if you haven't (I'd bet it's climbed steadily over the 9 weeks); expand or refresh the audience, or add a frequency cap/exclusion for people already hit heavily, to inject fresh eyes rather than re-serving the same pool.
4. Let those hook variants run 1-2 weeks. If hook rate recovers with hold/CVR still intact, you've bought yourself months more life from an asset that's already proven — far cheaper than a new shoot.

**Where the agency brief still belongs**

Start it in parallel as insurance, not as the fix — creative eventually does wear out for real, and you don't want to be starting from zero when hook refreshes stop working. But don't treat it as the answer to *this* dataset; the numbers say "fatigued," not "broken."
