# Sprint Brief — Testing Approach (Paid Social, Vantablue)

## 1. What the data is actually telling us

- 10 sprints, 4 hook cuts each ≈ 40 hook variants tested.
- All 40 sit on one concept: "close the books in 3 days."
- Performance has been flat for a full quarter despite continuous hook iteration.

**Diagnosis:** hooks are a message-framing lever _within_ a concept. When 40 framings of the same concept all plateau at the same ceiling, the constraint is the concept, not the framing. Cutting 6 more hooks next sprint tests the same variable that already produced 3 months of flat data — it won't tell leadership anything new, it will just confirm the ceiling again at extra cost.

## 2. The unforced error

Legal cleared a signed customer case study last month — a real customer, a real number (**71% reduction in close time**), third-party credibility. That's a different concept, not a hook: it replaces a claim ("you can close in 3 days") with proof ("this customer did"). It's sitting unused in a drive folder while the sprint plan proposes variant #41–46 of the same claim.

## 3. Recommendation

Reallocate next sprint from 6 hooks-on-one-concept to a **concept-level test**, using the case study as the second concept arm. This still satisfies "hooks are the cheapest test" — we keep hook variation, but nested inside two concepts instead of one, so the sprint finally produces a signal leadership hasn't seen yet: does concept move the needle where hooks don't.

## 4. Test structure

| Arm         | Concept                               | Creative units              | Hook cuts                              |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| A (control) | Close the books in 3 days             | 1 base ad                   | 3 hooks (continuation of current line) |
| B (new)     | Case study — 71% close-time reduction | 1 base ad (testimonial-led) | 3 hooks                                |

- Total: 6 cuts, same sprint budget/output leadership asked for — just split 3/3 across two concepts instead of 6/0 on one.
- Base creative for arm B: customer name/logo (if cleared), the 71% stat as the visual headline, quote or clip if available, CTA matched to arm A for clean comparison.
- Hook variants within each arm: keep to genuine framing differences (pain-first, proof-first, urgency, peer-comparison) so we're isolating concept as the variable, not re-adding hook noise.

## 5. Isolation & read

- Equal budget split between arm A and arm B, not equal split across all 6 ads individually — concept is the thing being tested, hooks are secondary.
- Hold platform, placement, and audience constant across both arms.
- Minimum spend/impression threshold before declaring a winner — set at whatever this account's usual significance threshold is (carry over from prior sprints' methodology).
- Primary metric: whichever bottom-of-funnel metric current sprints already optimize for (CTR alone won't settle this — a proof concept can win on CTR and lose on CVR, or vice versa).

## 6. Success criteria

- **Concept signal, not just winner:** does arm B beat arm A's best-performing hook by a meaningful margin? That's the answer to "is concept the ceiling."
- If B wins clearly → next sprint's brief shifts to building out a proof/case-study concept family, hooks nested inside it.
- If A and B perform similarly → the ceiling isn't concept either; that's useful too, it redirects the next test toward audience, offer, or format instead of another round of hooks.
- If B underperforms → we've lost nothing; arm A continues as-is and the case study gets a format-level test (long-form vs short cut) before being shelved.

## 7. Why this over "just run the 6 hooks"

- Same sprint cost, same "cheap test" framing leadership wants — this isn't a bigger ask, it's a reallocation.
- 3 months of flat data already answered "does another hook move this concept" — repeating that question burns a sprint on a known result.
- This is the only test that can tell leadership whether the plateau is a hook problem or a concept problem, which is the actual decision they need for Q4 planning.
