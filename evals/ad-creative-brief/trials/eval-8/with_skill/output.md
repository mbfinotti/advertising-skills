# Testing Approach — Next Sprint: CloseBooks3Day

## The re-rank leadership needs to see first

"Hooks are cheapest" is true on effort but the skill's own Testing Intent ordering is explicit that hook is also the **lowest-value** rung — it can't rescue a wrong angle, and after 10 months on the same concept family with a full quarter of flat results, the account is showing exactly the two named conditions that override the default hook-first ratio:

1. **The concept family is flat, and it's the only family ever run.** Per the framework: *"Promote [persona/angle] when the concept family is flat... when the flat family is the only family you have ever run (skip rungs outright)."* Four more hook flavors on a premise that's never been tested against an alternative teaches nothing new — it's the textbook "iteration paralysis" failure mode.
2. **There is an unfair advantage sitting unused.** A signed, legally-cleared customer case study (71% close-time reduction) is a named, quantifiable outcome — the exact proof point a B2B brief needs — and it's cleared and idle. Per the framework: *"a proof asset... already cleared and sitting unused... makes a proof test the cheapest rung rather than the third."* Proof testing here costs almost nothing: swap the evidence into the best-performing existing body, reshoot nothing.

Recommendation: **don't spend the sprint on 6 hook cuts. Split it across proof and hook, and flag angle for the sprint after.**

## Sprint allocation (the 6 cuts, re-split)

**3× Proof-test cuts** — swap the case study into the current best-performing hook/body. No new shoot: same layout, new evidence.
- `CloseBooks_V1_ProofCase_SocialProof` — case-study number becomes the hook itself (TEXT OVERLAY: "71% faster close — [Customer], verified"; SPOKEN/primary text: the customer's own framing of the before/after)
- `CloseBooks_V1_ProofCase_Objection` — leads with the objection the case study answers (e.g., "we tried automating close before and it didn't stick" → resolved by the named outcome)
- `CloseBooks_V1_ProofCase_Control` — same hook as the best-running current variant, only the proof point swapped, to isolate the proof effect cleanly

**2× Hook-test cuts** — new trigger types, not new flavors of whatever's already been run four times a sprint for a quarter. Pull the account's hook-performance-by-trigger-type log first; brief the two triggers with the fewest reps, not the two easiest to write.
- `CloseBooks_V1_Hook_[TriggerA]`
- `CloseBooks_V1_Hook_[TriggerB]`
(Tag each against the eight-trigger taxonomy once the log confirms which two are under-tested — don't default to pain agitation/identity call-out again if those are already the four that went flat.)

**1× Reserved / not built** — capacity held back, spent on scoping the angle-test brief for next sprint (persona interviews or review re-mining to source a second concept family), rather than a seventh hook cut. This is a planning slot, not a creative deliverable.

Deliverables arithmetic: **3 proof cuts + 2 hook cuts = 5 files, all IT (iteration) of the existing concept** — down from 6, with the sixth slot reallocated to angle-brief prep rather than produced this sprint.

## Hypotheses (typed)

- **Proof type:** *"If we lead with the close-process pain and the 71% verified close-time reduction, then finance/ops leaders evaluating close automation will [request demo / convert] at or under [current CPA/CPO benchmark]."*
- **Hook type:** *"If we open with [under-tested trigger] instead of [the trigger the last quarter of cuts has been rotating], then hook rate moves off its flat baseline without changing CPA/CPO."*

## Test block

| Field | Proof test | Hook test |
|---|---|---|
| Variable | Evidence (case study replaces prior proof point) | Trigger type only |
| Constant | Body/edit, offer, audience, placements, targeting frozen | Body/edit, offer, proof point, audience, targeting frozen |
| Minimum evidence | B2B volume is typically low — gate on leading signal (CTR/demo-request trend) at 1,000 impressions/variant/week for 3 weeks before any read; judge the real call on SQL rate / cost per opportunity at 30/60/90 days, not CTR alone | 2,000 impressions before reading hook rate |
| Deciding KPI + benchmark | SQL rate / cost per opportunity vs. account history (not CTR — a CTR win with a worse SQL rate is repurposed mid-funnel, not scaled) | Hook rate vs. this account's trailing average (flat-quarter baseline is the benchmark to beat) |
| On win | Scale; case study becomes the standing proof point across the concept; get legal to confirm the "71%" figure is cleared for ad-copy phrasing, not just the case-study document itself | Promote winning trigger type; retire the flat ones from the rotation |
| On loss | Angle survives (proof isn't the angle) — but a proof-test loss on a legally-cleared, quantified outcome is a stronger signal than a hook loss that the angle itself needs testing next | Trigger type retires; don't re-test it next sprint |
| Learning destination | Angle bank + hook-performance-by-trigger-type log | Same |

## What this doesn't fix, on purpose

This sprint buys a cheap read on whether unused proof was the missing ingredient and cleans up hook diversity. It does **not** test whether "close the books in 3 days" is even the right angle for this audience — that's a full-quarter flat result on the only family ever run, which is the angle rung's job, not hook's or proof's. Recommend scoping that as its own brief next sprint rather than folding it in here.
