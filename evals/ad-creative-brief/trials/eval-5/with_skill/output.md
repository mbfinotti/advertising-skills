## ⚠️ Three flags before the brief — these correct the stated plan, not just annotate it

**1. "Nobody searches for it yet" means Search almost dropped out of the format menu — it didn't, but the target keyword did.**
The skill's rule: *"Search enters the ranking only where keyword demand already exists; where it does not, it drops out rather than ranking last."* Nobody types "smart compost concierge" into Google, so an ad group built around that term has no traffic to serve, Ad Strength score notwithstanding. What *does* have volume: adjacent problem-aware queries — "compost pickup service," "curbside composting near me," "composting without smell/pests," "food waste collection service." This brief targets **that** demand and introduces Casaverde to people already searching for a solution to the problem, not to people searching for Casaverde. If your agency's plan was to bid on category-defining terms, that plan has zero volume — redirect the keyword list before spend goes live.

**2. Pinning all 6 headlines turns an RSA into a static ad you're paying RSA overhead to run.**
Skill rule, verbatim: *"pin at most 1-2 essential headlines; note in the brief that pinning restricts combination testing sharply, so every pin needs a stated reason (compliance line, mandated brand phrase)."* One published analysis puts a single pin at >75% less combination testing — pinning all six removes combination testing entirely. There is no learning left to collect; you'd be running one fixed sentence through Google's most expensive-to-build variant format. This brief pins **one** headline (brand name, reason below) and leaves five live for the algorithm to test.

**3. "Iterate until Ad Strength shows Excellent" is the specific behavior the skill tells every search brief not to do.**
Skill rule, verbatim, required in every search brief: *"Ad Strength is a content-quality signal with no direct effect on Ad Rank; do not add loosely relevant headlines to chase 'Excellent'."* Ad Strength rewards headline/description *count* and *keyword-stuffing*, not conversion quality — chasing it is a documented way to dilute a tight six-headline pool into fifteen loosely-relevant ones. This brief's test block uses cost-per-qualified-lead as the deciding KPI. Ad Strength is not tracked as a success metric anywhere below.

### The 3 strongest hooks, as asked for

1. **"No Smell. No Bugs. No Mess."** — *Pain agitation.* Opens directly inside the objection that kills DIY composting before anyone reads further. Near-zero cost to write; the evidence (mess/pests as the top blocker) is category-common knowledge, not yet review-sourced for this product — verify against real objection data before scaling spend on this line.
2. **"Casaverde: Compost Concierge"** — *Curiosity gap.* "Concierge" applied to food scraps is a genuine pattern interrupt for the category — it doesn't read as another compost bin. Resolution field: the gap closes on the landing page, which must open by explaining what a compost concierge actually does within the first line, or this reads as a trick.
3. **"Cancel Anytime, No Contract"** — *Contrarian / myth-busting.* Runs against the default assumption that a pickup subscription locks you in — removes the specific trust objection ("will this trap me") before the click, not after.

**Recommended first test:** pair Hook 1 (pain agitation) with Hook 3 (contrarian/risk-reversal) as the two unpinned headlines most likely to co-appear in Google's top combinations — cheapest to write, highest-ranked survivors of the awareness level (problem-aware favors agitation + differentiation, per the skill's awareness-to-hook mapping).
