# Mielette — Creator Section of the Brief

Before the field-by-field content: your plan has three items that the brief format this skill uses treats as **named failure modes**, not stylistic choices. I've corrected them below and explained why, using the skill's own language. If you want to keep any of the three as-is, say so and I'll write it that way instead — but you should hear the trade-off first.

⚠️ **Word-for-word script → talking points.** The skill's UGC variant is explicit: _"Talking points, not a script: the beats the creator must hit, in their own words."_ Sending 4 creators the identical 45-second script doesn't buy consistency — it produces one asset shot four times, reads as scripted (the exact thing that kills UGC's credibility), and burns your only chance to isolate which hook actually moves the number. The skill's round design for exactly this setup: _"brief 3-5 creators on the same objective with different hook directions."_ Same core message, same beats, four different openings.

⚠️ **"Unlimited perpetual rights" as standard.** Quoted directly from the skill: _"An unlimited-perpetual default is a negotiation failure, not a template default."_ It also inflates creator rate cards (perpetual+unlimited usage is the most expensive tier to buy) and the template requires the field be stated as where / how long / geography — "unlimited perpetual" isn't a value in that field, it's the absence of one. Defaulted below to a bounded term; tell me the real paid-usage window and I'll swap it in.

⚠️ **Skipping creator disclosure because Meta labels the ad "Sponsored".** Also direct from the skill: _"platform ad labels do not satisfy creator-level FTC disclosure obligations."_ Meta's Sponsored label is a platform requirement; FTC's endorsement disclosure requirement sits on the creator's own content (caption/on-screen) and is a separate legal obligation. This one isn't a creative trade-off — legal counsel should confirm, not overrule.

⚠️ **Three stakeholders sending notes independently, as they think of them.** This is the skill's own annotated bad-brief example, verbatim: _"'feedback as it comes' — unbounded stakeholders, no revision cap, no single approver: the revision loop that burns creator relationships."_ Fix isn't fewer opinions, it's one channel: brand, product and legal all feed into one owner, who sends creators one consolidated round per revision.

## 5 · HOOKS (creator-facing form)

Four creators, one concept, four hook directions — not four copies of one script. Each creator gets the same beat structure and proof point, and picks the hook they can deliver naturally.

**Beat structure (talking points, not lines):**
`Cold open (0–2s, visual hook)` → `problem, named directly (2–5s)` → `unboxing/first-bite moment` → `proof overlay (review quote or the France-trained-baker credential — confirm which proof point once section 3 is set)` → `CTA`

| Creator   | Hook direction             | SPOKEN (talking point)                                                            | VISUAL                                                      | TEXT OVERLAY                |
| --------- | -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Creator A | Pain agitation             | Opens on why grocery-store "French" pastry disappoints — in their own words       | Close-up bite, visible disappointment → cut to Mielette box | "This isn't French bakery." |
| Creator B | Identity call-out          | Names who this is for (someone who's actually had real French pastry)             | Direct to camera, box already on counter                    | "If you know, you know 🥐"  |
| Creator C | Curiosity gap              | Teases what's in the box without naming it yet                                    | Box opening, contents hidden until reveal                   | "What's actually in here?"  |
| Creator D | Social proof / credibility | Leads with why they said yes to this — genuine surprise angle, in their own words | Reaction shot, first bite                                   | "I didn't expect this."     |

Recommended first read: Creator A or B (pain agitation / identity call-out — near-zero cost, no substantiation burden). Curiosity gap and social proof are there to diversify the round, not to be dropped if one underperforms — read all four before calling a winner.

Give creators the talking points and the proof point, not a script. If a creator's own phrasing drifts from the beat, that's fine — outputs are shot-by-shot scripts and belong downstream to `ugc-ad-scripts` once a direction here is picked; this brief stops at direction.

## Feedback loop (applies across all 4 creators)

- **One approver**, not three parallel senders. Brand, product and legal route notes to that one owner.
- **One consolidated round per revision** — the owner merges brand/product/legal input before it reaches the creator, once per round, inside the 2-round cap above.
- Legal's note on disclosure wording is a pre-shoot gate, not a round-2 note — get it before creators start, so it doesn't eat a revision round.
- Send creative back only if it misses the beat structure or the proof point — not for stylistic preference; over-directing a creator on delivery is what makes UGC stop reading as UGC.
