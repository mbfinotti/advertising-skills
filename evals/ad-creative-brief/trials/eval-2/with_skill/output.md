# Hook Section — Herbalux, Problem-Aware / Meta Reels

**Definitions (per brief standard):** Concept = one distinct creative idea. Angle = the argument for why this person should care, as a human sentence. Hook = the opening 1–3 seconds (video). Format = the execution container. Iteration = a variant changing one element, not a new brief.

**Context carried into this section:** B2C/DTC, dietary supplement (regulated), Meta Reels, audience problem-aware (knows the problem, hasn't found a solution — per Schwartz, the right move here is agitate and build urgency, not reassure with proof), format is short-form video/UGC.

---

## Trigger deleted from this brief's menu

**Social proof / credibility (testimonial, before/after) — DELETED, not demoted.**

Legal has cleared zero customer outcomes this quarter. Per the skill's own rule: when no cleared customer result exists and none can be obtained, this trigger is struck from the menu entirely, not ranked low. Holding it at the bottom of a list is what gets it briefed by mistake next sprint.

I'm not including a testimonial or before/after hook, and I'd flag it if you're getting pressure to work around that — an editor asked to imply an outcome nobody signed off on is the fastest way to get the ad rejected by Meta review, or worse, into FTC territory (unsubstantiated efficacy claims in a regulated category are exactly what triggers action). "Social proof always performs" is true when the proof is cleared; it's a liability when it isn't. The fix below gets you proof-shaped credibility without a testimonial.

**Substitute, clearly labeled as a different trigger:** a formulation/certification credibility hook (option 8 below) — third-party testing, sourcing, manufacturing standard. This is product fact, not a customer claim, so it doesn't need the sign-off legal is withholding. It still needs its own substantiation check before it ships (see compliance note on that option) — don't treat it as a loophole around the same review.

## 5. HOOKS — 9 options

*(3–5 is the template default; 9 fills the "room to play" ask by doubling up the two near-zero-cost triggers rather than promoting anything expensive or non-compliant.)*

**1. Pain agitation — the plateau**
- SPOKEN: "You've tried eating better. Sleeping more. Nothing moved."
- VISUAL: mid-scroll energy — tired mid-day slump, not staged
- TEXT OVERLAY: "still exhausted by 2pm?"
- Evidence: problem-aware audiences respond to being named accurately before anything is sold; near-zero cost, one line against existing footage.

**2. Pain agitation — the wrong culprit**
- SPOKEN: "You blamed stress. It might be your gut."
- VISUAL: same body, different symptom entry point (digestive discomfort, not energy)
- TEXT OVERLAY: "it's probably not stress"
- Evidence: same trigger, different pain × persona cell — tests which specific pain this audience recognizes itself in.

**3. Identity call-out — the supplement graveyard**
- SPOKEN: "If your cabinet has five half-used bottles in it, this is for you."
- VISUAL: a cluttered shelf of abandoned supplement bottles
- TEXT OVERLAY: "we know, we know"
- Evidence: near-zero cost, names the audience directly rather than the problem.

**4. Identity call-out — the researcher**
- SPOKEN: "You read every label before you buy. Good. So will we."
- VISUAL: hand flipping a supplement bottle to the ingredient panel
- TEXT OVERLAY: "for people who actually read this part"
- Evidence: targets the label-literate segment of problem-aware buyers specifically — different persona, same trigger.

**5. Contrarian / myth-busting — more isn't the answer**
- SPOKEN: "More pills isn't the fix. Better absorption is."
- VISUAL: side-by-side of a crowded pill organizer vs. a single dose
- TEXT OVERLAY: "stop stacking, start absorbing"
- Evidence: near-zero to write; spends some capital by contradicting the "stack more supplements" instinct common in the category — flag for one round of legal read given the regulated category.

**6. Urgency / stakes — opportunity cost, not medical fear**
- SPOKEN: "Every week you wait is another week feeling like this."
- VISUAL: a calendar flipping past, same tired routine repeating
- TEXT OVERLAY: "how long are you giving this?"
- Evidence: frames urgency around time-cost, not a health-decline claim — deliberately worded to stay clear of the fear-based medical-urgency language Meta's health-ad policy flags. No scarcity/deadline claim is made here, so no offer-mechanics substantiation is owed.

**7. Curiosity gap — the missing variable**
- SPOKEN: "There's one thing most people's routine is missing."
- VISUAL: quick-cut of a normal daily routine, one step conspicuously absent
- TEXT OVERLAY: "guess what's missing →"
- Evidence: near-zero to write. Resolution field: loop closes on-screen at second 8–10 when the product appears — does not defer to the landing page, so it can't read as a bait-and-switch on Reels' short attention window.

**8. Credibility — formulation, not testimonial**
- SPOKEN: "Third-party tested. No proprietary blends. No filler."
- VISUAL: close-up of the ingredient panel / certification mark
- TEXT OVERLAY: "what's actually in it"
- Evidence: this is the compliant substitute for social proof — product-fact credibility, not a customer claim. **Compliance note: every specific claim here (testing standard, certification, "no filler") needs its own substantiation check before this option ships — confirm what's actually cleared before handing to the editor as final direction.**

**9. Pattern interrupt — stretch option**
- SPOKEN: (cold open, no line — visual carries it)
- VISUAL: something visually off-pattern for a supplement ad (e.g., product used in an unexpected, mundane moment rather than a lifestyle shoot)
- TEXT OVERLAY: none in first frame
- Evidence: highest cost of the set — needs its own frame, not a cut of existing footage. Include only if the editor already has footage flexible enough to reframe; otherwise treat as deferred, not deleted, since Reels placement rewards a scroll-stopping open and this is the trigger built for that.

## Recommended first test

**Option 1 (pain agitation — the plateau).**

Rationale: problem-aware audiences are agitated, not reassured — Schwartz's mapping puts agitation as the correct opening move at this level, and pain agitation is also the cheapest trigger to produce (one line against footage you already have). It's the highest-ranked surviving trigger the awareness level actually endorses, once social proof is off the table.

**Second test, if round one is flat:** Option 3 (identity call-out) — same cost tier, tests whether naming the audience outperforms naming the pain.
