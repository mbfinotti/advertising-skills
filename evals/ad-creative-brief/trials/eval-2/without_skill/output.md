Before the hooks — one flag: I've left the testimonial/before-after hook out, and I'd push back on including one this quarter.

**Why:** Legal didn't just withhold specific outcome claims — they said no customer testimonials at all can be cleared, and there are zero approved customer outcomes. A "testimonial hook" with no real, cleared testimonial behind it means either (a) the editor invents or dramatizes one, which is a false-testimonial/FTC endorsement-guide problem in a regulated category, or (b) it sits in the brief as a placeholder nobody can actually shoot — which just wastes the editor's prep time. Neither gets you the social-proof lift you're after; it just moves the compliance problem downstream to production.

What actually gets you social-proof performance without a testimonial: aggregate/number-based proof (review counts, community size, repeat-purchase rate) *if* those specific numbers are already substantiated and legal-cleared, expert/authority proof, and pattern-recognition hooks that make the *viewer* feel seen (which triggers the same "other people like me" response testimonials do, without claiming anyone's result). That's what fills the two testimonial-shaped slots below.

## Hook Options — [Product] Meta Reels
**Audience:** problem-aware · **Placement:** Reels · **Count:** 10
**Compliance note:** No customer testimonials, no before/after or outcome claims (zero approved this quarter, per Legal). Any stat, number, or claim in these hooks must go through claims-substantiation review before shoot — flag placeholders to Legal, don't guess.

| # | Angle | Hook (0–3s line) | Why it works | Compliance note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem callout | "If you're tired by 3pm no matter how much coffee you drink, watch this." | Names the audience's exact symptom — pattern interrupt for problem-aware viewers | Symptom framing only, no product outcome claim |
| 2 | Myth-bust | "Your fatigue probably isn't a caffeine problem. Here's what it actually is." | Contrarian open, reframes the category | Keep mechanism claims to ingredient function, not "fixes fatigue" |
| 3 | Curiosity gap | "There's a reason your multivitamin isn't working. It's not the dose." | Open loop, category-level insight | No comparative "better than X brand" claim without substantiation |
| 4 | Community/aggregate proof | "Over [X,000] people started their morning with this instead of coffee #3." | Social proof via scale, not individual outcome | Number must be real, current, and cleared — pull from Legal-approved stats only |
| 5 | Authority/expert | "A [nutritionist/dietitian] told us the one ingredient most energy supplements skip." | Third-party credibility without a personal-results claim | Expert must be real, credentialed, on-record; script their line, don't paraphrase into a claim they didn't make |
| 6 | Direct address / relatable | "POV: you've tried 4 energy supplements and none of them stuck." | UGC-style relatability, speaks as/to the audience, not as a customer reporting results | Talent reads this as a relatable scenario, not "I tried this product and..." — keep it product-agnostic until the reveal |
| 7 | Educational hook | "Nobody tells you this about how your body actually makes energy." | Value-first hook, builds trust pre-pitch | Any physiological claim needs substantiation sign-off |
| 8 | Ingredient spotlight | "This one ingredient is why some energy supplements actually work." | Specific, credible, shifts proof to formulation not people | Must match approved ingredient claims list |
| 9 | Cost/comparison-to-habit | "You'd spend more on coffee this month than on a month of this." | Reframes price against an existing habit, not against a competitor | Avoid naming/disparaging competitor brands |
| 10 | Objection-handling | "No, it's not 'just another multivitamin.' Here's the actual difference." | Pre-empts skepticism directly, works well for problem-aware/skeptical viewers | Difference claim must be substantiated, not implied superiority |

**For the editor:** hooks 1–3 and 7 need no legal sign-off beyond standard ingredient-claims review — safe to storyboard first. Hooks 4, 5, and 8 have a claims dependency (stat, expert quote, ingredient claim) — don't lock final VO/on-screen text until Legal confirms the specific wording. Hook 6 is the closest thing to a "testimonial feel" in this set; keep the talent brief explicit that they're voicing a relatable scenario, not reporting personal results with this product, or it drifts into the same territory Legal already closed off.
