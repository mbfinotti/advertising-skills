# Channel Plan — Tidewatch Alliance, 2026-09-12

## Headline finding — the 2023 plan cannot be repeated

The 2023 mix (Facebook + Google) no longer exists as an option for this campaign, for a legal reason, not a budget or targeting one.

- Under the EU's Transparency and Targeting of Political Advertising Regulation (TTPA, applicable since October 10, 2025), the largest social platform (Meta/Facebook) stopped running political, electoral, **and social-issue ads** across the EU from early October 2025. The largest search platform (Google) stopped EU political ads the same window, and its video platform (YouTube) from September 23, 2025.
- A campaign built to sway public opinion ahead of an EU Parliament vote — on fisheries policy, run by an ocean-conservation nonprofit — is exactly the "social-issue advocacy" category the ban targets. It is not a borderline case.
- Fines for non-compliant political ad delivery run up to 6% of global annual turnover — that liability sits with the platform, but it's also why the platforms pulled the product entirely rather than try to police it.

So: Meta (Facebook/Instagram) and Google (Search + YouTube) are **deleted at the disqualifier gate, before any scoring** — not "weaker," not "ranked lower." Any plan that puts spend there for this messaging in Germany, France, or the Netherlands is not executable.

## 1. Economics — adapted, not commercial

This skill's affordability gate (contribution margin → allowable CAC → payback) is built for a business selling something. A nonprofit awareness push has no price, margin, or LTV, so I'm not going to force those numbers — doing so would be invented precision.

The gate that actually governs this plan is **funding floor + disqualifier**, run directly: can €25k, split across three countries and three months, clear each surviving channel's minimum viable spend and learning threshold? That's the real ceiling here, worked in Section 3.

If you can give me a target (e.g., "20,000 petition signatures" or "2M qualified impressions across DE/FR/NL"), I can convert that into a cost-per-action ceiling and run a real pass/fail test in Section 6. Until then, the test design below uses reach and action-rate as directional pass criteria.
