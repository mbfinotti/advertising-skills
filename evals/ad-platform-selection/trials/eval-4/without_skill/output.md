## Short answer: The platform is right, the mechanism you picked (Lead Gen Forms) isn't, and $3,500 with zero test budget is premature.

### The math that matters

- **LTV**: $29 × 14 months = **$406** gross revenue per customer. At a plausible ~85% margin for a Chrome extension (low COGS), that's **~$345** in margin-adjusted LTV.
- **Healthy paid acquisition** wants LTV:CAC ≥ 3:1 → you need **CAC under ~$115** to make this sustainable, not just break even.

### Why Lead Gen Forms specifically don't fit

LinkedIn is the most expensive paid social platform per interaction (CPCs commonly $6–12+ in B2B niches). Lead Gen Forms exist to lower that cost by removing the landing-page step — but they were built for **sales-assisted, high-ACV motions**: someone fills a native form, an SDR calls, a $5–20K/year deal eventually closes. A $60–100 cost-per-lead is fine when one closed deal is worth $10K+.

Your product is self-serve at $29/mo. Run the numbers both ways:

| Scenario     | CPL  | Lead→paid | CAC        |
| ------------ | ---- | --------- | ---------- |
| Conservative | $100 | 5%        | **$2,000** |
| Optimistic   | $50  | 12%       | **$417**   |

Even your _best case_ lands right at LTV, with zero margin for support, churn, or refunds — and that's before accounting for the fact that Lead Gen Form fills are low-intent (one click, no commitment) and convert worse than people who actually land on a page and evaluate the product. There's no SDR in your funnel to rescue those low-intent leads either. On paper, this loses money.

### What I'd do instead

1. **Keep LinkedIn, drop Lead Gen Forms.** Run Website Conversion campaigns pointing straight at your Chrome Web Store listing or a free-trial signup — this matches a PLG motion (install = the conversion event) instead of manufacturing a lead-then-follow-up step you don't need.
2. **Add retargeting via Matched Audiences** on site/extension-page visitors — much cheaper CPMs, higher intent, and it's where LinkedIn budget is genuinely efficient.
3. **Don't commit the full $3,500 on day one.** Put $1,000–1,500 in for 4–6 weeks, measure real cost-per-install and install→paid conversion (you'll have this fast at $29/mo, no long sales cycle), then scale only if CAC lands under ~$100–150.
4. **Reserve the rest for near-zero-CAC channels that fit your unfair advantage**: organic LinkedIn content ("I ran LinkedIn ads for X, here's the tool I built") leans directly on your credibility and audience knowledge; Chrome Web Store ASO; recruiter communities (r/recruiting, Slack groups); G2/Capterra reviews; and a referral program — 14-month retention means happy customers worth harvesting for referrals.
5. **Check if pricing can move to per-seat.** Recruiters often buy for a team, not solo — even a modest seat multiplier meaningfully raises LTV and makes the whole paid-acquisition math work.

🎯 Bottom line: LinkedIn as an audience — yes, you're right, recruiters live there. LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms as the acquisition engine for a $29/mo self-serve product, funded with your entire monthly budget before testing — no, the unit economics don't support it as designed.
