Skeptical on this one. A few numbers change the picture:

**$500/month on CTV is not "starting cheap" — it's statistically invisible.** At typical CTV CPMs ($25–40), that's ~15–20K impressions/month. Spread across a real regional/national audience, that's not enough reach or frequency to register a brand lift, and CTV has no clicks — you can't attribute conversions to it at that volume either. You'd be paying for data you can't read.

**Verdora dominating CTV/podcast tells you their budget, not their channel strategy.** Those are awareness channels that work through frequency at scale — they're likely spending 10-20x your total monthly budget just there. Matching their channel mix on 1/20th the money isn't "starting small," it's underfunding a channel below its effectiveness threshold.

**Check the unit economics first.** $38 AOV × 34% margin = ~$13 gross profit/order. Your CAC ceiling depends entirely on repeat-purchase rate for a consumable like granola — that number should decide your channel mix, not what the competitor is doing.

My take: put the $14K where you can measure ROAS this month (Meta/TikTok/Google direct response) and prove a profitable CAC first. If you want a cheap awareness test, 1-2 committed weeks on a niche podcast with a promo code is more measurable per dollar than fragmented self-serve CTV. Revisit CTV once you can dedicate $8-10K/month to it alone.

What's your current CAC and repeat-purchase rate — that's really the number that should drive this.
