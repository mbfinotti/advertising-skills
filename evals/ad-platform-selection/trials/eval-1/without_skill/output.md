## The real question isn't "which platform" — it's "which segment," and the board's number is hiding that

Your board's 3.2:1 is a **blended** average across two businesses with almost nothing in common. Before picking a platform, look at what that blend is covering up.

### Unit economics by plan (not blended)

**Starter — $19/mo, 45% annual retention**
- Monthly gross profit: $19 × 0.82 = **$15.58**
- 45% annual retention → ~6.4% monthly churn → expected lifetime ≈ **15.5 months**
- LTV (monthly GP ÷ monthly churn) ≈ **$242**
- At $200 CAC: **LTV:CAC ≈ 1.2:1**, payback ≈ **12.8 months**
- ⚠️ That's a customer who barely repays acquisition cost before the average one churns. No safety margin — and this ignores support cost, discounts, and time value of money, which would likely push it to breakeven or negative.

**Scale — $549/mo, 92% annual retention**
- Monthly gross profit: $549 × 0.82 = **$450**
- 92% annual retention → ~0.7% monthly churn → expected lifetime ≈ **12 years**
- LTV ≈ **$65,000**
- At $200 CAC: **LTV:CAC ≈ 325:1**, payback ≈ **13 days**

### Why this breaks the board's story

A flat $200 CAC can't be true for both tiers simultaneously — a $549/mo account with a 12-year expected life doesn't get closed by a $200 ad-driven signup in any real B2B motion; that CAC belongs to a self-serve, low-intent funnel. That funnel is Starter.

🎯 **The $200/lead agency channel is almost certainly a Starter-acquisition machine wearing a "blended 3.2:1" costume.** Any paid platform optimizing for CPA at $200 will chase volume, and volume at that price point is Starter, not Scale. At $15K/month ÷ $200 ≈ 75 customers/month, if that mix skews Starter-heavy (likely, since that's what $200 buys), you're scaling a channel sitting near **1.2:1**, not 3.2:1 — well below the ~3:1 safety threshold your board thinks it has, and with a payback period (12.8mo) that eats most of the customer's expected lifetime (15.5mo).

### So: which platform?

Not Meta or TikTok, and not broad Google Display. Those optimize toward the cheapest conversion, which pulls you deeper into the Starter trap.

- **LinkedIn Ads**, targeting by job title (ops/supply chain/demand planning) and company size/headcount — the only major ad platform that lets you bias spend toward accounts that look like Scale buyers, even at a higher CPL than $200.
- **Google Search**, bottom-funnel commercial-intent keywords ("inventory forecasting software," "demand planning tool") — high-intent traffic self-selects toward buyers with a real problem, better fit for Scale than broad reach.

Accept that CAC on these will likely run well above $200 for a genuine Scale lead — that's fine, because Scale's LTV can absorb a CAC 100x higher and still clear 3:1.

### Before committing $15K/month

1. Don't hand the full budget to the agency's $200-CAC motion as-is — split-test $3–5K across LinkedIn + Google Search for 4–6 weeks.
2. **Tag every conversion by resulting plan tier**, not blended CAC. That's the metric that was missing from the board deck.
3. Route likely-Scale leads (company size, inventory volume, job title) to a sales-assisted demo path; route self-serve signups to Starter — separately measure CAC:LTV for each.
4. Push back on using blended LTV:CAC as the "safe to spend" gate — ask for it split by tier before the next budget conversation. A 92%-retention, $549 product and a 45%-retention, $19 product should never share one ratio.
