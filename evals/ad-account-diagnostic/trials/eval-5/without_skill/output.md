**Re: CPA increase — root cause, not market conditions**

The handover note attributes the CPA increase to industry-wide Q4 CPM inflation. The data doesn't support that as the primary driver.

**What's actually happening:**

- Blended CPM is +34% vs. the prior 60 days, but that average is being dragged by one ad set, not five.
- The main prospecting ad set (narrow stacked-interest audience) is +85% CPM. The other four ad sets are +2% to +6% — in line with normal seasonal auction pressure.
- On that same prospecting ad set, frequency has climbed from 2.1 to 4.8 while unique reach has been flat for a month.

**Why that pattern rules out "market-wide inflation" as the main cause:**

If rising CPMs were purely an external auction effect (more advertisers bidding into Q4), it would show up broadly across ad sets of similar type, not concentrated 15-40x more in one set. A genuinely external CPM increase also doesn't produce flat reach with more than doubled frequency — that combination is the signature of **audience exhaustion/saturation**: the stacked-interest audience is narrow enough that Meta's delivery system is now re-serving the same small pool of people repeatedly instead of finding new users, which drives up cost per impression in that ad set's own auction as it competes against itself.

The other four ad sets — which aren't saturated — show CPM movement consistent with genuine seasonal Q4 inflation. That's the real "external" component, and it's roughly 2-6%, not 34%.

**Bottom line:**

- ~5-6 points of the CPM increase (the four normal ad sets) is plausibly external/seasonal.
- The bulk of the blended +34% CPM — and by extension the CPA increase — is coming from one saturated, narrowly-targeted prospecting audience, which is an account-level, fixable issue (broaden/refresh the audience, add creative variants, or shift budget), not a market condition to "ride out."

**Recommendation:** don't confirm the market story as-is. The correct statement to the CEO is that Q4 auction pressure is a real but minor factor (~2-6% CPM on most ad sets), while the dominant cause is frequency-driven saturation in the prospecting ad set that the previous agency didn't refresh or expand — which is within our control to fix.
