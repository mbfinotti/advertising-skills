## Don't take the credit yet — and don't treat this as resolved

**The math first.** Target pace is 8M ÷ 6 weeks = 1.333M/week. Four weeks in, you should be at ~5.33M. You're at 4.9M — a 433K deficit, roughly 8% behind. To hit 8M by Nov 15, Alpine now needs 3.1M impressions in 2 weeks = 1.55M/week, 16% above their own average pace to date. That's not a rounding error to true up; it's a real acceleration they haven't committed to.

## Why the credit offer is the wrong move right now

- **It's premature.** Two weeks remain. Accepting a credit for "undelivered impressions" implicitly writes off the flight before it's over, when full delivery may still be achievable.
- **It substitutes money for exposure.** A credit buys you future impressions with a vendor who just under-delivered and stayed silent about it — not the impressions you contracted for, when you contracted for them. If your campaign has any date sensitivity (holiday camping/gift-buying season closing out by mid-November), a same-window make-good is worth more than a bill credit redeemable later.
- **It skips root cause.** You don't know *why* delivery is behind — inventory shortage, overly narrow targeting, a technical pacing issue, or something being throttled deliberately to protect margin on other clients. Without that answer, you can't judge whether the remaining two weeks will actually catch up, credit or not.
- **The non-disclosure is the bigger signal.** Your analyst caught it, not their account team. Offering a credit the moment they're caught reads as damage control, not account management. That changes how much you should trust their own delivered-impression reporting.

## What to do instead

1. **Verify delivery independently.** Don't rely solely on Alpine's dashboard/reporting. If you have third-party ad-serving or verification (e.g., CM360, DV360, IAS, DoubleVerify, MOAT) pull those numbers and reconcile against Alpine's 4.9M. Confirm the shortfall is a pacing issue, not a viewability/fraud issue hiding inside what they're calling "delivered."

2. **Demand a written catch-up plan, not just a credit.** Ask Alpine directly: what caused the shortfall, and what specifically changes in weeks 5–6 (added inventory, loosened frequency caps, more placements) to hit 1.55M/week. If they can't credibly explain how they get there, that tells you the shortfall will persist.

3. **Check the IO's own remedy language.** Most IOs with guaranteed-impression clauses specify what happens on under-delivery — often a make-good in additional impressions, sometimes at a bonus ratio, sometimes a flight extension. Read that clause before agreeing to anything Alpine proposes informally; their offer may be less than you're contractually owed.

4. **If a shortfall remains at Nov 15, negotiate a make-good, not a discount.** Preferred order:
   - Bonus impressions delivered in a short extension immediately after Nov 15 (days, not weeks), so it's still in-season.
   - If extension isn't workable, a credit — but weighted above 1:1 (e.g., 1.25–1.5x the shortfall) to reflect both the missed guarantee and the failure to disclose it proactively. A straight dollar-for-dollar credit is compensation for Alpine's convenience, not yours.

5. **Get it in writing now**, regardless of which remedy you land on — the current offer, your counter, and Alpine's rationale for the shortfall. Given they didn't surface this themselves, assume you'll need the paper trail for the next renewal conversation.

6. **Revisit future spend separately.** This is a trust issue independent of the make-good math. If Alpine can't proactively flag a pacing miss on a guaranteed IO, that's a reason to shrink reliance on them or add a second vendor for the next flight — not something the credit offer resolves.

**Bottom line:** hold off on accepting anything until the flight closes and you've verified the real shortfall. Push Alpine for delivery, not a bill credit, in whatever's left of the flight window; only fall back to a credit — at better than 1:1 — if delivery genuinely can't be made up.
