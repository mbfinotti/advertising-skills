## Math check

- Budget remaining: $40,000 − $26,000 = **$14,000**
- Days remaining (Oct 26–31): **6 days**
- Exact daily rate needed: $14,000 ÷ 6 = **$2,333/day**, not $2,400/day

$2,400/day × 6 days = $14,400 → total spend = $26,000 + $14,400 = **$40,400**. That's $400 over budget — close, but "almost exactly $40k" isn't quite right. Use **$2,333/day** (or $2,334 on the last day to round out) if you want to land on $40,000 precisely.

## The bigger issue isn't the $400

You're jumping the daily budget from a **$1,050/day** trailing average to **$2,333–2,400/day** — a **+122–129% increase**, applied as a single overnight step change. Two real risks come from that, not from rounding:

1. **Underspend, not overspend, is the actual danger.** Since the client is billed the full $40k regardless, any shortfall means Calder & Moss delivers less media value than what's billed — that's the commitment you're actually exposed on, not a $400 overage.
2. **Platforms may not absorb a >2x jump cleanly.** On Meta especially, a budget increase over roughly 20–30% in a single day risks knocking the ad set out of the learning phase and into a fresh (less efficient) learning period, which typically causes _under_-delivery for a day or two right when you need full delivery. Google Ads has smoother but not infinite pacing headroom either — daily spend can also cap below a sharply raised budget if the auction/audience can't absorb it.
