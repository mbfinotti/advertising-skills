## Pacing check

**Expected pace (linear):** $15,500 ÷ 31 days = **$500.00/day** average — which is exactly your daily budget setting. That's not a coincidence; it means Google is expected to spend the full monthly amount if it runs at the set daily budget every day.

**Expected cumulative through Oct 9:** 9 days × $500 = **$4,500**
**Actual cumulative through Oct 9:** **$4,410**

You're **$90 under** expected pace (-2%), not over. The campaign is tracking essentially on-budget for the month.

## Why Oct 9 looks alarming but isn't

Google Ads daily budgets are not a hard per-day cap — they're an **average**. Google is explicitly allowed to spend up to **2x** your daily budget on a high-traffic day (your $970 is within the $1,000 ceiling), as long as it compensates with lower spend on other days so the **monthly total never exceeds daily budget × days in the billing cycle**.

Check the math: if Oct 9 = $970 and days 1–8 total = $4,410 − $970 = $3,440, that's an average of **$430/day** for those 8 days — under budget. That's exactly the compensation mechanism working as designed, not a billing error.

Google also guarantees this contractually: it will never charge you more in a calendar month than (daily budget × number of days in that month). If overdelivery ever _would_ cause that, Google auto-credits the difference — there's no manual overage to claim, and no refund process exists for this because there's nothing to refund.

## On your plan

**Don't cut the daily budget to $400.** Two problems:

1. **There's no overage to compensate for** — you're already $90 under pace. Cutting to $400 now would put you on track for 31 × $400 = $12,400, roughly **$3,100 short** of your $15,500 monthly budget by month-end. You'd be under-delivering, not correcting anything.
2. **Filing for a refund of the $470** isn't actionable — Google won't process a manual claim for expected within-cap variance, since no overcharge occurred (see the auto-credit guarantee above).

## Recommendation

- **Leave the daily budget at $500.** The pacing algorithm is doing what it's designed to do.
- **Don't file a refund request** — there's nothing billed above the monthly-equivalent cap.
- **Just monitor**: track cumulative spend against the $500/day line and only intervene if actual spend diverges meaningfully (I'd use ~10–15% as a trigger) from the linear expectation later in the month, not off a single high-traffic day.
