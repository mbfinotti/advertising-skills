## Where you actually stand — Ferrostat LinkedIn campaign, Monday Sept 14 check-in

### 1. The "14% behind" number is comparing the wrong baseline

Straight-line calendar pacing (18,000 ÷ 30 days × 13 days elapsed = $7,800 expected) is the wrong yardstick for an account that **delivers almost entirely on weekdays**. September has 22 weekdays and 8 weekend days. Through Sunday the 13th, 9 of those 22 weekdays have elapsed — not 13/30 of the month.

**Weekday-adjusted target:**
- Budget per weekday: $18,000 ÷ 22 = **$818/weekday**
- Expected spend through 9 weekdays: 9 × $818 = **$7,364**
- Actual spend: $6,700
- Real gap: $664, or **~9% behind** — not 14%.

The naive calc effectively charges you for two weekend days (Sept 12–13) where the campaign was never going to spend anything. That's most of your "14%."

### 2. Your recent run rate is already close to what's needed

Trailing 7-day average is $610/**calendar**-day over Sept 7–13 (5 weekdays + 2 weekend days). Converted to a weekday rate: $610 × 7 ÷ 5 = **~$854/weekday**.

Required weekday rate for the rest of the month: remaining budget $11,300 ÷ 13 remaining weekdays = **~$869/weekday**.

$854 vs. $869 — you're pacing at **98% of the rate you need**, right now, with zero changes. Project that rate forward across the 13 remaining weekdays and you land at:

**$6,700 + (13 × $854) = ~$17,800 of $18,000 — 99% budget utilization.**

Even the more conservative, weekend-inclusive projection (trailing $610/day × 17 remaining calendar days) lands you at ~$17,070, or 95%. Either way, you are on a trajectory to land within a few percent of full budget without touching bids.

### 3. The lead count isn't a real signal yet — the lag makes it structurally unreadable

Your conversions report ~3 weeks (21 days) late. Today is day 13 of the month. Any lead tied to **September's** spend can't possibly have reported yet — 13 days in is nowhere near the 21-day lag window (first September-driven leads would start appearing around **Sept 22**, and keep accruing into October).

So the 4 leads you have on the board right now aren't underperformance against September's plan — they're **residual trickle from mid/late-August spend**, the only activity old enough to have cleared the reporting lag. Comparing "4 leads" to "25-lead monthly plan, 43% through the month" is comparing two things that aren't on the same clock. There is currently no valid read on whether September's spend is generating 25 leads' worth of pipeline or 5 — you won't know until the last week of September at the earliest, and won't have a full picture until well into October.

Reacting to this number today means reacting to noise.

### 4. Recommendation: don't push bids today

- **Budget pacing:** roughly on track (~99% weekday-adjusted projection) once you correct for weekday-only delivery. The apparent 14% gap is a measurement artifact, not a real shortfall.
- **Lead pacing:** unmeasurable right now due to the 3-week lag; the low count is expected, not alarming.
- Pushing bids up on the strength of both readings risks **double-correcting**: LinkedIn's own delivery pacing already smooths spend across the month, and layering a manual bid increase on top of a false "behind" signal is the classic way to overshoot budget in the back half of the month, chase CPL up, and still not know until October whether it bought you real pipeline.

**What to actually do instead:**
- Re-run this pacing check using the weekday-adjusted method going forward (worth baking into your standard Monday report so the 14%-style false alarm doesn't recur).
- Leave bids alone. If you want a lever to pull, watch **leading indicators** you don't have to wait 3 weeks for — CTR, cost-per-click, lead-form open rate — as a real-time read on interest/quality.
- Put a real check-in on the calendar for **~Sept 22**, when September-driven leads start clearing the reporting lag, and a fuller one in **early-to-mid October** once most of the month's spend has had time to convert. That's when "way behind on leads" becomes an answerable question.
