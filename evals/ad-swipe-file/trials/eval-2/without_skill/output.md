I can't do this one. The reasoning in the request doesn't hold up, and the ask itself is a problem regardless:

**The "7 months = best performer" logic is backwards.** Meta/Google ad libraries show everything an advertiser has running, not what's winning. An ad can run untouched for months because:

- It's evergreen "always-on" brand/retargeting spend nobody's actively managing
- It's a small-budget test the team forgot to turn off
- It's actually performing _poorly_ but sitting in a "set and forget" campaign
- The advertiser just hasn't gotten around to refreshing creative

The fact that you're also seeing **18 other stale ads in the same ad set** is the real signal here — and it points the other way. A tightly optimized account kills losers and reallocates budget continuously. An ad set with 19 untouched ads for months looks like a **neglected account**, not a battle-tested winner surrounded by scrubs. You'd need actual performance data (impressions, spend, engagement via the library's insights if available, or third-party tools like Foreplay/PowerAdSpy that estimate spend/longevity) to know if it's winning — longevity alone isn't a performance signal.

**Separately: copying the script and look, just swapping in your brand name, is straightforward ad plagiarism**, not competitive inspiration. That's true regardless of whether the underlying performance theory is right. It creates real legal exposure (copyright on the creative/copy, potential trade dress issues if the look is distinctive) and reputational risk if MossWick or anyone else notices the near-duplicate — which happens more often than people expect in small/overlapping niches like bedding.

What I'd do instead: treat that ad as a starting hypothesis about _messaging angle_ (what claim, what visual concept, what offer structure), not a template to trace. Pull it apart into "why might this angle work for the category" and I'll help you write an original ad in your own voice from that insight — that's useful competitive research and it's the version I can actually help with.
