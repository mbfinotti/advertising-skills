**The math, run against this skill's own outcome gate:**

- Your spend tier: $7K/month → sits in the "**under $10K/month**" bracket, benchmarked at **~4% win rate**.
- Your result: 1 winner / 21 launches = **4.8%** — at or slightly _above_ your tier's benchmark.
- Volume check: the gate only trusts a win-rate reading at "**roughly 20 launches per winner**." You're at 21. Your sample is adequate — this isn't noise, it's a real read.

**The podcast's 10-11% figure is the wrong comparator.** This skill's benchmark table only reaches 8% at the **$1M+/month** tier, and even those numbers are flagged as "directional... heavily weighted toward one vendor's DTC dataset." A podcast citing 10-11% for "the best creative teams" is describing an outlier tier — almost certainly not a $7K/month account. Benchmarking yourself against a top-1% team's rate and concluding your process is broken is a category error, not a diagnosis.

**Why ramping up collection specifically won't help, even if it feels productive:**

- The skill's own failure-mode list names this directly: _"Volume bias → volume tells you effort, not outcome."_ Collecting more raw competitor ads increases the size of the archive, not the odds a hypothesis converts to a winner.
- The workflow already caps raw collection at 20-30 ads/competitor _before_ classification — past that point, the lever isn't more ads, it's better corroboration of the ones you have (variant duplication, cross-competitor repetition, geographic breadth) and tighter hypothesis ranking (signal strength × gap in your own account, ease of production last).
- The outcome gate is explicit: a near-zero win rate at adequate volume means escalate to `ad-account-diagnostic` (strategy/PMF problem). You're not near-zero — you're on-benchmark — so that escalation doesn't apply either.

**What's actually worth checking instead**, before touching collection cadence:

1. **The pipeline gate**, the other half of this skill's judgment criteria: at least half of your saved swipe-file entries should eventually reach a test status (hypothesized → briefed → tested). If entries are piling up as an untested archive, that's a conversion problem upstream of win rate — worth auditing before anything else.
2. **Hypothesis quality**, not count: are your 21 ads tracing back to hypotheses scored on both signal strength _and_ absence from your own account (not just "competitor ran it a while")? A weak-signal hypothesis shipped because it was easy to produce will structurally underperform regardless of how many ads fed the file.
3. **Re-baseline the expectation you're managing the team against.** 4.8% at $7K/month spend is a normal, on-track result. If the real goal is to _move up_ the win-rate curve, that historically tracks with spend-tier growth and production capability, not swipe-file collection frequency — collecting faster doesn't buy you into the 8-11% bracket.

**Bottom line:** your swipe file process is not broken. Keep the current weekly cadence (20-30 ads/competitor, classify, corroborate, 5-8 hypotheses/session) and redirect the energy the ramp-up would have cost into checking the pipeline-conversion gate and hypothesis scoring discipline instead.
