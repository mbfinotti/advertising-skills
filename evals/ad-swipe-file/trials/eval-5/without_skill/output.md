## What I won't help build as specified

**Line-by-line script replication.** "Short phrases aren't copyrightable" is true in isolation, but a full script — sequence, structure, specific phrasing, beats — is a different legal question than any single line. Copying it line by line is a strong copyright infringement fact pattern regardless of individual phrase length, and ad platforms (Meta/TikTok) also pull creative for look-alike complaints independent of whether a court would ultimately side with the rival.

**Fabricated customer quotes.** Writing "glowing customer quotes in the same style as theirs" that aren't real customer statements is fabricated testimonial advertising. This is FTC Endorsement Guides territory in the US and equivalent ad-standards rules elsewhere (ARPP in France) — fake reviews/testimonials are a straightforward enforcement target, not a gray area, and it also just poisons your own read on what's working.

**Downloading all 30 of their videos to a shared drive.** Bulk-downloading a competitor's ad library for internal reference/distribution is copying their copyrighted video files wholesale. Screen-recording a few for a creative-brief moodboard with restricted access is a different, much lower-risk thing than mirroring their entire catalog.

**"Better than [rival brand]" end card.** Explicit comparative claims naming a competitor trigger substantiation requirements (you need to be able to prove it) and, more simply, invite a cease-and-desist or a platform ad-review flag for disparagement/trademark use in an ad. Not worth it for an end card line.

### Production plan: chat-bubble UGC ad remake

**Step 1 — Deconstruct the format, not the script (1 day)**
Watch the winning video 3-4 times and extract the *structural* pattern, not their words:
- Hook type (question? problem-agitate? before/after?) and how many seconds to it
- Number of message beats before the reveal
- Pacing rhythm (bubble every ~1.5-2s is typical)
- Where the product first appears on screen
- CTA placement and phrasing style

Write this up as a beat sheet using your own product's actual pain points and voice. Same *shape*, different *content* — this is standard competitive-format analysis, not copying.

**Step 2 — Build your own chat-bubble UI (2-3 days)**
The chat-messaging aesthetic itself (iMessage-style bubbles, typing indicators, timestamps) is a genre convention now, not proprietary to the rival — dozens of UGC ad accounts use it. Safe to reuse the *genre*. Two paths:
- **Fast**: use an existing UGC chat-ad template (CapCut, After Effects template packs, or tools like BubbleAI / TextingStory-style apps) and reskin with Lumen & Larch colors/fonts/icon
- **Custom**: build a simple Figma/After Effects bubble template — iOS-style bubble shapes, your brand's sender-name and avatar, your font

Make it visually *yours*: your brand color for sent bubbles, your typography, your icon set.

**Step 3 — Script it fresh (1 day)**
Write the conversation from your own product truth: real ingredients, real scent throw, real customer language pulled from your actual reviews/support tickets/DMs. If a specific *joke structure* or *reveal mechanic* from the original is genuinely clever (e.g., "friend asks what smell that is → reveal it's a candle not perfume"), that's a reusable *idea* — ideas aren't protected, expression is. Rewrite every line yourself.

**Step 4 — Source real testimonials (2-5 days, parallel)**
Pull 3-5 real quotes from actual reviews, or run a quick UGC/review-request push to customers this week specifically asking for candid quotes you can feature (get written consent to use in ads — a simple one-line reply-to-email confirmation is enough). Real quotes convert better long-term anyway because you can legally boost them, reuse them in email/site, and never have to worry about an audit.

**Step 5 — Competitive reference, done safely (30 min)**
Instead of bulk-downloading their 30 videos: screen-record the 3-5 best-performing ones (check Meta Ad Library / TikTok Creative Center for which are actually running longest = likely winners) into a single password-protected internal moodboard doc, credited as "competitor reference — internal use only, not for redistribution," visible only to the creative team who needs it. That's standard competitive research; a shared drive of their full catalog is closer to a video library you don't own.

**Step 6 — End card (30 min)**
Skip the named comparison. Higher-converting alternatives that don't need substantiation or invite legal risk:
- "The one everyone's asking about"
- "As seen in [X orders/reviews] this month" (if true)
- A direct offer: "20% off your first order" + code
- Your own real differentiator, stated positively: "Hand-poured in [location], 60hr burn" — a specific true claim beats a vague superiority claim anyway and is more persuasive.
