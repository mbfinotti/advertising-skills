## The real problem isn't volume

240 ads / 6 months, 15 used = **6% hit rate**. Doubling to 40/week (~170/month) without fixing that rate gets you ~10 useful ads/month buried in ~680 saved ones. Worse ratio, more noise, same team drowning in curation. Scaling collection now optimizes the wrong stage.

## Diagnose first

Before adding volume, find out what made those 15 count:

- Pull them. What do they share — format, hook, offer, competitor, funnel stage?
- Ask whoever briefed against them: found it themselves, or did someone surface it at the right moment?
- Check timing: were they used within days of saving, or resurfaced months later?

This tells you whether the bottleneck is **sourcing** (not finding the right ads), **surfacing** (finding them but not resurfacing at brief time), or **framing** (saved with no hypothesis attached, so nobody knows what to do with them).

My bet, based on a 6% rate and a spreadsheet as the system of record: it's surfacing and framing, not sourcing. A spreadsheet is a graveyard — things go in, nothing comes back out at the moment it matters.

## Restructure: split collection from curation

**Collection — automate and scale it freely.**
Volume is cheap and doesn't need a human. Use Meta Ad Library (free, API-accessible), plus a tool like Foreplay, PowerAdSpy, or Winmo/Motion for TikTok/Google coverage. Auto-pull by competitor on a schedule. This is where "40/week" or even 200/week costs nothing but API/tool budget — don't gate it on human time.

**Curation — keep small, make it a real job, not a side task.**
A human (or one clearly-owned rotating role) reviews the raw pull weekly and promotes maybe 10-15% into the working library, each tagged with:

- Competitor + format + hook/angle
- Hypothesis: "why would this work for us" (one sentence, mandatory)
- Funnel stage / campaign objective it maps to

An ad with no hypothesis attached doesn't get promoted — it's exactly the kind of entry that becomes one of the 225 nobody uses.

## Close the loop to briefs

The missing link is almost certainly this: nothing pushes curated ads into the brief process; someone has to go pull them. Fix it structurally, not by asking people to try harder:

- Weekly 20-min ritual, tied to the brief/test calendar, not a passive review: "3 angles from competitor library to consider this cycle."
- Tag by upcoming test slot/campaign theme, so briefs pull from a filtered view, not the full 200+.
- Retire ads after ~90 days unused — a swipe file that only grows becomes unsearchable, which is exactly the trap you're in with the spreadsheet today.

## Replace the metric

"Ads collected" is a vanity number — that's how you got to 240 with a 6% return. Track **utilization rate**: curated ads referenced in a brief or test, divided by curated ads added, per month. Target 25-30%. Report that to your CMO instead of raw counts — it reframes "double the ads" into "double the useful ads," which is a curation and process problem, not a collection problem.

## If your CMO still wants 40/week collected

Fine — let automation deliver that volume for near-zero marginal cost, but cap what a human touches at maybe 15-20/week for promotion to the curated library. That satisfies "better coverage" (nothing is missed, it's all captured raw and searchable) without multiplying the curation burden that's already only converting 6% of what you have.

## First two weeks

1. Audit the 15 winners — extract the pattern (2-3 days).
2. Stand up automated pull (Meta Ad Library + one paid tool) covering all 6 competitors (1-2 days setup).
3. Define the tag schema + hypothesis field, migrate the library out of the spreadsheet into something filterable — Airtable or Notion, not Excel (2-3 days).
4. Schedule the weekly curation-to-brief ritual on the existing brief calendar.
5. Report utilization rate, not ad count, at the next check-in with your CMO.
