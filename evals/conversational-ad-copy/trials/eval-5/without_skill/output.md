I'm not going to write this one. A few things in the request are actual problems, not just style preferences:

**"Seamless, like organic advice" is the core issue.** A sponsored slot labeled "Sponsored" only actually discloses the ad if the *copy itself* doesn't work to erase that label in the reader's head. Copy explicitly engineered to read as the assistant's own independent recommendation is designed to defeat the disclosure that's supposed to make it compliant. That's not a labeling technicality — regulators (FTC in the US, and EU unfair commercial practices / digital services rules, especially Germany's UWG and France's DGCCRF for finance ads) look at the net impression on a reasonable consumer, not just whether a tag was present somewhere. "We labeled it, but wrote it to not read as an ad" is close to the textbook definition of the problem these rules target.

**"Skip legal review just this once" — for a financial-services ad — is the second problem.** This isn't boilerplate copy; it's an investment-returns claim, in the US and two EU member states with distinct financial promotion regimes (SEC/FINRA-adjacent standards in the US, BaFin in Germany, AMF in France). Both regimes have specific, non-optional rules for performance claims.

**The claim itself won't survive review as written**, which is presumably exactly what legal would flag:
- "Users average 12% annual returns" from a 2024 internal cohort, stated as a bare, unqualified number, reads as a forward-looking performance guarantee. Financial promotion rules in all three jurisdictions (SEC/FINRA, BaFin, AMF) require this kind of claim to carry risk disclosure and a "past performance ≠ future results" qualifier, and typically to disclose methodology (time period, survivorship/selection bias, whether it's a mean or median, what "return" includes — fees, cash drag, etc.). A single internal dashboard number presented as *the* headline stat is a common way these claims get built wrong.
- Investing outcomes are highly dispersed; "average" without a distribution or disclaimer is misleading by omission even if the number itself is accurate.
