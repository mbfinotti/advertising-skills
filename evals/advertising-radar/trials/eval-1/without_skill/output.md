Here's your standing weekly watch list — built for 45 minutes, zero spend, text-only, covering paid LinkedIn + Google Search plus enough of the surrounding field that you're not flying blind.

I couldn't hit live search this session (tool access was blocked), so this is built from my training knowledge, not a fresh crawl. Do one 10-minute check the first week — click every link, confirm it still publishes — then treat it as fixed. Newsletters and agency blogs occasionally go dormant; institutional ones (platform blogs, LinkedIn's own research arm) almost never do.

## The 45-minute weekly block

Pick one fixed slot (Monday first coffee works well). Run it as a queue, not a browse session — open each source, skim, close.

| Time   | Tier | What                               |
| ------ | ---- | ---------------------------------- |
| 5 min  | 1    | Platform changelogs                |
| 10 min | 2    | B2B ad effectiveness research      |
| 20 min | 3    | Paid search + LinkedIn Ads tactics |
| 10 min | 4    | B2B SaaS growth / creative swipe   |

## Tier 1 — Source of truth (5 min)

- **Google Ads & Commerce Blog** (blog.google/products/ads-commerce) — official product/policy changes for Search Ads. Free, has RSS. Google is obviously selling Google Ads; read as "what changed," not advice.
- **LinkedIn Marketing Solutions Blog** (linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog) — official LinkedIn Ads product updates. Free. Same caveat: LinkedIn sells LinkedIn Ads.

## Tier 2 — Effectiveness research, not sales collateral (10 min)

- **LinkedIn B2B Institute** (b2binstitute.linkedin.com) — the single best free thing in B2B paid: research on brand-vs-demand balance, category entry points, long-vs-short campaigns, built with people from the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute. Funded by LinkedIn (they sell the ads), but the content is academic-adjacent, not pitchy. Highest-leverage 10 minutes of the week for a B2B fintech SaaS buyer.

## Tier 3 — Practitioner tactics (20 min)

- **Search Engine Land – PPC channel** (searchengineland.com/ppc) — daily/weekly industry news across Google Ads, Microsoft Ads, auction dynamics, policy shifts. Free, ad-supported, some vendor-authored pieces (bylines disclose the company).
- **B2Linked — "LinkedIn Ads Insider" blog/newsletter** (b2linked.com/blog) — AJ Wilcox's LinkedIn Ads-only agency. This is the closest thing to a dedicated LinkedIn Ads trade press: bid strategy, audience building, creative testing, benchmarks. Free blog + free email; they sell LinkedIn Ads management and a paid course — read the blog/newsletter, skip anything that funnels to a podcast (they also run one, ignore it per your no-audio rule).
- **PPC Hero** (ppchero.com) — agency blog (Hanapin/Logical Position lineage) with granular Google Ads tactics: scripts, bid strategies, account audits. Free. Agency content, selling PPC management. This is the one most likely to have gone quiet since my knowledge cutoff — verify first.

## Tier 4 — B2B SaaS growth & creative context, rotate weekly (10 min)

- **Demand Curve newsletter** (demandcurve.com) — weekly B2B/SaaS growth tactics, often with real campaign breakdowns. Free newsletter; they sell a growth course/community — the newsletter itself isn't gated.
- **Marketing Examples** by Harry Dry (marketingexamples.com) — short, visual breakdowns of ad copy, landing pages, positioning. Not paid-specific but directly useful for LinkedIn/Search creative and landing-page thinking. Free, no funnel.

## Optional, monthly not weekly (doesn't eat your 45 min)

- **Fintech Brainfood** by Simon Taylor (fintechbrainfood.com) — weekly fintech industry newsletter. Not about ads at all — it's market/competitor/audience context for who you're targeting. If you ever have a spare week, read one issue instead of Tier 4. He runs a fintech advisory/media business, discloses it.

## Mechanics

- Consolidate everything into one RSS reader (Feedly free tier handles all of the above) or one dedicated email folder with a filter — the goal is a single queue you clear in order, not five inboxes.
- If a week runs long, cut Tier 4 first, never Tier 1–2 — the platform changelogs and the B2B Institute are the ones that actually change how you spend money.
- Re-audit this list once a quarter (5 minutes): drop anything that's stopped publishing, keep everything else as-is. Don't rebuild it from scratch each time.
