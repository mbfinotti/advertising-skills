## The system

Two time budgets, kept separate so neither creeps:

- **60 min/week desk time** — reading/scanning. Hard cap, one sitting works better than scattered dips (Friday morning, coffee in hand, before the week's spend review).
- **Drive time (untimed, ~160 min/week across both warehouse round-trips)** — podcasts. This is dead time either way, so it doesn't count against your cap and can run longer without threatening the habit.

Reading budget breakdown (fits in 60 min):

| Time   | Source                                                                       | Why                                                              |
| ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 5 min  | Google Ads & Merchant Center "what's new" + Meta for Business changelog      | Direct account impact. Non-negotiable, check first.              |
| 15 min | Common Thread Collective content (blog/newsletter)                           | DTC-specific media buying tactics, closest to your actual job.   |
| 10 min | Marketing Brew                                                               | Broad B2C/ad-industry news, keeps you from tunnel vision.        |
| 15 min | Skim Meta Ad Library for 3–4 cookware/kitchen competitors                    | Free, no newsletter needed — literal 15-min ritual, not reading. |
| 15 min | Buffer — whatever's loudest that week from the podcast queue or a swipe file | Flex slot, don't over-plan it.                                   |

## Reading list — with the pitch, as asked

- **Meta Business changelog / Ads Help updates** — free, no pitch, official platform notices.
- **Google Ads Help "What's new" + Merchant Center announcements** — free, official, same deal.
- **Common Thread Collective** (blog, "The DTC Guide") — sells media-buying/creative services for DTC brands; content is genuinely tactical and B2C-specific.
- **Marketing Brew** (Morning Brew) — sells nothing to you, ad-supported; broad daily digest, good for "what's happening in the field" without a sales angle.
- **Foreplay's Creative Corner newsletter** — sells an ad-swipe-file SaaS tool; newsletter itself is a soft funnel into that, but the creative breakdowns are useful for a cookware brand thinking about static/video ad angles.
- **Modern Retail** (free tier) — sells a paid subscription tier; the free daily digest covers DTC/retail trends broadly, useful context beyond just ad platforms.

## Podcast queue — for the drives

Rotate these, don't ration them — you have more drive time than content needs:

- **Perpetual Traffic** (Tier 11 / Ralph Burns) — sells Tier 11 agency + mastermind; most tactically dense on Meta/Google buying specifically.
- **Honest Ecommerce** (Chase Clymer, Electric Eye) — sells Electric Eye's Shopify/agency services; interviews DTC founders and operators, good for the brand-side context around your spend.
- **The DTC Pod** (Ramon Berrios) — no hard product pitch, mostly sponsor-supported; founder/operator interviews, broadest B2C scan of the three.
- **Marketing Against the Grain** (Eric Siu, Single Grain) — sells Single Grain agency + Leveling Up courses; more general performance-marketing chatter, good filler.

## One honest note on recency

I wasn't able to re-verify these against live search this session (search access wasn't granted), so this is from my training knowledge as of January 2026 — podcasts/newsletters occasionally rebrand or go quiet. When you subscribe, a 30-second check that the feed's still active is worth it; nothing here should need re-verifying weekly.

## The actual habit

Friday, 60 minutes, same order every time: platform changelogs → CTC → Marketing Brew → Ad Library skim → flex slot. Podcasts auto-queue for the Tuesday/Thursday drives. No new sources added without dropping an old one — that's what keeps a scan "broad" instead of turning into scope creep that kills the habit by week six.
