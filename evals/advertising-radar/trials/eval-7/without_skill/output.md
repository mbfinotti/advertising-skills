# Microsoft Ads crash program (Sept 12 → Oct 6, ~7 hours total)

You already know paid search. The task isn't "learn advertising," it's "learn where Microsoft Ads diverges from Google Ads, and don't get blindsided." Budget accordingly — don't re-learn match types, Quality Score concepts, or auction theory.

## Skip entirely (you already know this)

- Keyword match types, negative keywords, ad rank/auction mechanics, Quality Score concept, campaign/ad group structure, RSAs, conversion tracking philosophy.
- General PPC strategy content. You don't need "PPC 101."

## Week-by-week (2h/week budget)

**1. Sept 12–18 — Mental model (2h)**

- Do the free **Microsoft Advertising Learning Lab** core certification modules (search their official Learning Lab, not a third party — it's built for exactly your situation: experienced Google advertiser, new to Microsoft). Target the Search Fundamentals + Campaign Setup tracks.
- Goal: leave with a "same/different" table in your head, not new PPC theory.

**2. Sept 19–25 — Mechanics that will actually trip you up (2h)**

- **Import from Google Ads**: the one-click import tool. Read how it handles bids/budgets/negatives on import — it does NOT do a clean 1:1 conversion, and this is the #1 way people mismanage a freshly-imported account. If Meridian's account was ever imported, check what drifted.
- **UET tag** (Universal Event Tracking) — Microsoft's equivalent of the Google tag. Separate tag, separate conversion goals, separate setup. Confirm before Oct 6 whether it's already installed and firing correctly on Meridian's site.
- **Microsoft Advertising Editor** — desktop bulk-edit tool, Google Ads Editor equivalent. Install it, poke around once.

**3. Sept 26–Oct 2 — Freight/B2B-specific risk items (2h)**

- **Editorial policy for transportation/logistics/moving categories.** Google has a well-known certification requirement for moving companies to prevent scam listings; check whether Microsoft Advertising has an equivalent restricted-category policy for freight brokers/trucking. This is the single highest-risk unknown — if Meridian's account isn't certified/verified and Microsoft tightens enforcement, ads can get suspended with little warning. Verify this before you're the one holding the account.
- **LinkedIn Profile targeting** — unique to Microsoft Ads (it owns LinkedIn), lets you layer company/industry/job-function targeting on top of search campaigns. Genuinely useful for B2B freight (shippers, logistics managers) and has no Google Ads equivalent — worth 20–30 minutes understanding since it's a lever you don't have on Google.
- **Call extensions/call tracking setup** — if Meridian's freight leads come in by phone (common in brokerage), confirm how call conversion tracking differs from Google's call reporting.

**4. Oct 3–6 — Handover prep (~1h)**

- Skim the Microsoft Advertising blog/"what's new" page for anything shipped in the last few months (Performance Max equivalent, automated bidding changes) — just headlines, not deep reads.
- Write yourself a day-1 audit checklist (below) so the first hour in the real account is structured, not exploratory.

## Day-1 checklist (once you have access, Oct 6)

- [ ] Confirm UET tag is firing and conversion goals match what Google side reports.
- [ ] Check account/campaign status for any policy flags or certification requirements pending.
- [ ] Review current negative keyword lists — if imported from Google, verify they didn't get mangled.
- [ ] Note current budgets/bid strategy per campaign before touching anything (baseline for comparison).
- [ ] Check who else has account access (prior agency?) and whether any campaigns are mid-flight.

## What not to bother with

- Shopping/Merchant Center content — irrelevant unless Meridian runs ecommerce, which a freight brokerage almost certainly doesn't.
- Deep automated-bidding-algorithm reading. Set it up conservatively on day 1, tune after you're heads-down in the account — that's real optimization work, not pre-reading.
- Community forums/blogs as an ongoing habit. You said this isn't a forever reading habit — treat r/PPC or industry blogs as a one-time "any Microsoft-specific gotchas for B2B/lead-gen accounts" search in week 3, not a subscription.
