## Buying committee — what the data actually shows

The VP's "6–10 stakeholders" stat is a Gartner enterprise-B2B average, typically observed on complex, high-ACV, multi-department rollouts. Denticor is a $7K/year tool sold to 1–3 location independent clinics — a small business, not an enterprise buyer. Applying that stat here without checking it against your own win data is the actual mistake.

Your 40 wins say the real committee has **2 active roles**, not 8:

| Role | Actual function in the deal | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Practice Owner** | Economic buyer, final sign-off. Often also the lead dentist in solo/small practices — same person, one persona. | Present on ~all 40 calls |
| **Office Manager** | Champion, day-to-day user, initiates and runs the evaluation, pre-sells the owner. | Present on ~all 40 calls |
| Lead Dentist (distinct from owner) | Only exists as a separate person in multi-dentist/associate practices; not a blocker on scheduling tools. | Rare, not in CRM notes as active |
| IT Consultant, Procurement, Compliance, Finance, Front-desk staff | No standing function for these at 1–3 location scale — the owner and office manager absorb them. | Absent from 40/40 wins |

A 1–3 location clinic doesn't have a procurement department, a compliance officer, or a finance team reviewing a $7K SaaS line item. Building 8 personas invents a committee that doesn't exist at this segment and spreads budget across audiences with zero purchase influence.

## Campaigns — the 2 real roles get paid spend, the other 6 get reactive collateral only

**1. Practice Owner (Economic Buyer)**
- Pain: no-show revenue loss, staff time cost, ROI/payback.
- Channels: Google Search (high-intent terms: "reduce dental no-shows," "dental recall software"), Meta look-alike audiences built from the 40 closed-won contacts, LinkedIn (title = Owner/DDS + practice-management group targeting), sponsorships on dental practice-management podcasts and local dental association events.
- Message: revenue recovered, payback period, "built for owner-operators, not DSO corporate." CTA: ROI calculator → demo.

**2. Office Manager (Champion/User)**
- Pain: manual recall follow-up, scheduling chaos, no-show admin work.
- Channels: Google Search ("dental scheduling software," "patient recall system"), Facebook groups for dental office managers, YouTube pre-roll workflow demos, office-manager association newsletters, retargeting.
- Message: hours saved per week, ease of use, peer testimonials from other office managers. CTA: self-serve free trial / direct calendar booking — let the champion evaluate without owner gatekeeping upfront.

**3–8. Lead Dentist / IT Consultant / Procurement / Compliance / Finance / Front-desk — no paid campaigns**
- No ad spend; building personas for roles absent from 40/40 wins burns budget without evidence they influence the deal.
- Keep one reactive asset per role for the rare edge case, handed over by sales only if asked:
  - Lead Dentist (when distinct from owner): 1-pager on clinical workflow impact.
  - IT Consultant: technical/security & integration spec sheet, relevant mainly if you move upmarket to multi-location groups.
  - Procurement / Finance / Compliance / Front-desk: no dedicated asset — fold any question into the owner/office-manager sales deck.

## When to revisit

Re-run this analysis if you move upmarket (DSOs, 4+ location groups) — that's where procurement and IT genuinely enter the committee, and the 40-win base will start showing it in the CRM notes before it needs a persona.
