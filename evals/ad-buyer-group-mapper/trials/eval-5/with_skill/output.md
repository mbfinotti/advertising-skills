## 3. Why the leadership brief needs correcting, not just executing

Both named problems are already explained by the skill's evidence base, and the proposed fix (countdown urgency, CFO + security reviewer, all gated) makes both worse, not better:

- **"Security review shows up in month five and reopens everything"** is the textbook late-arrival pattern: a gatekeeper reached only at validation, instead of during supplier selection, resets agreed work. The fix isn't an ad — it's moving the security/compliance pack earlier and **ungating** it, so InfoSec can self-serve before month five instead of ambushing the deal there. Gating that exact asset behind a form (as instructed) keeps the ambush pattern intact.
- **"An embarrassing share just fizzle out with no decision"** matches the published pattern almost exactly: 40-60% of B2B deals end in no-decision, and 56% of those are fear-of-messing-up (indecision), not preference for the status quo. Urgency/countdown pressure is evidenced to *backfire* on indecision — especially for economic buyers and gatekeepers, i.e. exactly the two roles leadership wants to target with it. Applying pressure to a CFO who's afraid of approving the wrong thing, or to a security reviewer who has zero stake in urgency, reinforces the freeze response that causes no-decision in the first place.

**What to run instead:** keep the underlying idea — quantify what waiting actually costs — but ship it as a self-paced calculator/benchmark the buyer controls, and pair it everywhere with proof that the known stall points (security, consensus) are retired. That's the risk-reduction reframe in the CFO and Gatekeeper rows above.

## 4. Sequencing notes

Mapped to the six buying jobs, not a linear funnel (90% of buyers loop back):

| Buying job | Roles reached | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Problem identification | Champion/Initiator, End user | Top-of-funnel, always ungated |
| Requirements building | Technical evaluator | Architecture doc |
| **Supplier selection** | **Security gatekeeper** (moved up from validation) | The single highest-leverage fix for failure pattern #1 — reach them here, ungated, before they'd otherwise show up cold at month five |
| Business case | Economic buyer (CFO) | Risk-reduction calculator, not countdown creative |
| Validation | Security gatekeeper (confirmation only — they should already be engaged) | If this is still their first touch, the fix above didn't land |
| Consensus creation | All roles, weighted toward risk reduction for CFO + gatekeeper specifically | This is where no-decision gets prevented, not where urgency gets applied |

**On gating:** don't gate the full committee cold against all 300 accounts — 70% of the journey completes before any seller contact, and fewer than 30% of eventual buyers ever fill out a form on the winning vendor's site. Two-tier it instead:
- **Ungated, always:** the security/compliance pack, the architecture doc, the planner demo invite — these carry the pre-contact phase and specifically fix the security-reopen problem.
- **Gated, selectively:** the deeper ROI/benchmark report for the CFO, once an account shows two engagements or lands on the intent-ranked/inbound list — this is where sales legitimately gets leads without blocking the roles that need frictionless access.
