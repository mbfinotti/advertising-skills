# Freightlane — Buying Committee Map, Messaging & Offers

## Why deals are stalling

A single champion cannot approve $60K. At that price point, mid-market shippers route the decision through 5–7 people. If your ads and content only ever reinforce what the champion already believes, the other stakeholders never get their own reason to say yes — the deal dies in committee, not in the champion's inbox.

Two structural fixes, both reflected below:

1. **Multi-thread the account** — run parallel messaging to every role simultaneously, not sequentially through the champion.
2. **Arm the champion** — give them a leave-behind kit, one asset per stakeholder, so they can sell internally between your touches.

## On the ad brief: reframe "influencers"

Sales is using "influencer" as a catch-all. It's actually six distinct roles above, each with a different objection. One generic ad creative aimed at "influencers" will underperform because a dispatcher and a CFO are blocked by opposite things (dispatcher: fear of surveillance; CFO: unproven payback). Set up ABM targeting (LinkedIn Matched Audiences, or Demandbase/6sense/RollWorks if licensed) by job function within the named target accounts, and run all threads concurrently once a champion is confirmed — don't wait for the champion to escalate internally, since that's the exact bottleneck causing stalls today.

## On the CEO's "every ad ends with Book a Demo" mandate

Complying as stated: every ad's final CTA button says **Book a Demo**. Where it will underperform: cold Finance, IT, and Ops audiences rarely convert on a 30-minute rep meeting as their _first_ action — they haven't been pitched, they've been advertised to. Recommendation to keep both the mandate and the conversion rate: put the role-specific proof point (ROI calculator, security packet, comparison sheet) _on the landing page itself_, above the booking form, so the click satisfies their objection before they hit the CTA. The button text stays "Book a Demo" everywhere; only the page content around it changes. If the CEO is open to it, propose an A/B test on the Ops and Finance segments only (calculator/packet as a secondary CTA) — flag it as a data point to bring back, not a unilateral deviation from the mandate.

## On the industry benchmark report

Don't publish a "big industry benchmark report" with no proprietary data behind it — it reads as generic, gets no press pickup, and is a credibility risk if anyone asks about methodology or sample size. But you're sitting on a real asset that's being ignored: **two years of call recordings**. Mined and anonymized, that's proprietary insight nobody else in freight visibility has:

- Recurring pain patterns, timelines, carrier-friction quotes → a defensible "State of Freight Visibility" piece, framed honestly as _patterns from customer conversations_, not a statistically representative industry survey.
- Before using it, confirm recording consent covers this use and check two-party-consent state requirements — worth a 15-minute compliance check given you're already SOC 2 Type II and presumably careful about data handling.
- The real payoff isn't top-of-funnel lead gen — it's **champion enablement**. Pull role-specific quotes and stats from the recordings into the one-pagers in the Committee Briefing Kit above. That's a direct fix for "our champion can't get the rest of the group moving": give them evidence to forward, not just enthusiasm.

## Immediate next steps

1. Build the Committee Briefing Kit (6 one-pagers) — reuse the ROI calculator and security packet already needed for ads.
2. Mine call recordings for the champion-enablement quotes/stats (compliance check first).
3. Launch ABM ad sets by job function, concurrent not sequential, CTA = Book a Demo per CEO mandate, with role-matched landing pages.
4. Track by committee role, not just account — you need to know if Finance and IT are converting, not just whether the account "engaged."
