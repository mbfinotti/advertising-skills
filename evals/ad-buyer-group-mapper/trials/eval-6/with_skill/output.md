# Buyer-group map — Hearthside Climate

## Your two questions, answered first

**1. Can you target each spouse separately with CTV household-graph ads? No — don't build the plan around that.**
CTV household graphs resolve to a *household* (an address, an IP cluster, a device cluster), not to a verified individual inside it. Some platforms weight delivery toward a probable "primary viewer" using soft signals (app logins, co-viewing models), but that's a lean, not a guarantee — both partners can and will see whatever plays on the household's screen. There's no mechanism here that reliably puts angle A in front of Partner A only and angle B in front of Partner B only.

What actually works per the skill's household-purchase section: **role-differentiated creative into one shared household audience**, not attempted person-level isolation. Run both angles (research-validation angle, no-lock-in angle) into the same CTV/addressable buy and let frequency capping do the work of getting both messages in front of both people over the flight, rather than trying to wire messages to specific humans.

**2. Should you build the same mapping for the $89 duct-cleaning tune-up? No — that's overhead here.**
The skill's rule: *"Apply this model only to high-consideration categories with a genuine second decider... Routine purchases have one decider, and this whole skill is overhead."* $89 with no contract, no financing, no multi-year commitment is a routine maintenance purchase — the contract-lock-in fear that defines your second decider on the $14K system doesn't exist at this price point. Building a two-role map here would be inventing a committee that isn't there.
Treat it as single-decider: whoever books it (usually the research-minded partner, per your own review pattern) is the whole audience. One broad message, no role split, no separate targeting exercise. If you're running it as a retargeting/cross-sell offer to past $14K customers, it doesn't need its own audience build at all — it rides the existing customer list.

## 3. Sequencing notes

- Both roles are present from the start for a household purchase — there's no problem-identification-only vs. validation-only split like B2B has (no procurement/legal/security stage here).
- **Weight the contract-risk angle toward risk-reduction, not urgency.** The skill's broader finding applies directly: no-decision (people just not deciding) beats losing to a competitor as the real threat on big-ticket purchases, and it's driven by indecision/fear of a bad call, not price shopping. Your co-decider's stated fear — "locked into a bad contract" — is exactly that fear. A pushy "book now" angle aimed at this role will backfire; a "here's exactly what you're signing, here's the out clause" angle works with it.
- The researcher role can tolerate slightly more proactive framing (comparison tools, "see your options") since their stated concern is getting it *right*, not getting *trapped*.

## Before spending media budget

Two structural gates this map hasn't cleared yet — do these before building the buy:

1. **Quantify the review pattern.** Skim the 220 reviews (or a sample) specifically counting how many name a two-person split matching the researcher/contract-risk pattern, versus single-decider or no-pattern reviews. This turns both role rows from *hypothesis* to *evidenced* and tells you if the pattern is a strong majority or a handful of anecdotes shaping this whole plan.
2. **Check your service-area household count against audience floors** before committing budget — this determines whether you're running two creatives into one healthy audience, or need to widen geo first. That's the `ad-audience-targeting` skill's job, not this one's, but don't skip it.
