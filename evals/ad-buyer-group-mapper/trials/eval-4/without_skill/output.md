# Corvine Data — Buying Committee Campaign Plan

## Reality check first

Six parallel campaigns don't fit your inputs. $15K/month ÷ 6 = $2.5K per campaign — at typical B2B LinkedIn CPCs ($6–12) that's roughly 200–400 clicks/month per role, too thin to read signal or optimize. And "one concept/month" from you means 5 of 6 campaigns would run on stale or no creative at any given time — the segmentation looks sharp on a slide, but in execution five campaigns limp along unfunded and un-refreshed.

The buying-committee logic is sound. The structure needs to be **sequential, not parallel**, so your one concept/month and your $15K both land on one role at a time with real weight — and you still cover all six roles over a quarter.

Below: the plan built that way.

## The six roles, for Corvine specifically

| #   | Role                         | Who                                           | Pain angle                                                       |
| --- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Technical Champion           | Head of Data Engineering / Data Platform Lead | Firefighting broken pipelines, alert fatigue, false positives    |
| 2   | Economic Buyer               | VP Data / Chief Data Officer                  | Cost of bad data — revenue leakage, rework, compliance exposure  |
| 3   | End User                     | Data Analyst / Analytics Engineer             | Doesn't trust the dashboard, no way to self-serve root cause     |
| 4   | Governance Influencer        | Data Governance Lead                          | No lineage, no audit trail, can't prove data quality to auditors |
| 5   | Risk / Compliance Gatekeeper | Security / Compliance Officer                 | Vendor risk review, SOC2, data residency — can kill a deal late  |
| 6   | Executive Sponsor            | CIO / CDO                                     | Board-level narrative: data as a trusted, governed asset         |

## Structure: Spotlight Rotation, not six parallel campaigns

One role is "in spotlight" per month, funded and creatively fresh. The other five stay warm on retargeting only — no new production needed, just budget to not go cold.

**Monthly budget split ($15K):**

- **$10K — Spotlight campaign.** This month's role, full-funnel LinkedIn (Sponsored Content + Conversation Ads), your one new concept of the month.
- **$3.5K — Retargeting/warm layer.** Site visitors and engaged prospects from _previously_ spotlighted roles, reusing their existing creative. Keeps pipeline from earlier months alive without new production.
- **$1.5K — Always-on search.** Branded + category intent terms on Google Search, evergreen text ad copy (write once, barely touches your production capacity). Catches high-intent buyers regardless of which role is in spotlight.

This means every dollar of new-creative spend concentrates on ~800–1,600 clicks instead of 200–400 — enough to actually learn something.

## Making "1 concept/month" stretch

Build each month's concept as **one root asset with swappable headline/CTA**, not six standalone ads:

1. Design the core visual + message once per month, around that role's pain angle.
2. Generate 3–4 placement variants from it (LinkedIn single-image, LinkedIn video/motion cut if time allows, retargeting display banner set) via a template tool — swap text, not the concept.
3. Retarget layer reuses this same root asset in later months at lower spend — no rebuild.

This keeps you at true 1 concept/month while still populating every channel and every warm-audience touch.

## Why not just thin-slice all six anyway

At $30K ACV with a 40,000-company ICP, your constraint isn't audience size — it's your own production bandwidth and a budget that can't fund six statistically meaningful tests at once. Spreading thin gets you six campaigns that all look busy and none that produce a learnable signal. Concentrating gets you one real read per month, six real reads per quarter, and a compounding creative library instead of six half-fed accounts.
